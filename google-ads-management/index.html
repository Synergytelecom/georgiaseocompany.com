What Does a Google Ads Agency Do Each Month?

A Google Ads agency picks the searches your ads answer, writes the ads, sets where and when they show, and keeps trimming what the budget buys. The account is in your business's name, and Google bills you there for the ads. We run it and report what it produced.

Google charges for a click whether or not anybody answers the call that follows, so hold off on ads until someone can pick up the phone during business hours. The month's work goes in this order:

  • Search terms, the words people typed before they clicked.
  • Negative keywords, added so a wasted search gets paid for once and blocked after that.
  • Ad text and the call button, rewritten when one version draws fewer calls than another.
  • Budget and schedule, moved toward the days, hours and towns that produce calls.
  • The report, with spend, clicks, calls and cost per call set beside last month's.

Google Ads Management: Which Searches Do You Pay For?

You choose keywords, and Google matches them to searches it judges related, so the search you paid for can read nothing like the keyword you picked. The search terms report lists the searches behind your clicks, and Google Ads management starts there every month. Searches from job seekers, do-it-yourselfers and towns outside your service area get blocked, and the ones that end in calls get more of the budget.

Location leaks too: by default Google reaches people in your towns plus people who've shown interest in them, and its location options let us narrow that to people who are in those towns or regularly in them.

You get two bills, and they stay separate. Google bills your account for the clicks and the leads. Agencies charge for the work as a flat monthly fee, by the hour or as a share of ad spend, and ours is a share: 15% of that month's ad spend, for accounts spending $1,000 a month or more on ads. Local Services Ads: How Do They Work, and What Counts as a Lead?

Local Services Ads (LSA) are the listings at the top of a Google search, with a business name, a star rating and usually a badge, and Google charges for them per lead where regular ads charge per click. The badge is now Google Verified, one badge in place of the older ones, Google Guaranteed among them, and a business earns it by passing Google's screening. Google has also discontinued the Money Back Guarantee that came with the old badge.

A lead is a call you answer or return, a text, an email, a voicemail or a booking request. Under Google's lead rules, a call missed during your business hours is charged too once the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, voicemail greeting included. Google reviews each lead for 30 days and credits the poor, duplicate and invalid ones automatically.

In August 2026 Google began moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads, starting with select plumbing, HVAC, electrical and roofing advertisers. The new campaigns carry no keyword list and take their facts from your Google Business Profile, so once your account moves, the profile's hours and phone number feed the ads too.

Google Ads or SEO: Which Comes First?

Ads come first when next month's schedule has holes in it, because a funded campaign can bring calls the week it starts and rankings usually take three to six months to move. SEO comes first when the phone already rings enough to cover the bills and the goal is to pay less for each new customer. When you run both, the ad budget can move to the searches still out of reach as the website and the map card start winning the others. Since we run both sides, one monthly report shows the ad calls and the rankings together.

Frequently asked questions

How fast do Google Ads start bringing calls?

Google Ads can run the day a campaign is funded and approved, and Local Services Ads start once the business passes Google's screening. The first weeks also show which searches pay, so we judge cost per call on a full month of your own data.

Do Local Services Ads replace regular Google Ads?

No. Local Services Ads charge per lead and sit at the top of the results, and regular Google Ads charge per click. As Local Services Ads move into Google Ads they become their own campaign type beside your search campaigns, so a business can run both.

Why was I charged for a Local Services Ads call that went to voicemail?

Google counts a voicemail as a lead. It also charges for a call missed during your business hours once the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, even when only your greeting played. Leads Google later judges poor, duplicate or invalid are credited back automatically.

Who keeps the Google Ads account if we stop working together?

You do. The account, its campaigns and its history have been in your name since the first day, and you can remove our access whenever you choose.
