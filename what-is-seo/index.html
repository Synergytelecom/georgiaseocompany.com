What Is SEO, in Plain English?

SEO is earning a spot in search results instead of renting one, and holding that spot takes ongoing work.

What Does SEO Stand For?

Search engine optimization. It's the work of making a website, and the business behind it, easy for Google to find, easy to understand, and trustworthy enough to show first when someone searches for what that business sells. Everything else, the tactics and the tools and the jargon, sits underneath that one sentence.

A paid ad occupies its slot for as long as the budget stays funded. A page that ranks organically, or a Google Business Profile that holds a map pack spot, keeps showing up without further spend, because Google has decided it belongs there.

Most owners meet the term as a line item on an invoice. The plainest test of whether it's doing anything is whether strangers who never heard your name are calling you.

What Holding an SEO Ranking Takes

Three things about that work come up in almost every first conversation.

Earned space behaves differently from rented space. Paid search and SEO both put a business in front of someone searching, and they're opposite kinds of investment. An ad is rented, visible while the budget runs and gone the hour it stops. SEO earns a position that keeps producing visibility with no per-click cost. The work behind it still costs something.

It moves in months. A new page takes time to rank, and a repaired Google Business Profile takes time to climb the map pack, because search engines have to crawl new content, compare it against everything already ranking, and then decide it deserves a spot. A pitch promising a first page spot within days is describing something other than SEO.

A ranking is a standing, and standings get defended. A website optimized once loses ground months later while competitors keep publishing, keep earning reviews, and keep improving their own sites. That upkeep is what a business hires us for. Every month we re-check what your towns are searching for, optimize the website and the Google Business Profile for the terms that bring in calls, and report what moved.

How Does SEO Work?

Underneath the jargon, search engines do three things, in order, every time:

  1. Crawl. Google sends automated programs across the web that follow links from page to page, reading what each one contains.
  2. Index. What gets crawled is stored in a massive catalog, tagged by what the page is about, so it can be pulled up later.
  3. Rank. When someone searches, Google scores every indexed page that might match and puts them in order. The signals group roughly into relevance (does this page answer the search), quality (is it trustworthy and well built), and, for local searches, proximity plus how complete the Google Business Profile is.

SEO work sits at each stage. Technical fixes make a site easier to crawl. Clear, specific content gives Google something worth indexing, which in practice means a real page for every service you sell, where one page stretched across all of them covers each one thinly. Citations, reviews, and a well-built Google Business Profile carry the trust signals that move a page or a listing up once it's already in the index.

Where Rankings Show Up on the Page

One results page stacks three bands, and each one runs on different rules. Paid ads sit at the top when anyone is bidding on that search, bought rather than earned, which is why SEO leaves that band alone. Under the ads is the map pack, a set of three local listings pulled straight from Google Business Profiles, running on proximity, category match, and review signals more than on the website itself. Below the map pack sit the organic results, the traditional ranked list of pages, earned through relevance and trust built over time.

That leaves SEO two contests on the same page, and a business can win one and lose the other entirely. A page can rank well organically while a stronger local competitor holds every map pack spot for the same search. Knowing which of the two brings your customers matters more than chasing both equally.

One more surface sits above all three. An AI-generated summary increasingly answers the search before the reader scrolls, and it draws that answer from the pages Google already trusts on the subject, which makes it downstream of the same work.

The Pieces of SEO Work

"SEO" breaks into distinct pieces of work, and most businesses buy some combination of them rather than all at once:

  • Local SEO covers the map pack specifically: profile setup, service areas, and review pace.
  • Google Business Profile management is the ongoing maintenance a profile needs once it's set up, since categories and rules shift over time.
  • Content built around real search terms gives Google specific pages to rank, one per thing a customer is actually typing.
  • Web design matters because rankings that point to a slow, confusing site waste the traffic they earn.
  • An SEO audit is usually where this starts, a look at what's broken before committing to anything.
  • SEO consulting fits a team that already has marketing capacity and wants direction more than execution.
  • Digital marketing is the wider picture SEO sits inside, alongside paid ads or social, for a business running more than one channel.
  • Put all of the above together and you have SEO services, the full engagement most businesses mean when they ask for "SEO."

A Short Glossary

SEO
Search engine optimization: the work of earning visibility in search results, as opposed to buying it.
Local SEO
The subset of SEO focused on the map pack and searches that include a location.
Google Business Profile
The free business listing behind both the map pack and the knowledge panel beside a search result.
Map pack
The set of three local business listings that appear above the standard results for most local searches.
Organic results
The standard ranked list of web pages, as opposed to paid ads or the map pack.
Keyword
A specific word or phrase someone types into a search engine.
Citation
A mention of a business's name, address, and phone number on another website or directory.
Backlink
A link from another website pointing to yours, one of the signals Google uses to judge trust.

Frequently asked questions

Is SEO the same thing as Google Ads?

No. Google Ads buys placement in the sponsored section of a results page for however long its budget stays active. SEO earns a position in the organic results or the map pack that keeps showing up without paying per click. Earning it takes work and time.

How long does SEO actually take to work?

There is no fixed number that applies to every business. How competitive the market already is, and how much work the site needs, both move the timeline. What is consistent is the direction. Rankings build over months, not days, and the work compounds the longer it continues.

Do I need a website to do SEO?

A basic web presence helps, but a lot of local visibility runs through the Google Business Profile rather than the website itself. A weak or missing website does cap how far rankings can go, since a page that ranks still needs somewhere to send the click.

What is the difference between SEO and local SEO?

Local SEO is the subset of SEO aimed at the map pack and searches that include a location. It runs on different signals than the rest of SEO, proximity and category and reviews rather than page relevance and links.

Can I do SEO myself?

Some of it, yes, especially claiming and completing a Google Business Profile. Ongoing content, technical fixes, and citation work take more time than most owners have available, which is usually where it makes sense to bring in outside help.
