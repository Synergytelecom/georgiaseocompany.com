Why Is My Website Not Showing Up on Google?

One free search splits this into two very different problems, and Google Search Console names the one you have.

Get a free visibility audit

Is It the Website, or Is It the Google Business Profile?

Owners call us and say they're invisible on Google. Then the map block at the top of their own results turns out to show the business fine, calls still coming off it, and what has gone missing is the website in the blue links underneath. That map block, the map pack, runs off the Google Business Profile (the free listing behind the map card) and reads almost nothing from the website. The organic results below it run off the website and read almost nothing from the profile. This one is about the website.

There's one case where a missing website barely matters. If your work arrives off a supply house's referral list or a manufacturer's dealer locator, and almost nobody types your service into Google, put the money where the calls already come from.

If the business is missing from the map block itself, Google Business Profile not showing up is the closer diagnosis. If nothing comes back under any search, including your own name, start at how to get your business on Google.

Your Business Name Comes Up and Nothing Else Does

Start with a free check. Search site:yourdomain.com in Google, using your own domain, and that operator returns the pages Google has indexed from it. If pages come back, indexing works and your problem is competitive. If nothing comes back, the problem is technical and the causes are a different list.

Your business name ranks because almost nobody competes for those exact words. The service plus the town is a different contest, against pages built for that search on purpose. A page written for Rome beats a single "Services" page trying to cover Floyd, Bartow, Gordon and Polk counties at once, because the specific page says the town in the title and says it again in the copy.

Photos uploaded at full camera size, and a menu that buries the services more than two clicks deep, drag down a page that otherwise reads fine. Owners ask us whether pausing a Google Ads budget is what dropped the site. It didn't, because ads and organic results are scored in separate systems.

Nothing Comes Back at All

Zero results from that search means Google has never indexed a page from the domain, and the cause is usually ordinary.

  • New domain, no links in. Google finds pages by following links from pages it already has, so a site launched last month with nothing pointing at it sits unseen for weeks. No fixed age applies: a clean new site with a submitted sitemap can turn up within days.
  • noindex. One line in the page's own code, <meta name="robots" content="noindex">, asking search engines to keep it out. Development sites carry it on purpose and it survives the launch. In WordPress it's a checkbox: Settings, then Reading, then "Discourage search engines from indexing this site." The same block can ride in the server's headers as an X-Robots-Tag, invisible in the page source.
  • robots.txt. This file at yourdomain.com/robots.txt tells crawlers where they may go, and one line reading Disallow: / closes the whole site.
  • Redirects dropped in a redesign. Rebuilds move pages to new addresses, and the old ones that already ranked have to be redirected with a 301 (a permanent redirect), or Google keeps requesting pages that answer 404 now and the rankings leave with them.

How Do I Check This Myself, Without Paying Anybody?

  1. Run the site:yourdomain.com search signed out of your Google account.
  2. Search the page's own code for the word noindex. In Chrome: right click, View Page Source, Ctrl+F.
  3. Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt in the address bar and look for Disallow: / on a line by itself.
  4. Verify the domain in Google Search Console (Google's free reporting account for site owners).
  5. Paste a page address into URL Inspection, the search bar across the top of Search Console.
  6. Open the Page indexing report and read the reasons listed under the graph. "Excluded by 'noindex' tag", "Blocked by robots.txt", "Discovered - currently not indexed" and "Not found (404)" each name a different problem.
  7. Open Security & Manual Actions. A manual penalty is rarely the answer here, and that report says outright whether one applies.

All of it is free.

What Search Console's URL Inspection Tells You

URL Inspection is where the guessing stops. Paste in a page address and Search Console answers in the same order every time: whether the page is on Google, whether robots.txt let Googlebot through, whether the server handed the page over, and whether indexing is allowed. The last line is the one that decides it, and on a page carrying a leftover tag it reads "noindex detected in robots meta tag."

Once those first three lines clear the server and robots.txt, one tag in one file is all that's left. Pull the tag, press Request Indexing on that same screen, and the page joins a queue. Google publishes no turnaround for that queue, so anyone quoting you one is guessing.

Cleared the Block and the Site Is Still Nowhere?

Clearing a technical block, or writing the service page that was missing, is work an owner can finish alone once they know which one it is. The harder case is a site that has been live for months, with real content on it, and still nowhere in the results. Closing that gap takes new pages and links earned from outside the site, over months.

Hand it off when one of these is true:

  • The vendor who built the site holds the login and won't release the access to look at any of this.
  • The honest answer is a page for every service and every town you serve, which is a build with a schedule attached.
  • You made every one of those fixes, gave it several weeks, and the search results look identical.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take Google to index a brand new website?

Days to weeks. A submitted sitemap and a few links pointing at the domain shorten it considerably. A site with nothing linking to it anywhere can sit unfound for a month or more, because Google finds pages by following links from pages it already knows about.

Does a noindex tag remove rankings a page already had?

Yes. The tag asks search engines to drop the page from the index, and any position it held goes with it. Pulling the tag and requesting indexing puts the page back in the queue, though it does not always return at the same position.

My website disappeared after a redesign. What happened?

Usually the redirects. A rebuild moves pages to new addresses, and every old address that already ranked needs a 301 pointing at its replacement. Without that map, Google keeps requesting pages that answer 404 and the rankings leave with them.

Can I diagnose this without buying a tool?

Yes, all of it. Google Search Console is free and names the exact reason a page is excluded from the index. A site: search in Google and a look at your own robots.txt file cover the rest.

Does pausing Google Ads make organic rankings drop?

No. Ads and organic results are scored in separate systems, so starting or stopping an ad budget does not move an organic position in either direction.
