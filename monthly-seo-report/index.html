What's Inside the Monthly SEO Report

Every month you get a report carrying three things: where your priority terms stand in each town you serve, both in the map pack and in the organic listings beneath it; a dated log of what got worked on, page by page and setting by setting; and what your competitors did that changed where you stand. The date is fixed at the start of the engagement, and a person will read the whole thing back to you on the phone when you want that.

Reporting comes bundled into the monthly work, never sold as a product by itself. The cycle behind it is short. What the market is doing gets read, the site and the listing get moved to fit, and the third step is this document. It's the only step you see directly, which is why it has to make sense to a busy owner rather than to another SEO.

Flat months get sent on the same schedule as good ones. They happen, most often in a first quarter while profile and citation repairs are still settling, and a market that held still is a real finding worth a line of its own.

Why Local SEO Reporting Comes Town by Town

Map pack position is several numbers wearing one label. The pack changes with the block a searcher is standing on, so a business can hold the top card in the town its office sits in and appear nowhere two exits up the interstate. Average those into one figure and the town you're losing vanishes inside the town you're winning.

So we pull position from inside every listed town, one line each. One client's report runs to thirteen Northwest Georgia towns, and it comes back first in the map pack across all of them on city-qualified searches. That result is thirteen separate checks, because thirteen checks is what it took to know it.

Organic position gets the same treatment where it matters, though it shifts less with location than the pack does. Your own Google Business Profile performance view fills in the rest: calls, website clicks, and direction requests, split by whether the person arrived from Search or from Maps.

How to Check the SEO Report Against Your Own Numbers

Two free screens you already own cover most of what's in it. Google Search Console lists every search that put a visitor on your site, with clicks, impressions, clickthrough rate, and average position beside each one. The performance view inside your Google Business Profile counts what the map card itself produced.

Read them together, and read them with your own company name filtered out. Searches carrying your business name come from people who already knew you, so leaving them in a total makes any month look better than the search work earned. Filter them out and what remains is strangers finding you on what you sell, which is the number this work answers for.

Where a line in the report and a line in Search Console disagree, ask, and somebody walks you through it. Usually the gap is a definition: a rank pulled from inside a town against an average taken across everywhere, or a call placed off the map card, which the website never sees.

The Report Lines That Sound Like Progress

Plenty of monthly reports are activity logs in a results costume. Four lines worth looking at twice, on ours or on anybody else's.

Counts of Work Delivered

A count of directory submissions, a count of blog posts, a count of optimizations performed. Those describe effort, and effort is genuinely part of what you're paying for. A month can be full of them while every position on your list sits exactly where it was, and the report will still read as a productive month.

Traffic Totals With Branded Search Left In

A total that includes people typing your company name measures your reputation and your ad spend more than it measures search work. Split branded from unbranded and both halves start telling you something.

Percentages Without the Raw Counts

A percentage gain sounds large until you see the number it grew from. A jump that reads as a multiple can come off a base of a few impressions, so the raw counts belong printed beside the percentage every time.

Rank Figures With No Location Attached

A position pulled from a data center somewhere is a different number than a position pulled from inside your town. When a rank figure doesn't say where it was taken, it hasn't told you much.

How the SEO Report Decides Next Month

The report is the hinge in the monthly cycle, and it's what keeps the work from settling into a routine. Every line in it produces a decision.

A town that moved gets left alone and watched. A town that sat still gets a reason found for it: a competitor who added reviews, a page that was never built for that market, a service area quietly leaving the town out. A term collecting impressions with no clicks gets its page rewritten. A profile that just appeared in a pack gets read to see what its owner changed.

That's how a report earns its place. It comes out the other side as a handful of ranked jobs for the coming month, and the following report says whether those jobs worked. Keyword research supplies the terms it tracks, and the free audit supplies the baseline the first one gets held against.

Who Gets Something Out of a Monthly SEO Report

It works for an owner who will spend fifteen minutes on it, ask one question, and let the answer change what gets worked on next. That's the whole ask, and it's the difference between a report that steers the work and a report that gets filed unopened.

It works a second way for a marketing manager who has to report upward, because the numbers need to be checkable by somebody else. Search Console access sits on your side, so any line can be traced to a screen your leadership can open themselves.

Two people get less from it. An owner who wants a live dashboard with alerts is describing something a monthly cycle doesn't deliver, and the free screens they already own do most of that job. And a business that wants reporting on work another vendor is performing is asking for oversight, which sits closer to SEO consulting than to a report.

Frequently asked questions

When does the report arrive each month?

On a date agreed at the start, and the same date every month after. Consistency matters because a report is mostly useful as a comparison against the last one.

Can I check the report against my own data?

Yes, and we encourage it. Google Search Console and your Google Business Profile performance view are free, already yours, and show the same clicks, impressions, and average position the report draws from. Both update on their own between reports, so if you want the numbers day to day, we will show you which screens to watch in each.

Do you send a report in a month where rankings held still?

Yes, on the same schedule. A flat month is information, and it usually points straight at what the next month should go after.

Why is map pack rank reported per town?

Because position changes with where the searcher is standing. One averaged figure hides the towns you are losing behind the town you are winning, and that gap is exactly what an owner needs to see.

Do we keep the reports if the engagement ends?

Yes. Every report you have received stays yours, and so do the accounts the numbers were pulled from.
