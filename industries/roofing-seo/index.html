A Big Purchase Gets Compared Like One

A full roof replacement runs alongside a new HVAC system or a kitchen remodel in what a homeowner is willing to spend, and most homeowners shop it with the same care: reading reviews closely, checking how recent they are, and comparing more than one estimate before signing anything. That makes review recency, not just review count, the single biggest trust signal in this trade. A profile sitting on reviews from three years ago reads as a business that either stopped doing good work or stopped asking, and either read costs the job to a competitor with a thinner but fresher review history.

The correct primary category is Roofing contractor. Beyond the category, manufacturer certification badges, if the business is a certified installer for a specific shingle brand, insurance and bonding proof, and before-and-after photos of completed jobs, ideally including storm damage work, all carry real weight, since a homeowner letting a crew onto a roof wants proof of both skill and legitimacy before a single quote gets discussed. Drone photography has become common enough in this trade that its absence stands out; a homeowner comparing three roofing estimates increasingly expects to see an aerial shot of the actual roof in question, not a stock image of a generic house, and a business still relying only on ground-level photos looks a step behind competitors who have already made the switch. Metal roofing has grown into an option homeowners specifically ask about during a replacement quote, and a service page that addresses it directly alongside standard shingle work avoids losing that comparison to a specialist competitor by default.

Older Roofs, the Same Storm Systems

Smaller Georgia towns carry a lot of older housing stock working through the tail end of a roof's normal lifespan, which creates steady replacement demand independent of any single storm. Carrollton, a Carroll County city just over 28,300 residents, is one example of a town with plenty of that older housing stock aging toward replacement on its own timeline, on top of whatever storm-driven demand comes through in a given year. The same severe spring and summer storm systems that drive tree service calls across north Georgia also drive roofing calls, hail and wind damage that turns into an insurance claim rather than a scheduled remodel. Both kinds of demand, aging roofs reaching the end of their life and storm damage needing an insurance-driven repair, show up across the same towns our local SEO work already covers, not concentrated in one metro alone. Where an insurance claim is involved, the buying decision runs through an adjuster's scope of work as much as the homeowner's own preference, and a contractor whose estimate lines up cleanly with that scope tends to close faster than one that leaves the homeowner to negotiate a gap between the two.

Numbers an Industry Average Cannot Answer for You

What is the fee spread, in your own market, between a repair and a full replacement? Out of the free inspections that went out this month, how many actually turned into a signed job? And of the inspection requests still coming in, how many trace back to an actual storm or insurance claim rather than a homeowner replacing on a normal schedule with nothing weather related behind it? None of those three answers live in a published average. They live in a business's own inspection log and its own calendar, which is the only place worth looking for them.

Frequently asked questions

Why does review recency matter more than total review count for roofing?

Because a roof replacement is a large, infrequent purchase homeowners research carefully. A profile with old reviews reads as inactive even with a high total count, while a smaller but fresher set of reviews signals a business still doing and asking about recent work.

What Google category should a roofing company use?

Roofing contractor is the correct primary category for this trade.

Do storm-driven jobs and scheduled replacements need different marketing?

They pull from different motivations, an insurance claim timeline versus a homeowner's own planning, so photos, service pages, and even review requests can be worded to match which situation actually applies.

How should a roofing business think about a slow month with no signed jobs?

By checking whether it fell during active storm season or not. A quiet month with no storm activity in the area reads very differently than a quiet month in the middle of a busy storm season, and the right response depends on knowing which one happened.
