Why Roofing SEO Starts With Review Dates

A homeowner puts a new roof in the same mental category as a new HVAC system or a kitchen remodel, and shops it the same way: reads the reviews closely, checks the dates on them, and gets more than one estimate before signing. That makes review recency the biggest trust signal in this trade, ahead of review count. A profile whose newest review is three years old reads as a business that either slowed down or stopped asking. Either way the job goes to a roofer with fewer reviews and newer ones.

Storm season is also when crews from out of the area turn up looking for claims work, so a Georgia homeowner comparing three estimates is often working out which of the three will still be reachable in two years. A profile carrying local reviews from the last few months answers that before anybody asks it out loud.

The Profile a Homeowner Checks Before the Estimate

Roofing contractor is the primary category, and the pick is the easy part. What's worth checking is what the listing says today, because a profile somebody set up years ago is often still sitting under a broader construction label, which puts it in front of searches the business can't serve and leaves it out of the ones it can.

Then the proof goes on: manufacturer certification badges where the business is a certified installer for a specific shingle brand, insurance and bonding documentation, and before-and-after photos of completed jobs including storm damage work. A homeowner letting a crew onto a roof wants evidence of both skill and legitimacy before a quote gets discussed.

The Drone Photos Homeowners Expect

Drone photos have become common enough that going without them is what gets noticed. A homeowner comparing three estimates expects to see their own roof from above. If you're already flying a drone to take measurements, the photos are a byproduct of work you do anyway.

Four Service Pages, One Job Each

Replacement, repair, storm damage, and metal roofing each get a page of their own. Metal has grown into an option homeowners specifically ask about during a replacement quote, and a page that addresses it alongside standard shingle work keeps that comparison from defaulting to a specialist competitor.

Two Kinds of Demand: Worn-Out Roofs and Hail

Smaller Georgia towns carry a lot of older housing working through the tail end of a roof's normal lifespan, which creates steady replacement demand independent of any single storm. Carrollton is one of those towns, with plenty of housing aging toward replacement on its own timeline. The same severe spring and summer systems that drive tree service calls across north Georgia drive roofing calls too: hail and wind damage that turns into an insurance claim.

Both kinds of demand show up across the same towns our Local SEO work already covers. On an insurance job the adjuster's scope of work decides as much as the homeowner does. Write an estimate that lines up with that scope and the job closes. Leave a gap and the homeowner has to go negotiate it, which is where these deals stall.

A Page for Every County the Crews Will Drive To

Roofing travels further than most trades. A crew will take a full replacement two counties over that it would never drive for a repair, so the town pages get built around the replacement radius and the repair radius separately.

Each of those pages names the county, the housing it covers, and the work you genuinely do there. A page that only swaps the town name into the same paragraph gets treated as the template it is, and it drags on the rest of the site.

The pages also have to survive the search results they land in. A roofing search fills up fast with directory listings and lead-broker sites selling your name back to you, and the way past them is a page with real local detail on it rather than another thin city page.

Frequently asked questions

Why does review recency matter more than total review count for roofing?

A roof replacement is a large, infrequent purchase homeowners research carefully. A profile with old reviews reads as inactive even with a high total count, while a smaller but fresher set signals a business still doing and asking about recent work.

What Google category should a roofing company use?

Roofing contractor as the primary, and the useful question is what your listing is set to right now. Metal roofing contractor, Gutter service, and anything else the crew genuinely does belong in the secondary slots underneath it.

Do storm-driven jobs and scheduled replacements need different marketing?

Yes. An insurance claim timeline and a homeowner planning a replacement on their own schedule are different situations, so the photos, the service pages, and even the review wording follow whichever one applies.

How long before a new roofing service page starts ranking?

City-qualified and long-tail terms move first, which is why we build the town pages and the four service pages before chasing the head terms. Roofing contractor plus a large city name is the slowest thing on the list, and a page with real photos and a real estimate process behind it climbs faster than a thin one.

Before Hail Season Fills Every Roofer's Calendar

The roofer taking claims calls in April is the one whose profile was already carrying fresh reviews in February. Review pace is the slowest-moving piece in this trade, which makes it the one worth starting first.
