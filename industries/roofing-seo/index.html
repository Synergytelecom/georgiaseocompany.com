A new roof is one of the biggest purchases a homeowner makes, and they shop it that carefully.

Fresh Reviews Beat a Big Old Review Count

A homeowner puts a new roof in the same mental category as a new HVAC system or a kitchen remodel, and shops it the same way: reads the reviews closely, checks the dates on them, and gets more than one estimate before signing. That makes review recency the biggest trust signal in this trade, ahead of review count. A profile whose newest review is three years old reads one of two ways, that the work fell off or that nobody has asked, and both cost the job to a roofer with fewer reviews and newer ones.

Storm season is also when crews from out of the area turn up looking for claims work, so a Georgia homeowner comparing three estimates is often working out which of the three will still be reachable in two years. A profile carrying local reviews from the last few months answers that before anybody asks it out loud. We haven't worked with a roofing company yet.

Then the proof goes on: manufacturer certification badges where the business is a certified installer for a specific shingle brand, insurance and bonding documentation, and before-and-after photos of completed jobs including storm damage work. A homeowner letting a crew onto a roof wants evidence of both skill and legitimacy before a quote gets discussed.

The Drone Photos Homeowners Expect

Drone photos have become common enough that not having them is what gets noticed. A homeowner comparing three estimates expects to see their own roof from above. If you're already flying a drone to take measurements, the photos are a byproduct of work you do anyway.

Four Service Pages, One Job Each

Replacement, repair, storm damage, and metal roofing each get a page of their own. Metal has grown into an option homeowners specifically ask about during a replacement quote, and a page that addresses it alongside standard shingle work keeps that comparison from defaulting to a specialist competitor.

Two Kinds of Demand: Worn-Out Roofs and Hail

Smaller Georgia towns carry a lot of older housing stock working through the tail end of a roof's normal lifespan, which creates steady replacement demand independent of any single storm. Carrollton, a Carroll County city of just over 28,300 residents, is one town with plenty of that housing aging toward replacement on its own timeline, on top of whatever storm-driven demand comes through in a given year. The same severe spring and summer systems that drive tree service calls across north Georgia drive roofing calls too, hail and wind damage that turns into an insurance claim.

Both kinds of demand show up across the same towns our Local SEO work already covers. On an insurance job the adjuster's scope of work decides as much as the homeowner does. Write an estimate that lines up with that scope and the job closes. Leave a gap and the homeowner has to go negotiate it, which is where these deals stall.

Frequently asked questions

Why does review recency matter more than total review count for roofing?

Because a roof replacement is a large, infrequent purchase homeowners research carefully. A profile with old reviews reads as inactive even with a high total count, while a smaller but fresher set of reviews signals a business still doing and asking about recent work.

What Google category should a roofing company use?

Roofing contractor. Picking it is not the hard part. What is worth checking is what the listing is set to right now, since a profile somebody set up years ago is often still sitting under a broader construction label, which puts it in front of searches the business cannot serve and leaves it out of the ones it can.

Do storm-driven jobs and scheduled replacements need different marketing?

They pull from different motivations, an insurance claim timeline versus a homeowner's own planning, so photos, service pages, and even review requests can be worded to match which situation applies.

How should a roofing business think about a slow month with no signed jobs?

By checking whether it fell during active storm season or not. A quiet month with no storm activity in the area reads very differently than a quiet month in the middle of a busy storm season, and the right response depends on knowing which one happened.
