The Reel Is the Sale, So the Page Exists to Get to It

In most trades the copy does the persuading. Here it does not. A prospect forms an opinion inside the first half minute of watching something you shot, and everything on the page is either helping them reach that moment or delaying it.

Which means the usual mistakes cost more in this trade than elsewhere. Work buried three clicks deep. A reel that takes six seconds to start. A homepage that opens with a paragraph about storytelling instead of the thing itself. The structural job is short: get the right sample in front of the right buyer fast, and make sure a search engine can still read the page around it.

Corporate Work and Weddings Are Different Businesses

A marketing director sourcing a brand video and a couple booking a wedding film are not the same customer, do not search the same way, and are not reassured by the same reel. Showing both on one page weakens the pitch to each.

They split cleanly into their own pages: commercial and brand work, event coverage, real estate video, training and internal communications, and wedding films. Each names a buyer, carries the samples that buyer wants, and answers the objection that buyer actually has. The corporate visitor wants to know you can hold a schedule and hit a brief. The wedding visitor wants to know their day is safe with you. Those are different pages, not different paragraphs.

A Studio Without a Storefront Still Needs the Profile Right

The category is Video production service. Most companies in this trade either work from home or from a space nobody visits, which makes this a service area business: hide the street address and publish the areas served instead.

The part most operators skip is that a Google Business Profile takes video, not only photographs. In a trade where the work is video, an empty video section on the profile is a strange omission, and it is one of the few places a competitor cannot easily copy you. Hours matter too, because a profile with none set reads as less complete than one that publishes them.

What a Buyer Wants to Know That the Reel Does Not Say

The reel establishes that you can shoot. It says nothing about the three things that decide the booking.

The first is what a project actually involves: how long from brief to delivery, how many rounds of revision, who writes the script, who is on set. The second is price shape. Almost nobody in this trade publishes numbers and almost every buyer wants a range, so even naming what moves a quote up or down separates you from a page that says get in touch. The third is proof you have done their specific thing before. A manufacturer looking for a plant tour wants to see a plant tour, and a generic showreel does not answer it.

Video Production SEO Month to Month

Two things get watched here that other trades do not need. The local searches, where a nearby buyer is looking for a company they can meet, and the work-type searches, which have no geography attached and can be won from anywhere.

The monthly pass checks both, then fixes whichever is weaker: a work type with no page, a finished project that never became a case study, a profile with no video on it. What changed and why arrives in writing on the same date each month.

Frequently asked questions

Should a video company put weddings and corporate work on the same page?

No. They are different buyers with different worries, and a page trying to reassure both reassures neither. Separate pages, each with the samples and the answers that buyer is looking for.

Can you add video to a Google Business Profile?

Yes, and in this trade leaving it empty is conspicuous. The profile accepts video as well as photographs, and it is one of the few profile fields most competitors never fill.

Should pricing go on a video production site?

A range or at least an explanation of what moves a quote. Almost nobody in this trade publishes anything, so a page that explains how projects get priced stands out against a wall of contact us for a quote.

When the Work Arrives Only Through People Who Have Already Seen the Reel

Referrals and past clients carry most video companies, and it works until the year somebody's marketing budget moves. Search covers the gap, but only if the pages exist before you need them.
