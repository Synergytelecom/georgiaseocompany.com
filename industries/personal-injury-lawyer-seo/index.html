The Search Happens Once, and Fast

Someone searching for a personal injury lawyer is almost never a repeat customer, and they're usually searching in the days right after a wreck or a fall. That search happens once, gets decided quickly, and the person making it has a lot riding on it. Which means the profile and the pages have to be right before it happens, because there's no second search to catch.

Intake speed sits right beside visibility. A firm that wins the search and is slow to answer the phone loses the case to whichever office picks up, no matter how the map pack looked at the moment the call was placed. Most of those calls land outside office hours, because a wreck doesn't wait for Monday.

The Page Should Match the Wreck They Just Had

We set the category to Personal injury attorney, since a searcher typing injury-specific terms is looking for that exact specialty. Then we build a page for each case type: car accidents, truck accidents, slip and fall, motorcycle accidents, wrongful death. Each of those searchers is describing a specific situation and expects language that matches it.

Reviews come in slower than case volume suggests, because clients hesitate to post publicly about litigation, even a case that went their way. The ask belongs well after a matter resolves, never while it's open, and a client asked at the right moment will usually say yes. Attorney headshots, bar association standing, and relevant certifications go on the profile described plainly, with no language promising a specific outcome, since no attorney can promise how a case will resolve. A bio that names the cases an attorney handles, car wrecks, workplace injuries, a defective product, does more than a generalist paragraph. Someone who landed on your truck accident page wants proof you've handled a truck accident before.

Frequently asked questions

Should a personal injury lawyer use the Law firm category or something more specific?

Personal injury attorney is the closer match and performs better for personal injury searches specifically, since it aligns directly with the search term.

Can a client leave a review if the case settled under a confidentiality agreement?

Often yes, as long as the review sticks to describing the attorney and the experience of working with the firm rather than a settlement figure or any detail the confidentiality clause actually covers. Coaching a client on that distinction before asking protects both the client and the firm.

What about a case that overlaps two categories, like a trucking accident that becomes a wrongful death claim?

Common enough to plan for. Land them on the page matching what they typed, so a truck accident search opens the truck accident page, then link plainly across to wrongful death. Follow the words they used, not the category you would have filed it under.

Can a personal injury lawyer's website mention case outcomes?

Carefully, and never as a promise about a future case. Past results can be stated plainly, but language that promises a certain outcome misleads readers and typically runs against bar advertising rules.
