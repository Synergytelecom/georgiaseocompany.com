The Days Right After a Wreck

Somebody searching for a personal injury lawyer is almost never a repeat customer, and they're usually searching in the days right after a wreck or a fall. Most of them type car accident lawyer near me, sometimes with a county or a road name on the end of it. That search happens once, gets decided quickly, and the person making it has a lot riding on it. The profile and the pages have to be right before it happens, because there's no second search to catch.

Intake speed sits right beside visibility. A firm that wins the search and is slow to answer the phone loses the case to whichever office picks up, however the map pack looked at the moment the call went out. Most of those calls land outside office hours, because a wreck happens on its own schedule.

The Page Should Match the Wreck They Just Had

We set the category to Personal injury attorney, since a searcher typing injury-specific terms is looking for that exact specialty. Then we build a page for each case type: car accidents, truck accidents, slip and fall, motorcycle accidents, wrongful death. Each of those searchers is describing a specific situation and expects language that matches it.

Reviews come in slower than case volume suggests, because clients hesitate to post publicly about litigation even when a case went their way. The ask belongs well after a matter resolves, never while it's open, and a client asked at the right moment will usually say yes. Attorney headshots, bar association standing, and relevant certifications go on the profile described plainly, with no language promising a specific outcome, since no attorney can promise how a case will resolve.

Bios do more when they name the work. Car wrecks. Workplace injuries. A defective product. Somebody who landed on your truck accident page wants proof you've handled a truck accident before, and only a specific bio gives them that.

Frequently asked questions

Should a personal injury lawyer use the Law firm category or something more specific?

Personal injury attorney. It's the closer match and it performs better for injury searches specifically, because it lines up with the search term itself.

Can a client leave a review if the case settled under a confidentiality agreement?

Yes, as long as the review sticks to the attorney and the experience of working with the firm rather than a settlement figure or any detail the confidentiality clause covers. Coaching a client on that distinction before asking protects both sides.

What about a case that overlaps two categories, like a trucking accident that becomes a wrongful death claim?

Follow the words they typed. A truck accident search opens the truck accident page, then links plainly across to wrongful death. Filing logic is yours; the page has to match their language.

Can a personal injury lawyer's website mention case outcomes?

Carefully, and never as a promise about a future case. Past results can be stated plainly, but language that promises a certain outcome misleads readers and typically runs against bar advertising rules.
