What We Do Every Month for a Georgia Trade Business

We check your market every month for changes in keyword demand, and we watch other markets and other trades for keyword trends worth moving on early. We optimize your Google Business Profile and your website for the terms your customers actually use, then track where you rank town by town and report what moved.

What changes from one trade to the next is the category on the profile, the pages the site needs, and the timing. A family clearing a garage on a Saturday afternoon is on a different clock than a builder ordering a boundary survey before a permit application, or a homeowner comparing three roofers a week after a hailstorm. That clock decides what a map pack spot is worth and what it takes to hold one.

Same-Day Trades

Big-Ticket, Slow-Shop Trades

When the buyer reads for three weeks first, the site does more of the work: complete service pages, visible credentials, and answers to the technical questions a shopper has. Run the same review push and the same page template across both and you overspend on one while underserving the other.

How the Work Changes From Trade to Trade

The scope stays the same shape whatever the trade. What moves is where the weight sits, and the difference is big enough to change what gets built first.

Junk removal, plumbing and tree service live on same-day decisions. A customer calls two or three companies inside the same few minutes, so review count and recency carry most of the weight and the profile has to look current on the day the storm comes through.

Roofing, general contracting and land surveying run the opposite way. The buyer reads for three weeks before anybody picks up a phone, which puts the work on the site: complete service pages, visible credentials, and answers to the technical question a shopper has already found somewhere else.

Then there are the trades where one customer is worth years. A dental practice, a law firm, a pest control route on annual renewals. One new patient, one new matter, one new contract is worth far more than the click that brought it, and the category on the profile decides more of that than most owners expect.

Frequently asked questions

My trade is not on your list. Can you still help?

Yes. The list is the trades with a page written for them so far, not a limit on who we work with. What changes between trades is the Google category, which pages the site needs, and the timing of the year. What stays the same is the work. Tell us the trade and the towns and the audit comes back the same way.

Do you work with more than one business in the same trade?

Not in the same market. One client per market per trade, so a plumber in a town gets the whole of the attention there and no competitor down the road is funding the same playbook. It does mean turning work away, and it is a real answer we have to give people.

Why does the Google category matter so much on these pages?

Because the primary category is most of what decides whether a business appears in the map pack for a given search, and it is the field owners most often get wrong. A broad label feels safer and matches fewer specific searches, so the profile ends up ranking for very little. Every trade page here starts with that call for a reason.

Is SEO different for a same-day trade than for a big-ticket one?

The scope is the same shape and the weight sits somewhere different. A same-day trade like junk removal or plumbing lives on review count and recency, because the customer calls three companies inside a few minutes. A big-ticket trade like roofing or land surveying gets read for three weeks first, so the site does more of the work with complete service pages and visible credentials.
