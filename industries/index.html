Why Map Pack Economics Change by Trade

Two businesses can sell completely different services, sit in completely different Google Business Profile categories, and still get the same generic "SEO for [industry]" pitch online. That pitch usually skips the part that matters: how the person searching for that trade behaves before they ever type anything into Google. A homeowner comparing three roofers after a hailstorm is deciding on a different clock than a builder ordering a boundary survey before a permit application, or a family calling three junk removal companies the same afternoon a garage needs clearing.

Those differences change what a map pack spot is worth and what actually wins it. A trade where customers call two or three competitors within the hour rewards whoever answers fastest and looks trustworthy on the spot, which usually means review count and recency carry outsized weight. A trade where the buyer researches for weeks before ever picking up the phone rewards a different mix: complete service pages, visible credentials, and a site that answers the technical questions a shopper has. Treating every trade the same way, the same review push, the same content template, wastes effort where it will not move anything and underinvests where it would.

The pages below are built around how each specific trade's customers actually search, not a swapped-in industry name over the same boilerplate.
