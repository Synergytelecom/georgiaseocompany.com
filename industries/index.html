Why One SEO Plan Does Not Fit Every Trade

Two businesses can sell completely different services, sit in completely different Google Business Profile categories, and still get handed the same "SEO for [industry]" pitch. That pitch skips the part that decides everything: how your customer behaves before they type a word into Google.

Three Trades on Three Different Clocks

A homeowner comparing three roofers after a hailstorm is deciding on a different clock than a builder ordering a boundary survey before a permit application, or a family calling three junk removal companies the same afternoon a garage needs clearing.

Those differences change what a map pack spot is worth and what actually wins it. A trade where customers call two or three competitors within the hour rewards whoever answers fastest and looks trustworthy on the spot, which usually means review count and recency carry outsized weight.

Where the Buyer Takes Their Time

A trade where the buyer researches for weeks before ever picking up the phone rewards a different mix: complete service pages, visible credentials, and a site that answers the technical questions a shopper has. Run the same review push and the same page template across both and you spend hard on the thing that will not move, and skip the thing that would.

The pages below are built around how each specific trade's customers actually search, not a swapped-in industry name over the same boilerplate.

The Fourteen Trades With a Page Today

Fourteen pages, each built around a different way that trade's customers decide:

  • Junk Removal SEO: same-day decisions, high job volume, review velocity as the main lever.
  • Tree Service SEO: emergency storm work next to scheduled trims, two different sales on two different clocks.
  • Land Surveyor SEO: infrequent, high-value, researched purchases with a long cycle and heavy referral weight.
  • Healthcare SEO: referral-heavy, symptom-driven searches, a relationship worth more than one visit.
  • Personal Injury Lawyer SEO: a single high-stakes search made in the days after an accident.
  • Law Firm SEO: unhurried, referral-driven searches for family, estate, and business matters.
  • Roofing SEO: a big-ticket, insurance-involved purchase most homeowners compare closely.
  • Dental SEO: a patient relationship worth years, not one visit, and the category call between Dentist and Dental clinic.
  • Moving Company SEO: a fresh search behind almost every booking, since so few households hire the same mover twice.
  • Pest Control SEO: a renewal-rate business more than a one-time-job trade, with Georgia's climate keeping demand steady year round.
  • HVAC SEO: three different jobs, each behaving like its own kind of sale: emergency repair, full replacement, and routine maintenance.
  • Plumber SEO: emergency calls that get answered, not compared, sitting next to planned repair work that gets shopped like anything else.
  • Electrician SEO: small repairs and full rewiring projects, priced and searched nothing alike despite coming from the same kind of customer.
  • Contractor SEO: a handful of large jobs a year, where photos work closer to a portfolio than a supporting trust signal.
