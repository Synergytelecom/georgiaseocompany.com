What Somebody Types Before They Call a Therapist

The search is specific because the reason is specific. Anxiety, grief, a marriage in trouble, a teenager who stopped talking, a panic attack at work last Tuesday.

Layered on top of that comes the practical filter: which insurance you take, whether you have an opening, and how far the drive is. A search like couples counseling in Rome or a therapist who takes Blue Cross carries all three questions at once.

Then there's the modality search, which comes from someone who has already read for a while. EMDR, DBT, play therapy, faith-based counseling. That searcher knows exactly what they want and will pass over a practice that never says whether it offers it. A single page listing fourteen approaches in a row satisfies neither the specific searcher nor the general one.

What Therapist SEO Is Up Against in the Results

Type a therapist search and the first screen belongs to national platforms and therapist directories. Those listings rank on the strength of the whole directory, and a private practice site will not outrank them on a broad term like counseling near me. Worth knowing before anybody sets a target.

That leaves two openings, and both are good ones. The map pack sits above the directories and shows local practices, not national platforms, so a claimed and complete profile competes there on even ground. And the long-tail searches, a specific approach plus a specific population plus a town name, are exactly what a directory profile is too generic to answer.

Those terms are quieter individually and they add up to people who call. Our Local SEO work goes after both at once.

The Counseling Category on Google Follows Your License

The right category here follows the credential on the wall, and there are several to choose between. Psychotherapist, Counselor, Mental health clinic, Marriage or relationship counselor and Family counselor each pull a different searcher. Psychologist is a licensed title with doctoral requirements behind it, so a licensed professional counselor or clinical social worker choosing it is claiming something the license doesn't support, and that's the mismatch we look for first on an existing profile.

The address field takes some thought too. Clients come to a counseling office, so the address belongs on the profile with the suite number, and a shared building means the suite is what tells somebody which door to open.

A group practice runs one profile per office location, with a page on the site for each clinician, their license, their specialties, and whether they're taking referrals right now.

Reviews Work Differently for a Counseling Practice

Every other trade in local search runs on asking for reviews. Counseling doesn't get to, and the reason is professional ethics rather than search strategy.

The codes that govern licensed clinicians restrict soliciting testimonials from current clients, and even a friendly public reply thanking someone by name confirms that person was in your care. Your license board and your own attorney set the line; the search work has to live inside it.

So trust gets built from everything else, and there's more of it than owners expect. Credentials and license numbers stated plainly. A real photograph of the actual room a client will sit in.

Fees and the insurance panels you're on, in writing. What a first session actually involves, minute by minute.

How fast somebody hears back after they reach out, and a phone number that reaches a person. Those signals do the work reviews do elsewhere, and they're all inside your control.

The Order the Work Runs In at a Therapy Practice

Nothing here needs a big launch. It needs the right order.

What a Therapy Practice Settles First

The profile gets claimed, the category matched to the license, the suite number added, hours set to when somebody can actually reach the office, and photos of the real space loaded. Then the site gets the pages that answer the practical filter: fees, insurance panels, what a first session looks like, and whether you're accepting new clients. Those four pages settle more inquiries than any amount of writing about theory.

What Comes After the Practical Pages

Now the specific pages go up, one per issue and approach you genuinely work with, each written in the words the person living it would use. Town pages follow for the communities clients drive in from. After that the year has its own pressure points.

January brings the people who spent a holiday with their family and decided something has to change. Late August brings the parents calling about a teenager three weeks into a school year.

The weeks around the holidays run heavy on grief and on couples work and run light on open appointment times, which is its own thing to write a page about. The monthly pass points the pages at what's coming, and the report shows which terms moved and where the inquiries came from. The results page carries the standings behind every account we run.

Frequently asked questions

Can a therapist ask clients for Google reviews?

Tread carefully. The ethics codes that govern licensed clinicians restrict soliciting testimonials from current clients, and a public reply that acknowledges someone can itself disclose that they were a client. Get the rule from your own license board and your attorney, then build visibility from credentials, clear fees, real photos, and a fast response path.

Which Google category should a licensed professional counselor choose?

Counselor or Psychotherapist, depending on how the practice presents itself, with Marriage or relationship counselor or Family counselor as a closer fit when that is the main work. Psychologist is a protected title with doctoral licensure behind it and should only be used by someone who holds it.

What about a practice that only sees clients by telehealth?

It changes what the profile can honestly claim. Google verification is built around a real location, so a telehealth-only practice needs its address and service area set to reflect how it actually operates rather than borrowing an office it does not use. The website then carries the geography, with pages for the areas you are licensed to serve.

Should each clinician in a group practice get their own page?

Yes. Clients pick a person, not a logo, and a page per clinician with the license, the specialties, the populations they work with, and current availability answers the question a referral source is asking. The Google profile stays one per office location.

Should a counseling practice publish its fees?

Publishing them filters better than hiding them. Somebody who leaves the page to go hunt for your rate usually stays gone, and a practice that states the session fee, the insurance panels it is on, and whether a sliding scale exists spends less time on inquiries that were never going to fit.
