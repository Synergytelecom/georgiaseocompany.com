What Carpet Cleaning Customers Search, and What the Map Pack Rewards

The searches are shaped like the problem. Pet odor removal. Pet stain cleaning. Upholstery cleaning. Tile and grout cleaning. Area rug cleaning. Move out carpet cleaning. Water extraction after a supply line let go. Behind each one is an event with a date on it: a landlord inspection, a closing, a new puppy, a party, an allergy season that finally got somebody's attention.

By then they've decided to hire somebody, and the search is only about picking which one, this week. Price comes up in the first thirty seconds, because almost everybody opens by asking what three rooms costs.

Distance First, Then the Review Dates

Distance sorts the pack, then reviews and completeness settle the order. Most companies here hide the address, which is correct when the work happens in the customer's house. Google still measures from where the business operates, so the towns worth chasing radiate out from the driveway the van parks in.

Recency matters more here than in a trade somebody shops for three weeks. A customer choosing today reads the review dates alongside the star average, and a profile that stopped collecting last spring looks tired beside one adding a review most weeks. Photos of the truck mount, the technician in uniform, and a before and after on a real stain beat a stock living room.

The Carpet Cleaning Category, and Two Mismatches That Cost Money

Price, Service Pages, and Reviews for a Carpet Cleaning Company

The Price Question the Site Refuses to Answer

This vertical trained its own customers to shop on a room count special, and then most of its websites answer the price question with a form. A caller who finds no starting structure assumes the worst and taps the next card. A per room or per square foot starting point, plus a sentence about what changes it, books more jobs than another paragraph of company history. It also heads off the complaint this trade is known for, a technician quoting one number at the door after the phone quoted another.

One General Page for Four Different Jobs

Carpet, upholstery, tile and grout, area rugs, pet treatment, and commercial work each get typed as their own search, and each reads better as its own page. The commercial page in particular has to be written for a property manager or an office manager buying on a schedule, which is a different pitch from the one that wins a homeowner with a stain in the hallway.

Reviews From Customers Who Come Back Every Other Year

A household cleans its carpets once a year or once every two, so the customer who loved the work two seasons ago hasn't had an occasion to say so since. Waiting for reviews to arrive on their own leaves a thin profile on a company with hundreds of jobs behind it. The reliable moment is the end of the appointment, with the technician asking while the floor still looks transformed. Property managers and agents who book monthly rarely review at all, since a vendor relationship never feels like an occasion, and those accounts are worth one ask a year at a natural checkpoint.

Frequently asked questions

What Google category should a carpet cleaning company use?

Carpet cleaning service as the primary. Upholstery cleaning service, Water damage restoration service, Air duct cleaning service, and Commercial cleaning service belong in the secondary slots only when the company genuinely performs that work. House cleaning service is the wrong home for a carpet cleaner and pulls a different buyer entirely.

Should carpet cleaning prices go on the website?

A starting structure, yes. Customers in this trade open with the price question, and a per room or per square foot starting point with a sentence about what changes it keeps the caller on the page. It also reduces the door quote complaint the industry has earned for itself.

Should a carpet cleaning company hide its address on Google?

Yes when the work happens in the customer's home and nobody visits the shop. Hiding the address doesn't change where Google measures distance from, so the pack you can win still centers on your real location, which is why the service area needs to match the routes the vans actually run.

How does a carpet cleaner get reviews from customers who only call every other year?

Ask at the end of the appointment. The customer is standing on a floor that looks transformed, the difference is right in front of them, and the technician is the person they just watched work. A text or a card with a direct link, handed over on site, beats an email sent after the van has left.
