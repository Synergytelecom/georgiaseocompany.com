HVAC contractor, or Air conditioning repair service?

We don't have an HVAC client. What's below is checkable on your own screen.

  1. Open your Google Business Profile, tap Edit profile, then Business category.
  2. One Primary field sits on top, with room for nine more beneath it. Ten in total is Google's published cap.
  3. Type air conditioning into the Primary field. Back come Air conditioning contractor, Air conditioning repair service, Air conditioning system supplier and Air duct cleaning service. Clear it, type HVAC, and you get HVAC contractor.

Air conditioning repair service is the one picked by mistake, and it's an honest mistake, since it describes what the crew did every day last August. As a primary it aims the profile at the repair searcher. HVAC contractor is the primary for a company that installs and services both heating and cooling, and it's where the change-out shopper lands. A change-out (the trade's word for a full system replacement) is the biggest ticket on the board. Air conditioning contractor fits a cooling-focused shop that does little heating install or repair. Heating contractor, Furnace repair service and Air duct cleaning service belong in the nine additional slots.

What a No-Cool Call Is Worth Against a Change-Out

Three job types come off one listing, and averaging them produces a figure that describes none of them. Last August's invoices sort into three piles:

  • No-cool ticket. Won on the phone in under a minute, at a price almost nobody negotiates. Clusters in July and August, then again on the first hard freeze in January as a no-heat.
  • Change-out. Won after two or three quotes and a week of reading, at many times a repair ticket. Trails the heat by a month or two, since the system that limped through August gets replaced in September or October.
  • Planned maintenance agreement. Two small visits a year, sold ahead of the season, and the only one you can put on a calendar twelve months out.

That third one is worth more than its own revenue, because it's the list your change-outs come off. Reviews split the same way: the customer whose house is cool again by dinner writes one unprompted, and the spring tune-up customer has to be asked inside the visit.
