Why Isn't Your HVAC Listing Showing Up for "ac repair near me"?

On an ac repair near me search in Rome or Dalton, Local Services Ads take the top block, the one with the green Google Guaranteed check, and that block is paid, billed per lead. We work the map pack under it, three listings deep, and the organic results below that.

The first question is where your work comes from today. A shop fed by a home warranty dispatch queue or a builder's account is running a relationship business, and search moves less for it than for a shop whose phone rings from strangers.

No-cool tickets arrive in clusters, on the days the heat index climbs, and whoever placed one has stopped reading. They tap Call on the map card, and voicemail sends them to the second listing.

HVAC Contractor, or Air Conditioning Repair Service?

Google allows ten categories: one primary, nine more beneath it. The primary is the one that aims the listing, and in this trade it's where the money is won or lost.

Air conditioning repair service is the one picked by mistake, and it's an honest mistake, since it describes what the crew did every day last August. As a primary it aims the profile at the repair searcher and leaves the replacement shopper to somebody else. HVAC contractor is the primary we set for a company that installs and services both heating and cooling, and it's where the change-out shopper lands. Air conditioning contractor fits a cooling-focused shop that does little heating install or repair. Heating contractor, Furnace repair service and Air duct cleaning service belong in the nine additional slots.

Three Jobs Off One Listing: Repair, Replacement, Maintenance

Three job types come off one listing, and averaging them produces a figure that describes none of them. Each one gets its own page and its own treatment:

  • No-cool ticket. Won on the phone in under a minute, at a price almost nobody negotiates. Clusters in July and August, then again on the first hard freeze in January as a no-heat.
  • Change-out. Won after two or three quotes and a week of reading, at many times a repair ticket. Trails the heat by a month or two, since the system that limped through August gets replaced in September or October.
  • Planned maintenance agreement. Two small visits a year, sold ahead of the season, and the only one you can put on a calendar twelve months out.

That third one is worth more than its own revenue, because it's the list your change-outs come off. Reviews split the same way: the customer whose house is cool again by dinner writes one unprompted, and the spring tune-up customer has to be asked inside the visit.

We check your market every month for changes in keyword demand and watch that swing between cooling season and heating season, so the visibility is already up when the calls start.

Replacement Shoppers Read Before They Call

A replacement shopper reads for a week before the phone comes out, and they search like it: a model number, a SEER2 rating, a refrigerant name. We watch those terms every month and build pages that answer them.

So the general page splits four ways: repair for the no-cool and no-heat calls, replacement and install for the shopper, maintenance agreements for the renewal, and system type, where heat pumps and ductless mini-splits get named. Commercial rooftop work sits under a commercial HVAC category with pages of its own.

Your Conditioned Air Contractor license number, Class I or Class II, is verifiable at verify.sos.ga.gov, and it belongs on the profile beside a photo of your own technician on a job site.

The replacement page carries what that shopper is reading for: what a change-out involves, how long the crew is in the house, what the efficiency numbers mean in a Georgia summer, and what financing looks like. Every one of those questions gets asked on the first call anyway, and a page that answers them ahead of time shortens the call and wins the comparison.
