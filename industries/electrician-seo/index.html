A Dead Outlet and a Panel Upgrade, One Listing

Electrician is the primary category, with no second listing type competing for that slot. No electrician is on our client list yet. What separates one listing from the next is everything built around that category.

Review timing follows job size. Someone who just had a panel upgraded or a room rewired will describe the work in detail when asked, because they thought about it for a week before saying yes. A dead outlet or a breaker that wouldn't reset is forgotten by dinner unless somebody asks on the spot, so that ask goes into the visit.

We split the service pages by job size as much as job type: small service calls and repairs, panel upgrades, EV charger installs, whole-house rewiring, and new-construction wiring. A homeowner with one dead outlet and a homeowner planning a full panel upgrade are searching completely different terms with completely different urgency behind them. Our Google Business Profile work keeps those permit and job photos going up every month.

New Construction and Old Wiring, Both at Once

New subdivisions generate one kind of electrical demand, and older established neighborhoods generate a completely different kind, often within the same county. Run a route sheet through Powder Springs, a Cobb County city near 19,630 residents, then another through Hiram, in Paulding County at just over 5,520, and both kinds turn up inside an afternoon. Newer houses bring new-construction wiring and, increasingly, EV charger installs, a market that barely existed a decade ago. Older houses still carry panels and wiring installed decades before current safety standards existed, and those generate a steady stream of rewiring and panel-replacement calls with nothing to do with new construction at all.

Planning Ahead of the Next Outage

Whole-house standby generator installs sit alongside both, a request that has grown as north Georgia homeowners weigh how long a severe storm can knock out power to a neighborhood. That pulls in another kind of searcher, someone planning ahead of the next outage, and it earns a service page of its own.

Shopping for the Charger First

New electric vehicle owners are driving panel upgrades too, since an older panel usually can't carry a Level 2 charger without one. That homeowner searches differently than either a repair call or a rewire, because they're shopping for the charger and finding out about the panel second. Whole-home surge protection gets bundled with a panel upgrade often enough to be worth its own line on that page.

Frequently asked questions

Does whole-house generator installation deserve its own page, separate from other project work?

Yes. That searcher is thinking ahead to the next time the power goes out, not responding to something already broken in the house today, a different mindset than someone booking a panel upgrade, and one page rarely serves both kinds of searcher well.

How does an electrician build a builder and contractor referral pipeline in the first place?

Often by asking to be added to a builder's preferred-vendor list directly, rather than waiting for the relationship to happen on its own. A single new-construction subdivision can produce years of repeat work from one relationship, which is why it is worth pursuing deliberately rather than treating it as luck.

What Google category fits an electrician?

Electrician, and there is no competing category worth weighing against it.

Why do project jobs generate more detailed reviews than small service calls?

Bigger jobs feel like decisions to the customer, so they tend to describe the work when asked. A dead outlet or a reset breaker is easy to forget about unless someone asks for a review right after.
