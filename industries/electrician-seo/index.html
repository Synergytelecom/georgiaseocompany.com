A Dead Outlet and a Panel Upgrade, One Listing

Electrician works as the primary category, with no second listing type competing for that slot. What separates one listing from the next is everything built around that category. When a review shows up, and how good it is, depends on the size of the job. Someone who just had a panel upgraded or a room rewired will describe the work in detail when asked, because it was a real decision. A dead outlet or a breaker that would not reset is forgotten by dinner unless you ask on the spot. No electrician client's result is attached to this page. The category call and that review pattern are what it stands on.

Service pages fit better split by job type: small service calls and repairs, panel upgrades, EV charger installs, whole-house rewiring, and new-construction wiring, since a homeowner with one dead outlet and a homeowner planning a full panel upgrade are searching completely different terms with completely different urgency behind them. Photos do something specific here beyond the truck-and-technician shot. Show that permits got pulled on the big jobs, a panel upgrade or a rewire, because unpermitted work comes back on a homeowner years later during a home sale or an insurance claim, and more of them know it now. New Construction and Old Wiring, Both at Once

Growth suburbs generate one kind of electrical demand, and older established neighborhoods generate a completely different kind, often within the same county. Powder Springs, a Cobb County city near 19,630 residents, and Hiram, in Paulding County at just over 5,520, both continue adding new construction that needs wiring done right the first time, alongside an EV charger install market that barely existed a decade ago. At the same time, older homes throughout both towns still carry electrical panels and wiring installed decades before current safety standards existed, generating a separate, steady stream of rewiring and panel-replacement calls that has nothing to do with new construction at all.

A third category sits alongside both: whole-house standby generator installs, a request that has grown as north Georgia homeowners weigh how long a severe storm can knock out power to a neighborhood. That work pulls in yet another kind of searcher, someone planning ahead of the next outage rather than reacting to a wiring problem in the house right now, and it deserves its own service page rather than getting folded into panel upgrades. New electric vehicle owners are driving panel upgrades too, since an older panel usually cannot carry a Level 2 charger without one. That homeowner searches differently than either a repair call or a rewire, because they are shopping for the charger and finding out about the panel second. That deserves a service page, not a footnote on the panel page. Whole-home surge protection is another add-on increasingly bundled with a panel upgrade, worth its own line on that service page rather than assumed as automatically included.

What a Service Call and a Rewire Are Worth

Average a quick service call against a whole-house rewire and the number describes neither one. Better questions, all three answerable from your own job list. How far apart are a small service call and a full project like a panel upgrade or a rewire in your market? How many of your project quotes get compared against two or three other electricians before the homeowner signs? And how much of your current work came through a referral, a past customer, another trade, a builder calling you back project after project, rather than a cold search? If the builder calls you back every project, search matters less to you. If nobody calls you back, search is the pipeline. Either way, it is worth knowing which one you are.

Frequently asked questions

Does whole-house generator installation deserve its own page, separate from other project work?

Yes. That searcher is thinking ahead to the next time the power goes out, not responding to something already broken in the house today, a different mindset than someone booking a panel upgrade, and one page rarely serves both kinds of searcher well.

How does an electrician build a builder and contractor referral pipeline in the first place?

Often by asking to be added to a builder's preferred-vendor list directly, rather than waiting for the relationship to happen on its own. A single new-construction subdivision can produce years of repeat work from one relationship, which is why it is worth pursuing deliberately rather than treating it as luck.

What Google category fits an electrician?

Electrician, and there is no competing category worth weighing against it.

Why do project jobs generate more detailed reviews than small service calls?

Bigger jobs feel like real decisions to the customer, so they tend to describe the work when asked. A dead outlet or a reset breaker is easy to forget about unless someone asks for a review right after.
