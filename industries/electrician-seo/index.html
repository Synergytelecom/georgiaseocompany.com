Electrician SEO has to cover both timelines, because the same homeowner searches for a dead outlet and a full panel upgrade.

A Dead Outlet and a Panel Upgrade, One Listing

Electrician is the primary category, with no second listing type competing for that slot. What separates one listing from the next is everything built around it: the pages, the photos, and the review pace.

A homeowner with one dead outlet and a homeowner planning a full panel upgrade search completely different terms with completely different urgency behind them, and both land on the same map card. One types electrician near me and calls the first listing that answers.

The other reads three profiles, twice, before dialing any of them. Our Google Business Profile work keeps that card answering both.

Review Timing Follows Job Size

Somebody who just had a panel upgraded or a room rewired will describe the work in detail when asked, because they thought about it for a week before saying yes. A dead outlet or a breaker that wouldn't reset is forgotten by dinner unless somebody asks on the spot, so that ask goes into the visit itself.

Both kinds count, and they arrive on different schedules. Building the small-call ask into the technician's close-out is what keeps the review count moving in the months between big projects.

Where SEO for Electricians Splits the Service Pages

The service pages split by job size as much as job type: small service calls and repairs, panel upgrades, EV charger installs, whole-house rewiring, and new-construction wiring.

Each of those pages says what the job involves, whether it needs a permit, and roughly how long the power stays off. A homeowner planning a rewire wants that last one answered before anything else, and almost no electrician site answers it.

What a Homeowner Checks on an Electrician Before Calling

Electrical work makes homeowners cautious in a way plumbing doesn't, because the failure mode is a fire rather than a flood. So the profile carries the license number where it can be read, proof of insurance, and photos of pulled permits on the larger jobs. Unpermitted work comes back on a homeowner years later during a home sale or an insurance claim, and more of them know that now.

Most electrician listings carry none of it. A homeowner comparing two profiles with similar star counts picks the one that proved it was licensed, so those permit and job photos go up every month.

New Construction and Old Wiring, Both at Once

New subdivisions generate one kind of electrical demand and older established neighborhoods generate a completely different kind, often inside the same county. Powder Springs over in Cobb County and Hiram in Paulding County both produce plenty of each.

Newer houses bring new-construction wiring and, increasingly, EV charger installs, a market that barely existed a decade ago. Older houses still carry panels and wiring installed decades before current safety standards, and those generate a steady stream of rewiring and panel-replacement calls with nothing to do with new construction at all.

Planning Ahead of the Next Outage

Whole-house standby generator installs sit alongside both, a request that has grown as north Georgia homeowners weigh how long a severe storm can knock out power to a neighborhood. That pulls in another kind of searcher, somebody planning ahead of the next outage, and it earns a service page of its own.

Shopping for the Charger First

New electric vehicle owners are driving panel upgrades too, since an older panel usually can't carry a Level 2 charger without one. That homeowner searches differently than either a repair call or a rewire, because they're shopping for the charger and finding out about the panel second. Whole-home surge protection gets bundled with a panel upgrade often enough to earn its own line on that page.

Frequently asked questions

Does whole-house generator installation deserve its own page, separate from other project work?

Yes. That searcher is thinking ahead to the next time the power goes out, not responding to something already broken today. It's a different mindset than a panel upgrade, and one page rarely serves both well.

How does an electrician build a builder and contractor referral pipeline in the first place?

By asking to be added to a builder's preferred-vendor list directly, rather than waiting for the relationship to happen on its own. A single new-construction subdivision can produce years of repeat work from one relationship, which is why it's worth pursuing deliberately.

What Google category fits an electrician?

Electrician, and there's no competing category worth weighing against it. Electrical installation service and Electric generator shop fill secondary slots when the work is genuinely there.

Why do project jobs generate more detailed reviews than small service calls?

Bigger jobs feel like decisions to the customer, so they tend to describe the work when asked. A dead outlet or a reset breaker is easy to forget about unless somebody asks for a review right after.
