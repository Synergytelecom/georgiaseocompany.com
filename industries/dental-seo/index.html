One New Patient Is Often a Whole Household

A household that moves to a new town has to find a dentist for everyone in it. One relocation puts four new patients in play at once, and most of them pick a practice by searching, which makes that first search worth more than the single appointment it looks like.

The household that books brings years of recall visits with it, plus whatever restorative or cosmetic work comes up along the way. How new patients find you decides how much of the work search is doing. A practice whose new patients mostly arrive on a neighbor's recommendation is using search as a supplement. A practice in a town full of new arrivals who know nobody is using it to get found at all.

The answer to that question sets how hard SEO for a dental practice has to pull.

SEO for Dentists Starts at the Category Field

Dentist fits a solo or small practice. A larger practice built around multiple providers under one shared brand more often fits Dental clinic, and a practice built around one dominant specialty, an orthodontist or a pediatric dentist, has its own closer-matching category. Getting this wrong leaves a practice buried under searches it was never positioned to win, exactly the kind of gap our free audit surfaces on an existing profile.

Why Routine Visits Produce No Reviews

Nothing dramatic happens at a routine cleaning. Nobody walks out of a six-month checkup reaching for their phone the way they would after an emergency, so the reviews only show up if you ask. Asking after every visit, and not only the memorable ones, is what builds a review base worth having, and it belongs in the monthly work.

What Each Service Page Should Solve

We build the pages around what a patient is trying to solve: general and preventive care, cosmetic work, restorative and implant work, and orthodontic referrals, each a different search with a different question behind it. A page organized around which insurance networks you accept earns its place too, since that question often decides a booking before the procedure does. Photos work differently here than on a repair trade. A picture of the team and the room a patient will sit in settles the anxiety a lot of people carry into a first dental visit, which a stock waiting room can't do.

What Dental SEO Covers Every Month

The month runs on a short list. Review requests go out from checkout. A post and a few photos go up on the profile. Insurance and hours get checked against what the front desk is actually telling people. The service pages get measured against what patients typed, and new-arrival towns get pages as the practice's draw widens.

The report names the searches you ranked for, the towns they came from, and how many calls and booking clicks the profile produced. Requests for directions count too, since in this trade that number is usually somebody already on the way in.

Where New Patients Are Moving In

A new arrival starts from zero. A family that has just unpacked in Kennesaw, or in Holly Springs up in Cherokee County, has no dentist, no hygienist it likes, and no reason to pick one name over another except what a search puts in front of it. Usually that search is dentist near me, run once, on a phone, in the first week or two after the boxes come off the truck. A practice in a town like that's competing for households that haven't chosen a dentist at all, which is a different job than holding onto the ones you already have.

Those searches show up with a town name attached, because a parent booking a first appointment for two kids is measuring the drive as much as the practice. A page that names the town and the streets nearby answers that question before the phone call does.

So the town pages get built where the households are landing, and the profile service area gets set to the radius a family will genuinely drive for a cleaning. Our results page has the current client rankings on it.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if a solo practice adds a second dentist and a second location?

Reconsider Dental clinic at that point, even if Dentist worked fine as a solo practice. Two locations under one shared name also usually make more sense as two separate profiles, since a patient searching a specific town wants the closer location first.

Who on staff should be the one asking for a review?

Whoever checks the patient out. The ask lands while the visit is still fresh and the patient is standing right there, which beats a text sent hours later once the day has moved on.

Should service pages be organized by procedure or by insurance accepted?

Both. A procedure page answers the specific treatment someone is searching for, and a separate page listing accepted insurance plans often decides whether that patient books at all, sometimes before the procedure question comes up.

Do photos matter differently for a pediatric or family-focused practice?

Yes, more. A parent researching a first visit for a nervous child is looking for signs the office is set up for kids: a waiting area, staff photos that look approachable, something past a clinical equipment shot. That reassurance often decides the appointment ahead of credentials.
