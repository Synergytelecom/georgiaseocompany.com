How Georgia Homeowners Actually Search for Solar

The early searches are questions. Is solar worth it in Georgia. How do solar panels work. Do I need a battery. Can I go solar without a loan. Nobody typing those is ready to sign, and most of them will sign with somebody inside a year.

The middle stretch gets concrete: solar installers near me, solar companies plus a town name, panel and inverter brands, warranty terms, permits and inspections. The last stretch is a company name typed straight into the bar, sometimes at nine at night after a canvasser left a door hanger, often with the word reviews behind it. That brand search decides more solar sales than any keyword in the trade, and whatever comes back is what the homeowner believes.

Alongside it runs a quieter lane almost nobody writes for: a warehouse roof, a poultry house, a church, a school district pricing a canopy over a parking lot.

What a Solar Site Usually Gets Wrong

It gets built as one long sales page aimed at the middle of the decision, with nothing for the person three months out and nothing for the person whose panels are already on the roof. The pages that win this trade are the ones a shopper can come back to a fourth time in June.

The Solar Category Call, and the Retail Read Beside It

Solar energy contractor is the primary for a company that designs and installs. Solar energy equipment supplier reads as retail, so a listing sitting there gets treated like somewhere to buy a panel rather than somebody to hire. Solar hot water system supplier answers a separate search for a separate product. Electrician and Roofing contractor earn secondary slots only when the company's own crew genuinely does that work, and both matter, because a homeowner thinking about panels is often thinking about a roof in the same week.

Service area is the setting that costs solar companies the most. Permitting happens jurisdiction by jurisdiction, and a county the company has never pulled a permit in is a county where the first job takes twice as long.

Draw it where the crew and the paperwork can both go. Our free audit covers the category, the service area, and what the brand searches turn up right now.

Incentive Pages Go Stale Faster Than Any Solar Page Should

Nothing in this trade changes as fast as the money. Federal treatment of residential solar has shifted more than once.

The terms a utility offers for power sent back to the grid get set by regulators and revised. And a Georgia customer's utility depends entirely on their address, since some towns buy power from a municipal system, some from an electric membership cooperative, and the rest from Georgia Power, each with its own interconnection paperwork.

A page stating something that expired does more damage than an empty page. The homeowner finds it, calls, hears a different answer, and quietly stops believing the rest of the site. So the durable version explains the mechanism: what interconnection is, who approves it, roughly how long approval takes, and what happens to the meter.

Anything with a figure in it gets dated and re-read on a schedule. Our content marketing work builds that library and keeps the perishable parts flagged.

The Orphaned Solar System Is an Open Search Lane

Companies in this trade open and close, and every closure leaves homeowners with panels on the roof, a monitoring app nobody supports, and a warranty printed on letterhead from a company that stopped answering.

Those people search with real urgency: solar panel repair near me, inverter replacement, my solar company went out of business, panel removal and reinstall for a roof replacement.

Almost nobody in the trade publishes for it, because service work carries thinner margins than installations. That leaves the quietest ground in solar wide open. High intent, low competition, and a service customer who buys a battery two years later. Roofing companies search it too, since panels have to come off before a tear off and go back on afterward.

Solar Reviews Come After the First Utility Bill

Install day sits too early for the ask. The panels are up, permission to operate hasn't arrived, and the thing the customer bought hasn't done anything yet. Asking then produces a review about how polite the crew was, which persuades nobody comparing three quotes.

The moments that work come later. The day permission to operate lands and the system switches on.

The first full billing cycle after that, when the utility statement shows up and the number is finally real, because that review is about the outcome and the next shopper reads it twice. And after a service call resolved quickly, since that's where a company proves it will still be around.

What reviewers discuss is narrow. Whether the production estimate the salesperson gave matched what showed up. Whether the timeline held, since permits and utility approval stretch and a company that explained that up front gets forgiven while a quiet one gets written up. Whether the roof leaked. Whether anybody answered the phone after the install.

Door to door selling puts its own weight on the profile, since canvassed customers write about pressure and about promises, and replies stay calm and specific to what the company controls. Photographs should be Georgia roofs: an array on asphalt shingle, a ground mount in a pasture, an inverter and disconnect on a brick wall.

Where Solar SEO Puts the First Quarter

The brand surface gets fixed first, because it pays fastest. A canvassed homeowner types the company name that same night, and the listing, the reviews, the replies, and the first screen of results for that name decide the sale before any keyword work matters. Category and service area get set in the same pass, against the counties where the company can actually permit.

The service and repair pages come second, ahead of the install pages. They rank sooner because so few competitors bother, and they produce calls from people who already own equipment.

The process pages follow: permitting, interconnection, timelines, what to expect and when. The early-stage question pages get started in the same stretch and get judged much later, since those take longest to place and pay longest once placed.

Install-intent ground in a metro market has to wait, because national companies hold it with real advertising budgets. Eric Kirby does the work and writes the report, one person start to finish, so the account never lands with somebody who has to be taught what interconnection means.

Solar buyers have learned to ask whether a company will still exist in five years, which is a fair question to put to an agency too, and the answerable half of it is already public: client rankings showing where the work stands this month, no phone call required.

Frequently asked questions

Which Google category fits a solar installation company?

Solar energy contractor for a company that designs and installs. Solar energy equipment supplier reads as retail and pulls the listing toward shoppers looking for panels to buy. Add Electrician or Roofing contractor as secondaries only when the company employs crews doing that work.

Should a solar website publish incentive numbers?

Publish how the incentives work, keep any figure dated, and re-read those pages on a schedule, because federal treatment and utility terms both change. A stale number on a live page costs more trust than an absent one, since the customer who finds it and then hears otherwise stops believing the rest of the site.

Is solar repair work worth writing pages about?

It's the corner of the trade with almost nobody in it. Owners of systems installed by companies that closed are searching for inverter replacement, panel removal for a roof job, and monitoring help, and very few installers publish for them. Those pages produce calls sooner than install pages do, and service customers buy batteries later.

How soon after installation should a solar customer be asked for a review?

After the system switches on, and again once the first full utility bill arrives. An install day ask gets a review about the crew before the customer knows whether the system performs, and the review that persuades the next buyer is the one written after the bill.
