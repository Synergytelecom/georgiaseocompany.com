Every Review Has to Come From a New Customer

We set Mover as the primary category. A company that also rents storage alongside its moving jobs can add Moving and storage service as a second listing. Mover stays primary, because it matches how most people search for this in the first place, and it's the call our Google Business Profile work starts with on a new account. We don't have a moving company on the client list yet.

Reviews work differently here than in almost any other trade. Your happiest customer won't leave a second one, because that household isn't moving again for years. Every review you have comes from a job you just did. So the ask goes to every customer, worked into the close-out the same way the final walkthrough already is, and that's how the count keeps climbing on new work.

A family moving across town and a household relocating across state lines are comparing on completely different terms, so we build a page per kind of move: local moves inside the same metro, long-distance and interstate, full-service packing on top of the move itself, and specialty handling for large or delicate items. Photos do more here than most owners expect. Branded trucks, uniformed crews, and furniture visibly wrapped and secured inside the truck, since a customer is handing strangers everything they own and would like to see how it gets treated before signing anything.

People Moving In Are Demand Nobody Owns Yet

Some of the trades on this site see demand spike after a storm or a hard freeze. Moving companies run on something quieter: households changing address. Douglasville is the Douglas County seat, near 40,540 residents. Villa Rica, just over 20,090, sits mostly in Carroll County. Neither town holds still. Households arrive, households leave, and every one of those moves needed a truck.

Some of that is local, one address to another inside the same town. The rest arrives from somewhere else, and that inbound work is the piece a mover with no local reputation yet can still win, because the person searching doesn't know anybody's name either.

Peak Season and the Rest of the Year

Residential moves pile into a few months around school calendars and lease turnover, then drop off hard, so a job booked in June says something different about the year than the same job in January.

Stretch of the yearWhat happens
Peak moving monthsCall volume climbs hard, crews run at full capacity, and quotes get compared quickly since competing movers are all busy at once.
The rest of the yearCall volume drops, and the moves that do come in are often less price-sensitive and more willing to book further ahead.

Which stretch to push visibility into comes down to your own capacity. Crews booked solid in June get nothing from more June calls. The same visibility in February fills a calendar that was going to sit empty, which is where the money in this trade usually is, so we time the work to the months your crews aren't already booked.

Frequently asked questions

Does a moving broker need a different category than an actual moving company?

Yes, and getting this wrong shows up as complaints. A broker that arranges moves through other carriers rather than running its own trucks should not list itself as Mover, since that category implies the business performs the move directly, and a mismatch generates complaints from customers expecting the company on the profile to be the one showing up on move day.

Why is review growth different for a moving company than for a recurring service business?

Because almost no household needs the same moving company again within a year or two. The review count only grows by asking new customers consistently, since there is rarely a second job from the same happy customer to fall back on.

Should office and commercial moves get their own page, separate from residential?

Yes, since the buyer and the timeline are both different. A business relocating an office is usually planning around a weekend to avoid downtime and cares about equipment handling and minimal disruption, concerns a residential moving page rarely addresses well.

What if a slow month happens during peak season instead of the off months?

That is a different signal entirely, and worth taking seriously. A quiet week in June or July, when most of the industry is at its busiest, points to a visibility or reputation problem rather than the calendar, since the demand causing everyone else's phones to ring is still there in the market.
