What Wins a Moving Company Listing

The correct primary category is Mover. A company that also rents out storage space alongside its moving jobs may add Moving and storage service as a second listing, but Mover is the category that matches how most people search for this service in the first place, the kind of call our Google Business Profile work starts with on every new account.

Review velocity runs on an unusual rule in this trade: a genuinely happy customer will almost never generate a second review, because the same household is unlikely to need another move again for years, if ever. That means the review count has to be built entirely from new relationships, one job at a time, with no returning customer to lean on later the way a recurring trade can. Asking every customer, not just the ones who happened to mention how smooth the move went, is the only way this actually compounds.

Service pages fit better split by the kind of move: local moves within the same metro, long-distance or interstate moves, full-service packing added on top of the move itself, and specialty handling for large or delicate items, since a family moving across town is comparing on completely different terms than one relocating across state lines. Photos carry real weight too: branded trucks, uniformed crews, and furniture visibly wrapped and secured inside the truck, since a customer is trusting strangers with everything they own and wants to see that trust is earned before signing anything.

Growth Towns Generate Moves Even Without a Storm or a Season

Some of the trades on this site see demand spike after a storm or a hard freeze. Moving companies see something steadier: population growth itself. Douglasville, the Douglas County seat near 40,540 residents, and Villa Rica, just over 20,090 and sitting mostly in Carroll County, both continue adding new households, and every one of those households needed a moving truck at some point to get there. A town with real population growth generates inbound moving demand on top of whatever ordinary local moving already happens inside it, and that inbound demand is exactly the kind of search a mover with no local reputation yet can still win.

The Math Runs on a Calendar, Not an Average Job

An average job value means less here than the calendar it gets measured against. Most residential moves cluster hard into a few months tied to school calendars and lease turnover, then fall off sharply the rest of the year, and a business planning its year around one typical month gets caught flat-footed by that swing every time.

Stretch of the yearWhat tends to happen
Peak moving monthsCall volume climbs hard, crews run at full capacity, and quotes get compared quickly since competing movers are all busy at once.
The rest of the yearCall volume drops, but the moves that do come in are often less price-sensitive and more willing to book further ahead.

What decides whether a given business should lean harder into search visibility during the slow stretch or the busy one comes down to its own capacity: a crew already booked solid in peak season gains little from more visibility it cannot service, while the same visibility during the quiet months can fill a calendar that would otherwise sit empty.

Frequently asked questions

Does a moving broker need a different category than an actual moving company?

Yes, and getting this wrong causes real problems. A broker that arranges moves through other carriers rather than running its own trucks should not list itself as Mover, since that category implies the business performs the move directly, and a mismatch tends to generate complaints from customers expecting the company on the profile to be the one showing up on move day.

Why is review growth different for a moving company than for a recurring service business?

Because almost no household needs the same moving company again within a year or two. The review count only grows by asking new customers consistently, since there is rarely a second job from the same happy customer to fall back on.

Should office and commercial moves get their own page, separate from residential?

Yes, since the buyer and the timeline are both different. A business relocating an office is usually planning around a weekend to avoid downtime and cares about equipment handling and minimal disruption, concerns a residential moving page rarely addresses well.

What if a slow month happens during peak season instead of the off months?

That is a different signal entirely, and worth taking seriously. A quiet week in June or July, when most of the industry is at its busiest, points to a visibility or reputation problem rather than the calendar, since the demand causing everyone else's phones to ring is still there in the market.
