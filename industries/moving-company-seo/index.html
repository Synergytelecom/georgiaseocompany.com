Every Review Has to Come From a New Customer

Reviews work differently here than in almost any other trade. Your happiest customer won't leave a second one, because that household isn't moving again for years. Every review you have comes from a job you just did.

So the ask goes to every customer, worked into the close-out the same way the final walkthrough already is. That's how the count keeps climbing on new work, and it's the piece that decays fastest if a month gets skipped.

SEO for Moving Companies Starts at the Category Field

Mover is the primary category, because it matches how most people search for this in the first place. A company that also rents storage alongside its moving jobs can add Moving and storage service as a second listing type. Mover stays primary, and it's the call our Google Business Profile work starts with on a new account.

Photos do more here than most owners expect: branded trucks, uniformed crews, and furniture visibly wrapped and secured inside the truck. A customer is handing strangers everything they own and would like to see how it gets treated before signing anything. A visible USDOT number belongs on the profile too, since it separates a licensed interstate carrier from one operating only within Georgia, and a customer comparing two quotes can look it up in a minute.

A Page for Every Kind of Move

A family moving across town and a household relocating across state lines are comparing on completely different terms, so we build a page per kind of move: local moves inside the same metro, long-distance and interstate, full-service packing on top of the move itself, and specialty handling for large or delicate items.

Office and commercial relocations get their own page as well. An office manager is buying on downtime and liability, which is a different pitch from the one that wins a family's kitchen table.

People Moving In Are Demand Nobody Owns Yet

Some trades spike after a storm or a hard freeze. Moving companies run on something quieter: households changing address. Douglasville is the Douglas County seat, and Villa Rica sits mostly in Carroll County, and both see households arrive and leave all year. Every one of those moves needed a truck.

Some of that is local, one address to another inside the same town. The rest arrives from somewhere else, and that inbound work is the piece a mover with no local reputation yet can still win, because the person searching doesn't know anybody's name either. They type movers near me against an address they haven't moved into yet, so the listing that looks complete gets the call.

Peak Season and the Rest of the Year

Residential moves pile into a few months around school calendars and lease turnover, then drop off hard, so a job booked in June says something different about the year than the same job in January.

SeasonWhat happens
Peak moving monthsCall volume climbs hard, crews run at full capacity, and quotes get compared quickly since competing movers are all busy at once.
The rest of the yearCall volume drops, and the moves that do come in are often less price-sensitive and more willing to book further ahead.

Which stretch to push visibility into comes down to your own capacity. Crews booked solid in June get nothing from more June calls. The same visibility in February fills a calendar that was going to sit empty, which is where the money in this trade usually is, so we time the work to the months your crews are still open.

Frequently asked questions

Does a moving broker need a different category than an actual moving company?

Yes, and getting it wrong shows up as complaints. A broker that arranges moves through other carriers rather than running its own trucks shouldn't list itself as Mover, since that category implies the business performs the move, and the mismatch generates complaints from customers expecting the company on the profile to be the one on the truck.

Why is review growth different for a moving company than for a recurring service business?

Every review comes from a job you just did. Almost no household needs the same mover again within a year or two, so the count only grows by asking new customers consistently.

Should office and commercial moves get their own page, separate from residential?

Yes, since the buyer and the timeline are both different. A business relocating an office plans around a weekend to avoid downtime and cares about equipment handling and minimal disruption, concerns a residential moving page rarely addresses well.

Should a mover build pages for the towns it moves people to, or only the ones it works out of?

Start with the towns you dispatch from, then add the ones you regularly run trucks into. A page for a metro you've never sent a crew to ranks for a quote you would have to hand off, and that costs you the review as well as the job.
