Real Estate Agent SEO in Georgia Starts on the Seller Side

Buyers search inventory and the portals own it. Sellers search for a person, and that search is still open.

Two Real Estate Searches, and One of Them Is Winnable

A buyer types homes for sale with a town after it, and the page that comes back belongs to Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, and Trulia, with a brokerage feed or two mixed in. An individual agent's listing pages carry the same MLS records with far less authority behind them, so that lane is a hard place to spend a marketing budget.

A seller types something else entirely. What is my house worth, realtors in Rome GA, who sells land in Polk County, how do I sell a house that needs work. Those searches are looking for a person, and what comes back for them is thin enough that one agent can hold the ground.

The third search is the agent's own name. A referral gets handed a name at a cookout and looks it up before dialing, and whatever comes back decides whether the call happens at all. Between the seller lane and that name check sits most of the work for most agents.

Your Real Estate Agent Listing and the Brokerage Office Listing

Google allows a licensed professional who is the public face of their own practice to hold a listing, and it allows the brokerage one for the office. Two listings at the same suite number, with the same name pattern and the same category, collapse into each other, and the agent's is the one that loses.

So the split gets made on purpose. The agent's listing carries the agent's name as it reads on the license, the Real estate agent category, and a phone number that rings the agent's cell. The office listing keeps Real estate agency and the brokerage name. An agent who files Real estate agency for themselves at the brokerage's address has created a duplicate and usually gets treated like one.

Hours a Real Estate Agent Actually Works

Showings happen Saturday morning and Sunday afternoon. A profile that reads closed on the weekend is dark through the busiest hours of the week for the exact search it wants, and hours are among the fastest things on a listing to correct.

Realtors Lose the Website When They Change Brokerages

The site a brokerage hands out lives on the brokerage's domain. Everything it earns belongs to that domain, and the next move to a new office starts the climb over. So the first question on a real estate account is who holds the domain name, and the answer needs to be the agent.

What sits on it matters just as much. An IDX wrapper generates identical pages on thousands of agent sites out of the same feed, which gives Google nothing to prefer. The pages that earn are the ones a feed can't produce: what a seller does in the ninety days before a listing goes live, what a Georgia closing takes out of the seller's proceeds, how the appraisal and the inspection actually go, the subdivisions and school zones the agent sells in by name, and the acreage and farm side of the market where the national portals are weakest.

The name has to match everywhere too. The license record at the Georgia Real Estate Commission, the profile, the site, and the MLS should all read as one person, since a middle initial that appears in three places out of four is enough to make an agent look like two.

Real Estate Reviews Go to Zillow While the Map Pack Reads Google

At the closing table, the review request an agent already has usually points at Zillow, because that platform pushes hard for it and the badge shows up where agents get graded. Those reviews build a Zillow page. The three listings above the search results read Google, and nothing crosses between them.

So the ask names Google out loud, and it happens while the keys are still in somebody's hand. An agent closes a handful of deals a year, which makes every one of them a review earned twice, once by doing the work and once by asking for it. A review that names the town and says whether it was a purchase or a sale tells the next seller more than a bare five stars can.

Season is the other half of it. Listings turn over in spring, so the visibility work belongs in December and January, and a free audit in the fall leaves enough runway for it to matter. The first quarter of an agent account reads like this. The agent listing gets split off from the office listing, the category and the weekend hours get set, and somebody finally settles who holds the domain name, which tends to be the most uncomfortable question of the engagement. Once that's answered the seller pages get written and the name gets squared across the license record, the MLS, and the profile. By the end of it there's a town by town read on where the seller terms actually stand.

The year after that has one shape. Seller research runs from late December through February, the listing appointments happen in March and April, and the closings that pay for all of it land in May and June. Which means the pages producing an April listing appointment had to be published in the fall. The results page stays public, which is the same reason a good agent puts sold prices on a page.

Frequently asked questions

Can an agent have a Google Business Profile while working out of the brokerage office?

Yes. Google allows a listing for an individual practitioner who is the public face of their own practice. Use the agent name from the license, the Real estate agent category, and a phone number that rings the agent, and leave the office listing to the brokerage.

Should a real estate agent try to rank for homes for sale searches?

Rarely worth the budget. The portals own inventory searches. The winnable ground is seller intent, the agent name, and the corners the portals handle badly, which in north Georgia usually means land, acreage, and farms.

Do Zillow reviews help an agent rank on Google?

They build a Zillow profile and stop there. The map listing counts reviews left on Google, so the closing table ask has to say Google specifically or the count on the listing stays flat.

Real Estate Sellers Start Searching in January

An agent who wants seller calls in April is competing in January, because that's when the searches producing those calls get decided. The gap between the two months is the whole opportunity.
