What Actually Decides a Map Pack Seat Here

Gainesville is known well past its own city limits for its poultry industry, a long-standing part of the local economy. A map pack seat for an ordinary local service search turns on something narrower than that. Category match, review count, and how recently those reviews landed decide the seat, the same three signals in a city of 47,700 as in a city a fifth that size.

A median of 11, with the full range topping out at 20, is a catchable number for a business asking every customer for a review over a few steady months. It describes a market where the leading business on each of these searches has yet to make review requests part of the regular routine.

Why a Review from Last Month Counts More Than One from Three Years Ago

Raw review count is only part of what decides a seat. Google also weighs how recently those reviews landed, alongside whether the business's category and service area match what someone typed. A business sitting on 20 reviews collected years ago, with nothing recent behind them, can lose a seat to a competitor whose smaller pile arrived steadily this year. That's worth holding onto before you read the 9-to-20 range above as a settled order.

If your Gainesville business already holds a handful of reviews from past customers, the fastest lever is usually that base rather than a fresh start. Go back to it, ask for a new round, then hold the pace past the point where the number starts looking respectable. A profile that collects two or three reviews a month for six months reads very differently to Google than one that collected ten reviews once and went quiet.
