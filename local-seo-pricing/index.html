What Local SEO Work Actually Includes

Someone standing in a parking lot who searches for your trade and their town is not going to scroll down to read a blog post: they tap one of the handful of results sitting at the very top of the page, call the number printed on it, and never load a single website in the process. That behavior, not a ranking theory, is why local seo pricing gets asked as its own question separate from a general seo cost search, and why the work behind it is scoped around the Google Business Profile first. The category filed on that profile, the service area it declares, and how recently it has collected reviews compared to the couple of businesses sitting beside it are what decide whether a searcher sees your name or somebody else's.

Underneath the profile sits citation work, keeping your business name, address, and phone number consistent across the directories Google checks when deciding how much to trust a listing, and city or service area pages built for each town you actually work in rather than one page trying to cover all of them at once. Drop any one of those pieces and the whole effort tends to plateau even when the rest looks solid.

The map pack runs on a different set of signals than the organic results below it, so the labor behind it looks different too: less broad content production, more profile management, citation cleanup, and a review system that keeps running month after month rather than a single burst of activity at the start.

Why Cheap Local SEO Packages Underdeliver

A flat monthly fee well under the bottom of that range is not automatically a bargain, and understanding why comes down to arithmetic rather than opinion. Writing a real page for one city takes research and time, and a business serving eight or ten towns needs eight or ten of those pages, not one template with the city name swapped out. Asking recent customers for reviews on a consistent schedule takes ongoing attention, not a one time email blast. Cleaning up citation data across dozens of directories by hand takes real hours.

At a price that cannot cover that labor, the work tends to lean on shortcuts: a single generic page reused across every town, automated citation submissions with no cleanup behind them, or no review system at all beyond whatever was already happening before the contract started. In our experience, the map pack position is usually the first place that shows the gap, since it runs on exactly the signals a shortcut skips.

One example from our own client work: a junk removal company we manage in Northwest Georgia sits at the very top of its map pack for the searches that name both the service and the city, evidence that the profile, citation, and review mechanics above are not theoretical.

Questions Worth Asking Before You Sign a Local SEO Contract

Because this is a narrower job than a full organic campaign, it is also easier to check whether a proposal actually covers it. A few questions tend to separate real local SEO work from a package borrowing the name:

  • How many city or service area pages are actually included? A single generic page covering "our service areas" is not the same deliverable as a written page per town.
  • What category will they set on the Google Business Profile, and why? A vague answer here usually means nobody has looked at your specific trade yet.
  • How often do review requests actually go out? A one time setup is not a review system.
  • How will citation cleanup be verified, not just claimed? Ask to see a before and after for a past client, not just a promise that it happened.

A proposal that answers all four in specifics is doing the work described earlier on this page. One that answers in generalities is worth pricing against the low end of the range above, not the middle.

What is actually included in a local SEO engagement at that price?

Google Business Profile management, citation building and cleanup across the directories Google checks against your listing, an ongoing routine for prompting fresh reviews, and location pages written for each town you actually serve. A report can list all of that as delivered work even in a month where the map pack position never actually moves.
