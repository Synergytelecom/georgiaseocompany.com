Snellville's Split, and a Page Full of Directories

Snellville, a Gwinnett County city of roughly 22,300 people, returned a full map pack on all six terms we measured in the July 2026 capture. The reviews behind the leading business split unevenly, four terms at one figure and two at another.

SEO company, SEO services, Local SEO, and digital marketing agency all returned a leader at 23. Web design company and web designer both sat higher, at 47. Notably, digital marketing agency grouped with the lower figure here, not the higher one, a different pattern than the same term showed in several other towns in this measurement.

What a 28 Percent Directory Share Means

Just over a quarter of the organic listings across Snellville's six searches, 28.3 percent, were directory and aggregator sites, not businesses with a presence in Snellville, one of the higher shares in this fourteen-city band. A results page that crowded with directories leaves less organic room for any one local business, on top of whatever is happening in the map pack itself.

None of that changes what the pack numbers themselves say. A 23-review leader is a reachable target, and the 47-review side, while a longer climb, is still a fraction of the toughest numbers measured elsewhere in this same band.

Snellville Breaks the Pattern Found Almost Everywhere Else in This Band

In several other towns in this fourteen-city measurement, digital marketing agency turned up the highest or one of the highest review counts on the page. In Snellville it did not. It grouped with the lower-numbered side of the split, alongside SEO company, SEO services, and Local SEO, while web design company and web designer held the higher figure instead.

We do not treat that as a rule this measurement can explain with certainty. Which specific local businesses happened to claim and build out a given category first varies town to town, and Snellville's version of that history produced a different grouping than several neighboring towns did. What matters practically is not the explanation. It is that a Snellville business should read its own city's table above rather than assume a pattern seen elsewhere in this band applies here too.

It is also the clearest argument on this entire page for measuring fourteen towns separately instead of publishing one regional average.

Fitting This to a Snellville Business

Half a job does not work in Snellville. A trade or clinic with a modest review count is closer to the 23 than it might assume, and a design-focused business should be planning for the steeper 47, but the pack seat is only half of what either one is playing for. A strong profile paired with a thin website still gives ground on a results page where more than a quarter of the organic listings belong to directories rather than local businesses. A strong website paired with a thin profile misses the pack seat sitting directly above them. The two fronts move together or they do not move.
