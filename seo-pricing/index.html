How Much Does SEO Cost Per Month?

SEO for a Georgia business runs $300 to $10,000+ a month. Where you land inside that span comes down to how many towns and keywords are in scope, what shape the site is in already, and whether the job is earning a position or defending one. There's no single search engine optimization price that covers a state this varied, which is why the honest answer starts as a band and narrows once somebody looks at your market.

Here's what the monthly fee buys. Every month we look at what's being typed in your market, we review other markets and other websites for new keyword trends, and we optimize your Google Business Profile and your site for the terms that matter where you work. You get a report each month on where your rankings stand and what moved, and you can check it against your own Search Console.

Client rankings sit on a public dashboard that our own clients read. If it's the site itself you're pricing rather than the search work behind it, our website cost breakdown covers that.

Do You Sell SEO Packages?

Not as a fixed price list, and it's worth saying why. A tiered package is a labor budget with a label on it, and the label carries from one business to the next while the work underneath changes completely. Two businesses paying the same monthly figure can need a different page count, a different review pace, and a different amount of citation repair, because they compete in different towns against different businesses.

So the quote gets built against your market instead. Before a number exists we read the towns you sell in against whoever ranks in them today, check what condition the site and the listing you already own are in, and count how many markets need a position of their own. The number lands inside one of those bands, with the month's work written out beside it, and you can hold it against any SEO services package you were quoted somewhere else. Publishing tiers is a fair way to sell this and plenty of good work gets sold that way. A tier has to be priced for the average of every market it might be sold into, and yours is the only one you're paying for.

What SEO Packages Priced Well Under the Range Buy

Flat-fee offers that sit far under the bottom of those bands are easy to find, and it's worth knowing what the money buys before you answer one. Monthly labor costs what it costs: research, writing, technical fixes. A price that far under the market has to save that money somewhere, and usually it saves it on automated listing blasts and copy nobody wrote for your business specifically. That work fills a monthly report while the ranking sits still.

Here's what a bottom-of-range package typically leaves out:

  • A written page per town. One "service areas" page stands in for eight or ten real ones.
  • Citation cleanup by hand. Automated submissions go out, and the mismatched records nobody corrected stay mismatched.
  • A review routine that keeps running. One setup wave, then nothing.
  • Rank checks pulled per town. A single national position stands in for the town-by-town picture.

The other tell is a promised outcome attached to the low price. No vendor controls how Google ranks anything, so a low fee paired with a confident promise about where you'll land deserves the most scrutiny in the stack, and the promise is the part to ask about first.

How to Read a Quote Before You Sign Anything

The number at the top of a proposal tells you less than the specifics underneath it. Before you set two prices side by side, ask which pages or keywords get worked this month, who does the writing, and how results get measured against where you started. A vendor answering in generalities is describing activity, and a vendor answering in specifics is describing a plan.

  • Ask which pages, and which term each one targets. A quote naming URLs and the term behind each is describing scoped work. A quote that says "content included" is still guessing.
  • Ask for the rank history itself. A good agency can show you every position it holds this month.
  • Ask what you keep if you leave. Your Google Business Profile and your site access should transfer to you whether or not the engagement continues.
  • Ask whether the price accounts for your town. We look at what the competition actually looks like in a market like Dalton or Cedartown before scoping anything, because a national rate card treats every Georgia zip code like a metro.

Frequently asked questions

What will my own engagement cost?

It becomes a specific number after one conversation about your business. The three things that decide it are how many towns and keywords are in scope, what condition your current site and Google Business Profile are in, and how contested your trade already is where you sell. Most Georgia small business engagements land in the $750 to $3,000+ per month band.

Does the price change if my business already has a Google Business Profile set up?

Somewhat. A profile with some history, clean categories, and no suspension flags means less cleanup up front, but it rarely changes which range you land in, since the ongoing research, writing, and citation work costs the same either way. A messy profile adds work.

What happens to the price if I need SEO for more than one location?

Each additional location adds its own Google Business Profile, its own citation set, and usually its own page, so a multi-location engagement scales up from the single-location ranges. Two locations in neighboring towns often cost less to add than two locations far apart, since some of the research and content work overlaps.

Will the price go down once my rankings are established?

Not usually. What the budget buys shifts. Early months lean on research, citation cleanup, and new pages. Once a ranking holds, that same budget goes toward defending the spot and adding new keywords, because a competitor fills whatever gap you leave.

Should I choose a monthly retainer or a one-time project?

Pick the retainer if your trade or town is contested, since a ranking there needs new content and upkeep just to hold the spot it already won. Pick the one-time project if the job has an end: a technical cleanup, or a fresh batch of pages against a set deadline, priced at a project rate in the $500 to $7,500+ range.

What do SEO packages cost for a small business?

A small business buying month to month usually lands in the $750 to $3,000+ band, and a one-town map pack push sits lower, at $300 to $1,500+. Page count, how many towns get their own rank check, and whether a person writes the pages are what move you between those two. Ask about all three before you set two prices beside each other.
