Why There Is No Single Answer to 'What Does SEO Cost'

Search for seo pricing and you will find dozens of pages promising one clean number, and none of them can actually give you one, because the honest answer depends on facts specific to your business. A trade competing in a single small town needs a different scope than a specialty practice pulling customers from well outside its own zip code in a busier market. Ten target keywords cost less to research, build, and track than a hundred. A site with clean technical bones takes far less work to rank than one dragging years of unresolved problems behind it, and a business starting from zero visibility needs different work than one defending a spot it already holds.

What follows are published 2026 market ranges, gathered from broader industry pricing data rather than pulled from our own invoices, so you have real numbers to hold any proposal against. Where a given engagement actually lands inside those bands depends on someone sitting down with your market, your competitors, and the site you already have.

What a Rate Well Below the Range Actually Buys

Emails offering SEO for a flat fee that sits far under the bottom of the local band above are easy to find, and it is worth understanding what that money actually pays for before answering one. Skilled monthly labor, research, writing, and technical fixes, costs what it costs, and a price that undercuts the published range by that much has to come from somewhere. Usually it comes from automated listing blasts and copy nobody wrote specifically for your business, work that can look like activity on a monthly report without ever moving a real ranking.

The other tell is a promised outcome attached to that low price, since no vendor controls how Google ranks anything. In our experience, offers that pair an unusually low fee with a confident promise about where you will land are the ones worth the most scrutiny, not the least. A number that low is not a discovery of hidden efficiency; it is a sign the labor described above simply is not happening at the scale a real ranking effort requires.

How to Read a Quote Before You Sign Anything

The number at the top of a proposal tells you less than the specifics underneath it. Before comparing prices, ask what pages or keywords are actually being worked on this month, who is doing the writing, and how results get measured against where you started. A vendor who cannot answer in specifics is describing activity, not a plan.

  • Ask for the actual pages, not a page count. A quote naming real URLs and the specific term each one targets is describing scoped work; a quote that just says "content included" is still guessing.
  • Ask to see it, not hear about it. A vendor confident in its own numbers will show a rank history rather than describe one. We keep ours public, on our results page, for exactly that reason.
  • Ask what you keep if you leave. Your Google Business Profile and site access should transfer to you whether or not the engagement continues.
  • Ask whether the price accounts for your actual town. We look at the real competition in a market like Dalton or Cedartown before scoping anything, rather than pricing off a national assumption that treats every Georgia zip code like it is fighting the same crowd as a big metro market.

Frequently asked questions

Are these your prices?

No. The ranges above are published 2026 market figures gathered from broader industry pricing data, not a menu of what we charge. What your own engagement costs depends on your market, the condition of your current site, and how many keywords or towns are in scope, and that only becomes a real number after a conversation about your specific business.

Does the price change if my business already has a Google Business Profile set up?

Somewhat. An existing profile with real history, clean categories, and no suspension flags usually means less cleanup work up front, but it rarely moves which range you land in overall, since the ongoing research, writing, and citation work costs about the same either way. A profile that is a mess actually adds work, not the other way around.

What happens to the price if I need SEO for more than one location?

Each additional location adds its own Google Business Profile, its own citation set, and usually its own page, so a multi location engagement scales up from the single location ranges rather than sitting inside them. Two locations in neighboring towns often cost less to add than two locations far apart, since some of the research and content work overlaps.

Will the price go down once my rankings are established?

Not usually, though the work itself shifts. Early months lean heavily on research, citation cleanup, and new pages; once a ranking holds, that same budget tends to go toward defending the spot and expanding into new keywords rather than shrinking, since a competitor filling the gap you leave behind is the real risk of pulling back.

Should I choose a monthly retainer or a one time project?

Pick the retainer if your trade or town is genuinely contested, since a ranking there needs new content and upkeep just to hold the spot it already won. Pick the one time project if the job is bounded instead, a technical cleanup or a fresh batch of pages against a defined deadline, priced closer to the audit and cleanup range above.
