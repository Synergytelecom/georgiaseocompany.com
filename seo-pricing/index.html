Why There Is No Single Answer to 'What Does SEO Cost'

Search for seo pricing and you will find dozens of pages promising one clean number, and none of them can actually give you one, because the honest answer depends on facts specific to your business. A trade competing in a single small town needs a different scope than a specialty practice pulling from a fifteen mile radius in a busier market. Ten target keywords cost less to research, build, and track than a hundred. A site with clean technical bones costs less to rank than one carrying years of neglect, and a business starting from zero visibility needs different work than one defending a spot it already holds.

What follows are published 2026 market ranges, gathered from broader industry pricing data rather than pulled from our own invoices, so you have real numbers to hold any proposal against. Where a given engagement lands inside those bands only becomes clear once someone actually looks at your market, your competitors, and the site you already have.

What a Rate Well Below the Range Actually Buys

Emails offering SEO for a flat fee that sits far under the bottom of the local band above are easy to find, and it is worth understanding what that money actually pays for before answering one. Skilled monthly labor, research, writing, and technical fixes, costs what it costs, and a price that undercuts the published range by that much has to come from somewhere. Usually it comes from automated listing blasts and copy nobody wrote specifically for your business, work that can look like activity on a monthly report without ever moving a real ranking.

The other tell is a promised outcome attached to that low price, since no vendor controls how Google ranks anything. In our experience, offers that pair an unusually low fee with a confident promise about where you will land are the ones worth the most scrutiny, not the least.

How to Read a Quote Before You Sign Anything

The number at the top of a proposal tells you less than the specifics underneath it. Before comparing prices, ask what pages or keywords are actually being worked on this month, who is doing the writing, and how results get measured against where you started. A vendor who cannot answer in specifics is describing activity, not a plan.

  • Ask for a before and after. A real engagement can show rankings measured at the start next to rankings measured today, for a client in a similar spot.
  • Ask to see it, not hear about it. A vendor confident in its own numbers will show a rank history rather than describe one. We keep ours public, on our results page, for exactly that reason.
  • Ask who owns the accounts. Your Google Business Profile and site access should stay yours whether or not you keep paying.
  • Ask what happens if your market is small. A quote scoped for a crowded metro area does not fit a Georgia town of a few thousand people, and a fair proposal reflects that.

Frequently asked questions

Are these your prices?

No. The ranges above are published 2026 market figures gathered from broader industry pricing data, not a menu of what we charge. What your own engagement costs depends on your market, the condition of your current site, and how many keywords or towns are in scope, and that only becomes a real number after a conversation about your specific business.

What should a small business expect to pay for SEO each month?

A single market local push falls in the $300 to $1,500+ per month band under the 2026 figures, while a broader campaign reaching further into a small business keyword list moves up to $750 to $3,000+ per month. Markets with less competition, which describes most of Georgia outside metro Atlanta, tend to land toward the lower end of those bands.

Why do SEO quotes for what sounds like the same service vary so much?

Scope is the real variable hiding behind the sticker price. One proposal might cover a single town and a handful of keywords, another covers a whole state and hundreds of search terms, and the fair way to compare them is by what ships each month and how it gets measured, not the number at the top of the page.

Can I do my own SEO instead of paying for it?

The basics, yes, including plenty of the Google Business Profile groundwork that does not require a specialist. What usually breaks the plan is time: SEO is ongoing labor that compounds only when someone keeps doing it week after week, and most owners run out of hours long before they run out of ideas.

Should I choose a monthly retainer or a one time project?

Both are legitimate and fit different problems. A retainer suits ongoing competition, since a ranking needs new content and upkeep to hold its spot, while a project fits a bounded job, a technical cleanup or building a fresh batch of pages on a defined deadline, priced closer to the audit and cleanup range above.
