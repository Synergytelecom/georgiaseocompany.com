What SEO pricing looks like in Georgia right now

SEO pricing in Georgia comes down to three things: what the market charges, what a monthly engagement here covers, and how to hold one quote against another. We run this work for Georgia service and trade businesses from our office in Rome, and the ranges below are the market bands our own quotes land inside.

How Much Does SEO Cost Per Month?

SEO for a Georgia business runs $300 to $10,000+ a month. Where you land inside that span comes down to how many towns and keywords are in scope, what shape your website is in already, and whether the job is earning a position or defending one.

There's no single search engine optimization price that covers a state this varied, which is why the honest answer starts as a band and narrows once somebody looks at your market.

Here's what the monthly fee buys. Every month we look at what's being typed in your market, we review other markets and other websites for new keyword trends, and we optimize your Google Business Profile and your website for the terms that matter where you work. You get a report each month on where your rankings stand and what moved, and you can check it against your own Search Console. The terms are month to month, with no long-term contracts.

Client rankings sit on a public dashboard that our own clients read. If it's the website itself you're pricing rather than the search work behind it, our website cost breakdown covers that.

Do You Sell SEO Packages?

Not as a fixed price list, and it's worth saying why. A tiered package is a labor budget with a label on it, and the label carries from one business to the next while the work underneath changes completely.

Two businesses paying the same monthly figure can need a different webpage count, a different review pace, and a different amount of citation repair, because they compete in different towns against different businesses.

So the quote gets built against your market instead. Before a number exists we read the towns you sell in against whoever ranks in them today, check what condition the website and the listing you already own are in, and count how many markets need a position of their own.

The number lands inside one of those bands, with the month's work written out beside it.

Publishing tiers is a fair way to sell this and plenty of good work gets sold that way. A tier has to be priced for the average of every market it might be sold into, and yours is the only one you're paying for.

Most agencies charge for the audit and the cleanup together. Our SEO audit is free, so you only pay for the cleanup work that follows it.

What Does Local SEO Pricing Look Like for One Town?

Single-town map pack work runs $300 to $1,500+ a month. That's the local SEO pricing band most one-town Georgia businesses land in. The map pack is the block of three listings Google puts beside a map, above the regular results, and on a phone search pairing a trade with a town each card carries a phone number, so a business holding a spot gets calls that never load a website.

Local SEO is scoped around your Google Business Profile and the towns you work in, a narrower and usually less expensive job than an organic campaign spanning many keywords or towns. Here's what the money pays somebody to do each month:

  • The profile. We work its category and service area against what your towns search for.
  • Reviews. Every customer gets the ask after the job, once they've left or the crew has gone, by text or email with your profile's review link.
  • Citations. Your name, address and phone stay consistent across the directories Google checks.
  • Town webpages. Each town you serve gets a webpage with something true to say about that town.
  • The report. You see where you stand in the map pack, town by town, after we've checked your market for changes in keyword demand.

Chasing rankings across several towns, or competing in a crowded trade, puts a business higher in that band or past it into the campaign ranges above. Suspended profiles and stacks of duplicate listings are repair work before the monthly work starts.

What Does Google Business Profile Management Cost?

The listing itself costs nothing, since a Google Business Profile is free, so a management fee pays for labor. Google's help says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so no fee buys a position either.

We don't publish a profile-only price, and we don't print a market average for profile work, because we have no measured figure to stand behind. Ask anybody quoting you an average who measured it and when. Profile management can start alone, and the monthly SEO plan it can widen into adds work on your website. The nearest range we publish is the one-town band above, and it pays for more than profile work, including a webpage per service area, so don't read it as a profile price.

Town count, the listing's condition and the competition in your trade set the number for profile work. A profile with some history, clean categories and no suspension flags means less cleanup up front, but once the job widens into a monthly plan it rarely changes which band you land in, since the ongoing research, writing and citation work costs the same either way. Messy profiles add work. The monthly list is on our Google Business Profile management webpage.

What Is the Local SEO Cost for Contractors and Home Service Businesses?

The local SEO cost for contractors runs through the same published bands, and scope decides which one applies:

  • One town. $300 to $1,500+ per month for one profile, one town and a webpage per service area.
  • Two or three counties. $750 to $3,000+ per month for a handful of services across them.
  • A contested trade or ten towns. $3,000 to $10,000+ per month when each town needs its own webpage.

We don't publish prices by trade, because we have no vetted per-trade figures to print. Your trade shows up in the quote through how contested it is in your towns, and a home service business with a long service area needs more webpages and more rank checks than one that stays close to the shop.

The useful comparison is one closed job: hold what it's worth against the monthly fee and count the extra calls it takes to cover that fee. A large ticket needs very few of them, and a small ticket needs a lot more. The work itself is on our contractor SEO webpage, with the other trade groups on home service SEO.

What Do SEO Packages Priced Well Under the Range Buy?

Flat-fee offers that sit far under the bottom of those bands are easy to find, and it's worth knowing what the money buys before you answer one. Monthly labor costs what it costs: research, writing, technical fixes.

A price that far under the market has to save that money somewhere, and usually it saves it on automated listing blasts and copy nobody wrote for your business specifically. That work fills a monthly report while the ranking sits still. At a price well under $300 a month, the budget can't cover a person doing the four jobs below, so a package at that price typically leaves them out:

  • A written webpage per town. One "service areas" webpage stands in for eight or ten real ones.
  • Citation cleanup by hand. Automated submissions go out, and the mismatched records nobody corrected stay mismatched.
  • A review routine that keeps running. One setup wave, then nothing.
  • Rank checks pulled per town. A single national position stands in for the town-by-town picture.

The other tell is a promised outcome attached to the low price. No vendor controls how Google ranks anything, so a low fee paired with a confident promise about where you'll land deserves the most scrutiny in the stack, and the promise is the part to ask about first.

How to Read a Quote Before You Sign Anything

The number at the top of a proposal tells you less than the specifics underneath it, so ask these before you set two prices side by side. A vendor answering in generalities is describing activity, and a vendor answering in specifics is describing a plan.

  • Ask which webpages, and which term each one targets. A quote naming URLs and the term behind each is describing scoped work. A quote that says "content included" is still guessing, and one "our service areas" webpage is a different deliverable from a written webpage per town.
  • Ask who does the writing. A person writing each webpage and a template with a town name swapped in are different jobs at different prices.
  • Ask for the rank history itself. Good agencies can show you every position they hold this month, measured against where each client started.
  • Ask what you keep if you leave. Your Google Business Profile and your website access should transfer to you whether or not the engagement continues.
  • Ask whether the price accounts for your town. We look at what the competition actually looks like in a market like Dalton or Cedartown before scoping anything, because a national rate card treats every Georgia zip code like a metro.
  • Ask which category goes on the Google Business Profile, and why that one. Vague answers here usually mean nobody has looked at your trade yet.
  • Ask how review requests go out. The answer to hear is the every-customer routine in the monthly list above, because a one-time setup sends one wave and stops.
  • Ask how citation cleanup gets verified. Before and after records from a past listing show it happened.

Frequently asked questions

What will my own engagement cost?

It becomes a specific number after one conversation about your business. The three things that decide it are how many towns and keywords are in scope, what condition your current website and Google Business Profile are in, and how contested your trade already is where you sell. Most Georgia small business engagements land in the $750 to $3,000+ per month band.

What do SEO packages cost for a small business?

A small business buying month to month usually lands in the $750 to $3,000+ band, and a one-town map pack push sits lower, at $300 to $1,500+. Webpage count, how many towns get their own rank check, and whether a person writes the webpages are what move you between those two.

What happens to the price if I need SEO for more than one location?

Each additional location adds its own Google Business Profile, its own citation set, and usually its own webpage, so a multi-location engagement scales up from the single-location ranges. Two locations in neighboring towns often cost less to add than two locations far apart, since some of the research and content work overlaps.

Will the price go down once my rankings are established?

Not usually. What the budget buys shifts. Early months lean on research, citation cleanup, and new webpages. Once a ranking holds, that same budget goes toward defending the spot and adding new keywords, because a competitor fills whatever gap you leave.

Should I choose a monthly retainer or a one-time project?

Pick the retainer if your trade or town is contested, since a ranking there needs new content and upkeep just to hold the spot it already won. Pick the one-time project if the job has an end: a technical cleanup, or a fresh batch of webpages against a set deadline, priced at a project rate in the $500 to $7,500+ range.
