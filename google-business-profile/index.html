What Is a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile is the record Google keeps about a local business: its name, its category, where it is or which areas it serves, its hours, phone number and website, plus photos and customer reviews. Google describes it as a way to manage how your business shows up on Maps and Search at no charge.

Plenty of owners find a listing they never built. Google updates profiles from sources such as user reports and licensed content, so a business can have a public listing, reviews and all, before anybody who works there has claimed it. Claiming it and passing Google's verification is what gives you ownership of what it says.

Not every business qualifies. Google limits profiles to businesses that make in-person contact with customers during their stated hours, either at a location customers visit or by traveling to the customer. Its policy overview rules out online-only businesses, lead generation companies and properties listed for rent or sale.

Older articles call it Google My Business, or GMB. It's the same listing under its current name.

Where a Google Business Profile Shows Up

One profile feeds three places on Google, and each one is a chance to get the call.

  • The map results on a local search. Search a service and a town, and a short list of businesses with a map beside it usually sits near the top of the results. Every entry in that list is a Business Profile.
  • Google Maps. The same listings, found by somebody searching inside the Maps app or on the Maps website.
  • The panel beside a name search. Search a business by name and its profile fills a box with the hours, the star rating, the reviews, and buttons to call, visit the website or get directions.

A customer can call you, get directions or read your newest review straight from the listing, without ever opening your website. Google counts those actions. A verified profile's performance report shows how many people viewed the listing, the search terms that surfaced it, and how many called, asked for directions or clicked through to the website.

Using a Google Business Profile for Local SEO

Google's own help names three things behind local results. Relevance is how well a profile matches the search. Distance is how far the business sits from the person searching. Prominence is how well known the business is, which Google says draws on things like how many reviews it has and how many websites link to it. The same help says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

That splits the work three ways. Relevance comes from the profile itself: the primary category, the services and every field filled in completely and accurately. Distance is fixed by where the business is and where the customer stands, which is why a listing that leads in its home town can drop out of the map two towns over. Prominence builds over time, through the reviews customers leave and the other websites that link to yours.

The website matters for a second reason. The map results come from profiles, and the ordinary results underneath come from websites, so a business needs both to show up in both. The profile's website link decides where a tap lands, and the name, address and phone on the website should match the profile exactly, so the details customers and Google find on each one agree.

The Rules Google Holds Every Profile To

Google publishes its rules in the guidelines for representing your business, and a profile that breaks them can be suspended. Five of them apply to every local service listing:

  • The name is the real-world name. It's the name on your signage, website and paperwork, with no services, towns or slogans added. Google says unnecessary information in the name can get a profile suspended.
  • The address is a real place of business. P.O. boxes, mailbox rentals and virtual offices don't qualify, and a business that serves customers only at their locations hides its address and lists its service areas.
  • There's one profile per business. A second listing for the same business counts as a duplicate, and Google keeps duplicates out of Search and Maps.
  • Prices and promotions stay out of the listing's own fields. Google's content policy bars time-limited promotions, prices and direct calls to action there. Offers and updates go in posts.
  • Reviews come from real customers, unpaid and unsteered. That means no incentives, no asking only the customers you expect to be happy, no requesting particular wording, and no pressing anybody to write one on the spot.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Google Business Profile the same thing as Google My Business?

Yes. Google My Business was the earlier name for the same free listing, so a guide that says GMB is describing the profile you manage today, even when its screenshots show older screens.

Does Google charge for a Business Profile?

No. Google lets you add or claim a profile and manage it at no charge. Google Ads is a separate, paid product, and Google's help says there's no way to pay for a better local ranking.

Can I have a profile if customers never come to my location?

Yes. A business that travels to its customers sets up a service-area profile, hides its address and lists the areas it serves. Google's guidelines say that area shouldn't reach farther than about two hours of driving from where the business is based, though a larger one can suit some businesses.

Who should own the Google account that holds the profile?

The business owner. Give employees or an agency manager access. Managers can edit the listing and reply to reviews, but they can't add or remove users or delete the profile, so the ownership never leaves your hands.
