Buford Runs the Pattern Backward

Buford, home to the Mall of Georgia and straddling the Gwinnett-Hall county line, has roughly 18,600 people. Our July 2026 measurement found one of the three highest review bars in this fourteen-city band here: three of the six search terms share an identical 62-review leader. What makes Buford's version of that high bar unusual is which searches hold it.

SEO company, SEO services, and Local SEO, three separate terms, all returned the exact same leader review count of 62. Web design company and web designer both came in lower, at 48. Digital marketing agency sat lowest of all, at 29.

A High Bar, Held by the Terms You Would Expect to Be Easy

In several other towns in this same measurement, the plainer SEO-labeled searches turned up the softest numbers and the design-labeled searches turned up the toughest. Buford flips that. Here, the businesses ranking for SEO company, SEO services, and Local SEO have built the deepest review history in town, while the web design and digital marketing searches, usually the harder climb elsewhere, are the more open lane here.

Useful, and specific to Buford, not a general rule about how these searches behave. It also means a single citywide figure cannot describe Buford accurately. The 62 and the 29 sit on two different starting lines, not one market.

We Do Not Know Why Buford Runs Backward, Only That It Does

It would be easy to reach for a tidy explanation here, something about Buford's retail-heavy footprint, or about which local businesses happened to claim which category first. We are not going to offer one with more confidence than the data supports. What this single measurement can say is that the pattern holds across three separate terms on each side, not a fluke produced by one unusual search.

What it cannot say is why, and pretending otherwise would be the kind of invented explanation this page is built specifically to avoid. The useful takeaway is simpler than a theory. Check which side of Buford's split matches your own positioning before assuming either the harder or the easier number applies to you, rather than borrowing an assumption from how these searches usually behave in the rest of this band.
