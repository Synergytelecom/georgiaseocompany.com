Gwinnett County SEO on a Two-County Address

An address sitting on the Gwinnett County line creates a data problem before it creates a ranking problem. Directories copy whichever county and postal city they find first, so one aggregator files you in Gwinnett, the next in Hall, and a third invents a postal city you have never used. Google compares those records against each other, and disagreement reads as uncertainty about a basic fact.

Squaring them takes patience rather than cleverness. One spelling of the street, one suite format, one postal city, one phone number, repeated across every listing that carries your name. It is dull work, and it moves positions more reliably than anything that sounds more sophisticated.

The service area does the other half. Sugar Hill and Suwanee sit close enough that a Buford business genuinely competes there, and Flowery Branch is a short run up the lake. Listing the towns your calendar actually reaches makes you eligible for those searches. Listing half the metro makes you convincing in none of them, because a service area that claims everything tells Google nothing it can act on.

One more thing about a line like this: the county in your own copy should match the county on your profile. A page that keeps saying Hall while the profile says Gwinnett hands a reader and a crawler two answers to the same question, and neither answer gets the benefit. Pick the one that matches where you are registered and where your customers think you are, then use it everywhere without exception.
