The Spelling Problem in LaFayette Listings

The town takes a capital F in the middle, and almost nobody outside Walker County types it that way. Google has several Lafayettes to sort between, and the directories feeding it record this one three or four different ways, sometimes inside the same company's own records.

That turns into a real cost, because your business name, your street address and your phone number have to read the same in every place Google checks them. A citation reading Lafayette, another reading LaFayette, and a third that abbreviated your street differently are three weaker versions of you, and Google splits the credit between them. Sorting that out is unglamorous work that moves rankings more often than any clever idea does.

Start with your own paperwork. Insurance certificates, invoices and the state registration often disagree on the spelling and the street abbreviation, and Google reads every one of them through the directories that copy them.

The cleanup then runs in a set order, beginning with the record tied to your verification, since that is the one Google leans on hardest. That version becomes the master. The invoices, the certificates, the registration and the third party listings get moved onto it one at a time after that. Working the other direction, editing whichever listing annoys you most first, usually produces a fourth version of your business instead of one.

The same rule covers the towns you cover. Rossville and Chickamauga are Walker County too, and a customer up there is running a different search than somebody standing on the LaFayette square.
