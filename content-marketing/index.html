What we mean by content marketing

We look up what people in your market are typing into Google, then build the pages that answer it: the services you sell, the towns you sell them in, and the questions customers ask before they call. Pages get written for the person searching first and structured so Google can read them second. The starting point is usually a site with thin or missing service pages, or a blog that's been publishing for years and brings in no search traffic.

Here's the work we turn down. Content built for social sharing or brand awareness, with no search demand behind it, is a different discipline and we don't take it on. We'll also tell you when a term you want has no measurable demand, because a page aimed at a phrase nobody types produces nothing no matter how well it's written.

Most content programs run the other way around: pick a topic that sounds relevant, publish it on schedule, hope traffic follows. Most of it never ranks, because nobody checked whether anyone searches it. We check first, using the same keyword data behind our own SEO work, and a short list of pages that each rank for something beats a long list that ranks for nothing.

What gets built

The scope is set by what your market searches for:

  • Keyword research. Terms relevant to your business, checked for monthly search volume before a word gets written.
  • Service and location pages. What you do, and where you do it, in the words customers use for both. These come first for most Georgia service businesses.
  • Supporting articles. The questions customers ask before they call, one page per question, each tied to a term with demand behind it.
  • On-page structure. Titles, headings, and internal links that tell a search engine what each page covers and how the pages connect.
  • Content audit. What you've already published, and whether any of it targets something people search.

Before a page gets written

Nothing gets written until a term clears two checks. The first is whether people in your market search it at all, and at what volume, read off keyword data rather than guessed at. The second is what already ranks for it, because the pages currently holding that search show what a new one would be up against, and sometimes show that a page you already have needs an update instead of a replacement.

Then the outline gets built around the questions a searcher has, in the order they'd ask them. A page aimed at one clear question beats a longer page covering four, since the long one answers all of them partway.

This is also how a topic gets turned down before anything is written. The cheapest page to skip is the one nobody would have found.

How a town page earns its place

A page for each town you serve only works when each one is about that town. The same paragraph with the name swapped out is what Google's spam policies call a doorway page, and a large order of location pages is the easiest way to build a stack of them by accident.

So every town page carries what's true there: the county it sits in, the work you get called out for in that market, the drive it takes to get there, the neighborhoods and corridors a customer would recognize. When there's nothing true to say about a town, that's usually a sign you don't sell there yet, and the page waits until you do.

What a month of content work covers

We check that demand monthly, because what people search shifts with the season and with whatever your competitors just published. A term that carried your spring can go quiet by September, and the page built for it goes quiet with it. Catching that in the month it happens keeps it a small correction.

Every month we report which pages are ranking and which ones need another pass. It comes page by page, tied to the term each one was built for, so a page that isn't earning its place shows up as a line you can point at rather than a number in a total. Then the next month's writing gets aimed at what that report shows.

Frequently asked questions

How do you pick what content to write?

We start from keyword research, checking what your market searches for, and prioritize topics with measured demand rather than topics that only sound relevant.

Do you write blog posts or just service pages?

Both, depending on what the keyword research shows. Some businesses need service and location pages built out first; others already have those and need supporting articles targeting specific customer questions.

How often should new content get published?

Consistency matters more than volume. A steady, modest publishing schedule targeted at researched search terms outperforms an inconsistent flood of posts with no keyword behind them.

What if a keyword we want to target has no search volume?

We say so before writing anything. Content aimed at a term nobody searches will not produce traffic no matter how well it is written, and we would rather redirect that effort toward a term with measurable demand.

Does content marketing include social media posting?

No. Our content work is built around search demand and published to your website. Social distribution of that content is something a client can handle separately or with a dedicated social vendor.

Two years of blog posts and no search traffic?

The useful first answer here isn't a content calendar. It's what people in your market type into Google, and whether the pages you already have cover any of it. Send us the site and the services you want more of, and we'll tell you what's missing.
