What Technical SEO Services Actually Fix

Technical SEO is the work of making a site legible to a search engine: what gets crawled, what stays indexed, how quickly a page renders on a phone, and what the markup claims about the business. It puts no new words on a page. It clears the road for the words already sitting there.

A five page site built last year on a current platform usually has almost nothing wrong at this layer, and a monthly technical retainer on it would buy you less than the same money spent on pages or reviews. This work earns its keep on sites carrying size or history: a few hundred addresses, a platform move somewhere in the past, a rebuild that left half its old paths pointing nowhere.

If you want to know which case you're in before spending anything, that's the free SEO audit. If you'd sooner work down a checklist yourself, the free version lives on how to rank higher on Google.

The Technical SEO Checks That Run Every Month

Six things come back around every month, and the list barely changes because the failures barely change:

  • Index coverage. Which pages Google holds, which ones it looked at and passed on, and which ones it hasn't gone looking for yet.
  • Core Web Vitals. Largest Contentful Paint under two and a half seconds, Interaction to Next Paint under two hundred milliseconds, Cumulative Layout Shift under a tenth, read on phone hardware because that's where your customers are.
  • Redirects and status codes. Chains collapsed to one hop, temporary redirects made permanent where permanent was the intent, and dead links found before a customer finds them.
  • Canonicals and duplicates. One address per page, with the insecure, the www, the trailing slash and the tracking parameter versions all pointing home.
  • Structured data. The markup that tells Google what kind of thing a page describes, checked against what Google currently accepts.
  • Internal links. Pages nothing points at, and pages your strongest ones ought to be pointing at.

The reason it repeats is that the failures come back. A platform update rewrites how addresses get generated. A plugin adds a script that pushes the load time past where it was last spring. Somebody publishes a page and the theme drops a second H1 into the header. None of that announces itself, and by the time it shows up as a ranking change it has usually been sitting there for a month.

How Technical SEO Feeds the Monthly Loop and the Map Pack

The month runs in a fixed order. Read what the market has started searching for, move the site and the listing to meet it, then write down what changed. The technical layer is where a shift gets absorbed cheaply. A page aimed at a term that just picked up does nothing at all while it sits outside the index, so coverage gets read before anybody writes a word.

The map pack is a separate contest. Those three cards come off your Google Business Profile, so a title tag rewrite leaves them where they were. Two technical things still land there. The page your map card points at is what Google reads to confirm the business matches the listing, and a landing page that takes six seconds on one bar of signal out in Gordon County loses the caller the card just earned. Position in the cards themselves is local SEO work.

What a Technical SEO Audit Reads

Most of the answer sits in Google Search Console, which is free and already yours. Its page indexing report sorts every address on your site into states, and two of those states account for most of the missing pages we turn up.

Crawled and Passed Over

Google fetched the page, read it, and left it out. That's usually a page saying close to nothing, or saying the same thing as another page you already have. The repair happens on the page, which makes it on page SEO work more often than server work.

Discovered and Never Fetched

Google knows the address exists and hasn't spent the crawl on it. On a small site that points straight at internal linking, since a page nothing links to gets found last. On a large one it points at crawl budget draining into something useless, like a product filter minting a fresh address for every combination a visitor clicks.

Markup That Says What the Business Is

Structured data is the other half of what an audit reads, and most sites carry either none of it or a decorative pile of it. The useful pieces are short. A LocalBusiness block naming the business, the address and the phone. A Service block on each service page. A BreadcrumbList so the trail Google prints under your result matches the trail on the page. An FAQPage block where the page really does answer questions. Markup describing things the visitor can't find on the page is the kind that earns a manual action, so what goes in has to match what's visible.

Then come the ones nobody expects. A noindex tag that shipped from staging into production and quietly emptied a whole section. A robots file still disallowing a folder some build script wrote two years ago. Each of those is a one line correction sitting behind a month of wondering.

Who Should Buy Technical SEO and Who Should Wait

Four situations make this the first check rather than the third:

  • Traffic fell on a date you can name, and that date is when a new site went live.
  • The site carries a few hundred addresses or more, or runs filters that spin up new ones on their own.
  • Search Console shows far fewer indexed pages than the site actually has.
  • Pages load slowly enough that you notice it yourself, on your own phone, on your own site.

Two situations say wait. A site with no page aimed at the searches you want has a content gap wearing a technical costume, and the diagnosis matters because the two cost different money. A business missing from the map pack in its own town gets more from an hour on the profile than from a week on the server.

Frequently asked questions

Is technical SEO a one time project or a monthly service?

Both, in that order. The first pass is a project, because a backlog of redirects, canonicals and speed problems has an end to it. After that it becomes a monthly check, since platform updates, new pages and plugin changes reintroduce the same handful of failures.

How do I tell whether my site has a technical SEO problem at all?

Open Google Search Console and compare the indexed page count against the number of pages you know you published. A wide gap, or a traffic drop that lines up with a launch date, points at this layer. A site that ranks for nothing at all usually has a content problem wearing technical clothing.

Does technical SEO include page speed work?

Yes. Speed is measured against Core Web Vitals, and PageSpeed Insights returns a free speed report for any address you paste in. We work image weight, render blocking scripts, hosting and layout stability, and we say so when the remaining gain would take a rebuild.

Will technical fixes alone raise my rankings?

They lift a ceiling more often than they lift a position. A page Google cannot index cannot rank, so the fix is a precondition. Where the site was already crawlable and reasonably quick, the gain from technical work is usually small and the money does more on pages, the profile, or reviews.

Do you need access to my hosting or my site code?

For the diagnosis, no. Search Console, PageSpeed Insights and a crawl of your public pages cover most of it. Carrying out the repairs needs whatever access your platform requires, and that access stays yours when the engagement ends.
