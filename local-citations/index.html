What Local SEO Citations Are, in Plain Language

A citation is any place online listing your business name, address and phone number, whether or not it links to you. Apple Business Connect, Bing Places for Business, Yelp, Data Axle, Foursquare, your county chamber directory, the supplier page still carrying your old suite number. Google measures records like those against your Google Business Profile when it decides how far to trust the listing.

Two limits worth knowing before you spend on it. This work is dull, cheap and capped: when the profile itself sits unverified or on the wrong primary category, correcting fifty directories moves nothing until that's handled. And when a business has one address, one phone number and no history behind it, there's usually little to clean, so a monthly fee for it is money that would do more on reviews or pages.

Citations get sold beside link building and the two are separate jobs. A citation is a record. A link is a vote.

The word citation makes this sound more technical than it is. Google is trying to answer one question about your business: does this place exist where it says it does, under this name, reachable at this number. Every independent source agreeing pushes that answer toward yes. Every source disagreeing gives it a reason to hold back, and holding back on a local business looks like a card that belongs to somebody else.

The Citation Work That Runs Every Month

Aggregators Before Directories

A handful of data companies feed hundreds of sites that copy from them, and Data Axle is one of the names to know. Correct a listing on a small directory and the bad record can arrive again next quarter from upstream, so the upstream record gets fixed first and the downstream copies get checked after it settles.

Duplicates, Old Addresses and Retired Numbers

These are the ones that actually cost you. A second listing left over from a business you bought, a suite number that changed, the phone line you used before the tracking number, a closed location nobody ever took down. Each one hands Google a reason to hedge on which record is really yours.

The Sites Your Trade Needs

Past the general listings, every trade has a short set that carries weight with customers as well as with Google. Contractors get read on Angi and Houzz. Attorneys get read on Avvo and Justia. Clinics get read on Healthgrades. A junk hauler or a tree crew gets compared on Google and on the county chamber page, and not much beyond that. Which is why a package promising three hundred submissions is mostly padding, and why the list gets built per trade.

What One Clean Record Looks Like

Every listing carries the same six things, and the profile is the master copy. The business name exactly as it reads on the profile, with no city or keyword bolted onto the end. The street address in one format, suite included, or the address hidden and the towns listed for a business customers never visit. One primary phone number. The site address, with the secure version and the correct slash. The categories closest to what the profile holds. And hours that match what a customer would find if they drove over.

Month to month it turns into a spot check, since records drift on their own. A directory redesigns, an aggregator refreshes from an older source, somebody claims a listing that was already yours. Ten minutes a month on a short list holds the whole thing in place, which is what this line item actually buys.

How Citations Show Up in Your Map Pack Position

Google describes local ranking with three words: relevance, distance and prominence. Consistent business records feed the trust behind all three, since a listing Google can corroborate in twenty places is a safer thing to put in front of a searcher. Proximity to the person searching still outweighs the whole category, and a wrong primary category still overrides it.

So set the expectation where it belongs. Clean citations rarely produce a jump you can point at on a chart. They lift a drag, and they stop the slow bleed where a stale record splits the signals pointing at one business. The visible half of this work is on our local SEO page, and one client's full build, in the order it ran, is on the proof page.

The order the work runs in follows from that. The profile gets set first, since it's the master copy every other record gets compared to. Duplicates come next, because a second listing is the one problem in this category that can hold a business out of the pack by itself. Aggregators follow, then the trade list, then the long tail nobody thinks about. Running that sequence backwards is the common mistake, and it means correcting downstream copies of a record that's still wrong at the source.

Frequently asked questions

How many citations does a local business need?

Fewer than most packages sell. A short list of accurate records on the sites Google actually reads beats a hundred submissions to directories nobody visits. Accuracy across the important ones is the whole point of the exercise.

Do citations still matter now that Google has its own profile data?

Yes, as corroboration. Google weighs whether independent sources agree with what your profile claims, so contradictory records can hold back a map pack position that otherwise looks healthy from inside the dashboard.

What counts as inconsistent business information?

A different suite number, a missing unit, an old phone line, a shortened business name, or an address formatted so differently that it reads as another place entirely. Small formatting differences are usually fine. Two genuinely different addresses or phone numbers are the real problem.

Can I fix my own citations?

Yes, and plenty of owners do. Each site has its own claim and edit process, most are free, and the work is tedious more than difficult. What trips people up is the aggregators feeding other sites, and duplicate listings that need merging instead of editing.

Do you use an automated citation service?

Tools help for submissions to the large aggregators. Duplicates, merges and mismatched records get handled by hand, because an automated push can create a second listing where the job was to correct the first one.
