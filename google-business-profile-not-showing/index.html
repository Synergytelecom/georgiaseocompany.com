Google Business Profile Not Showing Up, and It Used To

A Google Business Profile not showing up is a different problem from one that was never set up. Maybe yours showed up fine last year.

Maybe it's still sitting in your dashboard looking healthy. What's changed is that it's missing from Maps and Search for the searches that should bring it up, or it has quietly dropped out of the map pack it used to hold.

Two very different situations wear that same symptom. A suspension or a duplicate listing is a repair, with a specific path to walk and an end to it. Getting outworked by a competitor is the case most owners are actually in, and that one is answered with upkeep, month after month.

Is It the Profile or the Website?

A dropped map pack spot and an invisible website are different problems with different fixes. A plumber's website can rank nowhere for "plumber" and the phone still rings all day, because the Business Profile holds the map pack on its own. A fast, well-built site can sit unseen locally because nobody ever filled out the profile behind it.

Which page you need comes down to what's actually missing, so search your service plus your town and look at what comes back. No listing for you in the map block at the top means you're in the right place.

A listing in the map but no sign of your site in the blue links underneath points to why the website is not showing up on Google. Nothing of yours anywhere, under any search, usually means no profile has been set up yet, and how to get your business on Google is the place to start.

Two Places to Look, and the Order to Look in Them

Some causes show up the moment you search from outside. Others only appear once you're logged into the dashboard. Working in that order saves a step.

Visible From Outside the Account

  • Plain competitive loss. Nothing is broken. The profile is live and being outranked by competitors with a more complete profile, a stronger category match, or more recent reviews. Put your listing side by side with whoever holds the spot now and this one shows up fast.
  • A gap in service area coverage. Search from a neighboring town the business covers; if nothing turns up there, the towns list on the profile is too narrow or was never filled in.
  • A duplicate quietly splitting the identity. Search the business name plus the city; a second, older, or auto-generated listing competing with the real one is visible the moment it appears in that search.

Visible Only From the Dashboard

  • A suspended status. A rules review, frequently triggered by an edit to the name, address, or category, can take a profile down without much visible warning outside the account itself.
  • A verification step stuck in limbo. An edit or a video check still pending review holds a profile back from showing at all, and the dashboard is the only place that says so.
  • A category quietly mismatched to how people search. Two categories with almost the same name can rank for entirely different searches. Catching it means opening the dashboard and comparing your own category against what a competitor winning that search has selected.

Check These Yourself First

  1. Search your business name in a signed-out or private window. Your own login biases what Google shows you, so a signed-out search is the only reliable way to see the listing a customer sees.
  2. Check your Business Profile dashboard directly for a status flag. A suspended or pending verification status is usually spelled out there in plain language, where a search from outside only shows you the symptom.
  3. Review and update your service areas. List the towns and radius you serve, and update that list whenever the crew's range changes.
  4. Reconsider your primary category against two or three close alternatives. Look at what similar businesses currently outranking you have listed, and make sure your own choice matches how customers search, which is often different from how the business describes itself internally.
  5. Search your business name plus your city. A duplicate found there has to be reported or merged, so write down exactly what you found before moving on.
  6. Add a few current photos and check your posting history. A profile that hasn't been touched in months signals to both Google and a searcher that the business may have gone quiet, even when it very much has not.

When to Bring In Help

Checking a status flag, updating service areas, and reconsidering a category are things most owners can do themselves in under an hour. Suspensions and duplicates are a different, more technical category of problem, and worth treating that way rather than guessing at a fix.

Three cases are worth handing off:

  • A suspension. Start with the notice itself, because the appeal has to answer the exact reason it names. Reinstatement runs on evidence. Business documents, consistent name and address data, proof the business operates as described. A rejected first attempt makes the next review stricter.
  • A duplicate that needs a formal merge. Merging incorrectly can lose reviews that took years to accumulate.
  • A competitor posting, updating photos, and collecting reviews on a schedule yours hasn't kept. Nothing is broken here, so there's nothing to repair. Our Google Business Profile management page covers the work that closes that kind of gap.

Frequently asked questions

Should I just delete the profile and start over if it won't show up?

No, and it is worth stopping on this one. Recreating a profile that already has history throws away reviews and trust signals that took years to build, and starting fresh rarely fixes whatever was wrong in the first place. Diagnose the specific cause first; a brand new listing is closer to a last resort than a first move.

Google wants a video walkthrough before it will verify us. Is that normal?

Yes, and it has become more common, particularly for businesses without a public storefront. Paperwork and signage go a long way toward proving a business exists on camera; a vehicle or the equipment itself rounds it out for trades where that applies. Polish is not the point, evidence is.

Is there a limit on how many service areas I can list?

Yes. Google caps the list, so the field is not unlimited and there is no room to pad it with every town in driving range. List the towns and counties the business actually serves, rather than a smaller or larger footprint than reality.

Can I speed up a suspended profile's reinstatement?

Not by resubmitting the same appeal repeatedly. A single, complete filing with the right supporting documents moves faster than several rushed, incomplete ones.

Does a low review count explain a missing profile?

It can be a contributing factor, since review count and recency are part of what Google weighs, but it is rarely the only cause. Category, service areas, and status are worth checking first.
