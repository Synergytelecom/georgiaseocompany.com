What Link Building Services Do for a Local Business

A link is another website pointing at yours, and search engines have always read one as a kind of vote. Link building is the work of earning those votes on purpose. For a Georgia service business the useful ones are few and specific: the chamber of commerce you pay dues to, the association your license comes through, the supplier that lists its dealers, the ball field with your name on the fence, the county paper that covered the storm your crews worked.

Which brings up where links sit in the order of operations. Most businesses we look at have a profile problem or a page problem, and links are the third item on that list. If you're missing from the map pack in your own town, start on local SEO and come back to link building in a few months.

The other thing to know going in is the pace. Nobody earns fifty of these in a month, and a report claiming they did is describing something you'd rather not own. A handful a month, from places a person in your county would recognize, is what this looks like when it's real. That pace is also why the work is easy to fake and easy to oversell, which is where the cheap packages find their opening.

The Link Building Work That Happens Every Month

  • A link gap read. Which sites point at the businesses holding the spots you want, and which of those would plausibly point at you.
  • The local list. Chamber directories, association member pages, supplier dealer locators, sponsorship pages, vendor lists, partner and alumni pages.
  • Being useful to a reporter. A county outlet needs somebody in your trade to explain something a few times a year, and that is a real link with a real audience behind it.
  • Internal links. The links you already control, pointed at the pages that need the help.
  • A read on what points at you now. Including the junk that arrived on its own, and whether any of it warrants a disavow.
  • The month's ledger. Which links landed, which sites they came from, and which asks went nowhere.

Most of what makes this work is already sitting in your own records. The suppliers you buy from, the associations taking your dues, the events you wrote a check to last year, the general contractors you sub for, the nonprofit whose parking lot your crew cleared after the ice storm. Each of those is a relationship that already exists, which is why the ask lands. Cold outreach to strangers is the part with the low reply rate, and we spend the least time there.

Local SEO Link Building Looks Different From the National Kind

National link building is a volume business: guest posts, digital PR campaigns, resource page outreach, all of it aimed at moving a site in a market with no geography in it. A roofing company in Floyd County plays a different game. One chamber listing, one supplier dealer page and one write up in the local paper carry more for it than twenty generic articles on sites nobody in Rome has heard of.

Links feed the prominence side of local ranking, which is the part still open to you once the address is fixed and the category is right. So that's the size of the prize: a slow compounding push on the organic results, and a lighter one on the three cards.

Geography also decides which links are worth chasing. A business working Floyd, Bartow, Gordon and Polk counties has four county chambers, four sets of local events, and four papers, and a link from each one says something specific about where the business operates. A link from a national blog says nothing about Cartersville. That's the practical difference between local link building and the national version, and it's why the target list gets built from the map rather than from a database.

The Link Building We Turn Down

Some of what gets sold as link building is a liability with an invoice attached. Google publishes a link spam policy, and the patterns named in it are the same ones the cheap packages run on.

  • Networks selling links by the thousand, priced per link, delivered as a spreadsheet.
  • Private blog networks, meaning a stable of sites that exist for the sole purpose of linking out.
  • Mass directory submissions, most of which nobody reads and none of which mention your town.
  • Reciprocal link trades arranged at scale.
  • Spun or machine written guest posts placed on sites that will take anything.

We'll also say when a link is worth having for reasons that have little to do with search. A chamber listing that sends you two calls a year earns its dues even if it never moves a ranking.

Who Needs Link Building and Who Should Spend Elsewhere

It pays for a business in a contested trade or a metro market, where everybody left in the race already has the listing and the site in order. It pays for a program running across several counties at once. And it pays for a site whose on page and technical work is clean and whose position has stopped moving anyway.

It's the wrong first purchase for a single town business with an unclaimed listing, for a site with three pages on it, and for a market where the pack is thin enough that a complete profile and steady reviews would take the top card on their own.

There's a middle case worth naming, because it comes up in most Georgia towns we look at. The business is visible locally, holds a card in its home town, and gets beaten everywhere else by a firm out of metro Atlanta that has been earning links for a decade. Links are part of closing that gap and they're the slow part. Pages for the towns and a review pace that beats theirs move first, links carry the ground after that, and any plan claiming to do it the other way around is selling the expensive step first.

Frequently asked questions

How many links does my business need?

No single number applies across markets. The useful comparison is the set of sites linking to the businesses currently outranking you, since that shows what the search you want has cost so far. In a small Georgia town that set is often short enough to read through in one sitting.

Do you buy links?

No. Paid link placement violates Google guidelines, and the risk lands on your site instead of on the vendor who sold it. We earn links through memberships, suppliers, sponsorships, local press, and work worth mentioning.

How long does link building take to show up?

Slower than on page work and slower than profile fixes. A link has to be found, crawled and weighed, and the effect compounds across many links over months. That is why it is a poor first purchase for a business that needs the phone to ring soon.

Are citations the same thing as links?

No. A citation is a listing of your name, address and phone number, and its job is consistency so Google trusts one business record. A link is a vote from another site. Some citations happen to carry a link, but the two get bought and repaired for different reasons.

Should I disavow bad links pointing at my site?

Usually no. Google ignores most low quality links on its own, and a disavow file built carelessly can cut off links that were helping. It earns its keep where a site has a history of paid link buying or an obvious spam attack.
