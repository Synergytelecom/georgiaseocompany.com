Carroll County runs far enough north to south that its two ends behave like separate markets. Carrollton anchors the bottom of it, I-20 crosses the top, and a business selling into both has to be found twice.

Two Ends of Carroll County, Two Different Customers

Carrollton is the county seat and the commercial center of this corner of west Georgia, with Adamson Square downtown and the University of West Georgia keeping a steady population of people who arrive knowing nobody. US 27 runs north and south through it, carrying on past Bremen and Cedartown and eventually to our office in Rome.

The top of the county is a different economy. I-20 crosses it, Temple and Villa Rica sit on the interstate, and a good share of the households up there commute east in the morning and do a fair amount of their buying on the way home. The deciding happens in a car, on a phone, before anybody turns off the exit.

A business trying to answer both ends from one listing and one page usually turns up strong in the half it sits in and absent from the other. The remedy is ordinary. Coverage gets drawn to the roads you really run, and each town that sends you work earns a page carrying its own name.

Who Actually Turns Up on a Carroll County Search

Very little of the field here was built by an agency. Your map cards fill with other Carroll County businesses plus Douglasville, Bremen and Villa Rica companies that drew their coverage wide enough to take you in. Most of that field was claimed in one sitting years back, and keeping a listing current is a habit most businesses lose inside a quarter.

Drawing a Service Area for a County This Wide

Small counties tempt owners into modest coverage areas, and big ones tempt them into enormous ones. Carroll invites the second mistake, because the run from the Alabama line to the Douglas County line is long enough that a circle covering all of it takes in ground your trucks have never seen.

The honest version follows your invoices. Take a routine month from last year's books, and the ground it covered is the service area, while the towns that billed inside it are the pages worth writing. For most businesses based down near the courthouse, that ordering comes out Carrollton first, then Mount Zion and the western side toward Alabama, then the interstate towns once the jobs justify the drive.

Lake and golf communities complicate it in a useful way. Fairfield Plantation sits over near the Douglas line and behaves like its own small market, with households that search by the name of the community rather than the name of the county. Those names cost almost nothing to rank for and practically nobody writes pages for them.

Frequently asked questions

Our address is in Carrollton but we work the I-20 towns. How does that get handled?

Coverage on the profile reaches up the county, and every interstate town sending real work gets a page of its own. The pin stays in Carrollton, since that is the address you can be verified at, and the pages do the work of showing up in Temple or Villa Rica.

Is the university worth building anything around?

Indirectly. It keeps a steady flow of households arriving who know nobody in town, and that is exactly the customer a search decides. It shows up in how a page gets written far more than in any keyword you would target on purpose.

How many Carroll County towns should our site have pages for?

As many as you would genuinely take a job in. For most trades down here that lands at three or four, and the list should grow out of last year invoices rather than out of a map.

Can you work with the site we already have?

Often. A good number of accounts start on the site that already exists, with the profile rebuilt and a few town pages added, and replacing it only makes sense when the structure underneath is past saving.
