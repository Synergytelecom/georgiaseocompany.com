Three Quotes for the Same Site Rarely Mean the Same Thing

Picture a plumbing company asking for an eight page site: home, four service pages, three towns served, one contact form. Send that exact scope to three different builders and the replies can land anywhere from four figures to five, and the gap is rarely padding on one end or laziness on the other.

The lowest quote often assumes you write every word yourself and drops a stock theme over it with the logo swapped in. The middle quote usually includes written copy for each page but treats how search engines read the site as an afterthought, something a plugin is supposed to handle on its own. The highest quote is the one that priced in research up front: what your customers type into Google, headings and page titles built around those exact terms, and load time checked before launch instead of after a complaint comes in.

None of those three builders is necessarily overcharging or underdelivering. They are pricing three different jobs that all happen to be called a website. Reading a quote means working out which of the three you are being offered. Two questions get you there faster than the price does: who writes the words on each page, and who is responsible for how those pages are built to be found.

What Owning the Site Costs After It Launches

The build price is a down payment, not the full bill. A domain runs $10 to $20+ per year and hosting for most small business sites runs $5 to $50+ per month regardless of which build type you picked in the table above.

Upkeep is the line item that does depend on that earlier choice. A site running on a plugin heavy platform is software, and software needs someone watching for updates, patching, and backups, work published at $30 to $150+ monthly for routine patching and $200 to $500+ for a fuller plan that layers on hands-on content edits and a monthly performance summary. A site built by hand has no plugin stack to patch, so that monthly line shrinks to near zero and the freed up budget goes toward new pages instead.

What a Site Nobody Visits Costs You

None of the numbers above account for what a site earns once it is live, which is the number that decides whether any of them were worth paying. A site nobody can find through search brings in nothing, no matter how little it cost to build. A pricier site built to be found starts paying itself back the first time a stranger reaches it through a search instead of a handed out business card.

One example of that math: a land surveying client of ours had the site built and optimized as a single project rather than two, and it has kept pulling new visitors from search well past launch at no added monthly spend beyond what the build already covered. In our experience, the businesses that spend the most bought the cheapest option first, watched it sit unseen for a year, then paid again for a rebuild plus the search work that should have shipped the first time.

Frequently asked questions

Are these your prices?

No. These figures are published 2026 market ranges, gathered so you have something to measure a proposal against. They are not what we charge for a website. Our number depends on page count, who writes the copy, and how much search engine foundation gets built in from the start.

Why did three quotes for what sounds like the same site come back so different?

Usually because they are not pricing the same job. A low quote often assumes you supply the writing yourself and skips search structure entirely; a higher quote prices in research, page by page writing, and a technical foundation built to be found. Ask what is included before setting two invoices side by side.

Does a plugin free site really need less upkeep than a WordPress build?

Usually, on the ongoing side. A plugin heavy platform needs $30 to $150+ per month in basic patching and updates just to stay current and secure, while a site assembled with no plugin layer at all has very little comparable to maintain, so that budget can go toward new pages instead.

Does a website price normally include the SEO work, or is that priced separately?

It depends on who you ask, which is worth pinning down before you sign. Some builds price in page structure, load speed, and keyword researched content from the first wireframe; others hand over a finished template and treat search visibility as a separate project to buy later. Ask which one is on the table.

Is a cheap site worth it if my budget is tight right now?

A site is worth what it earns back, so the comparison that counts is cost against customers produced, not cost against the invoice. In our experience, a modest budget spent on a site nobody can find through search does less for a business than a smaller number of well built pages that rank.
