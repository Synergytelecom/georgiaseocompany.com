The Range Depends Entirely on Who Builds It

How much does a website cost is really three different questions wearing one headline, because a do it yourself builder, a freelancer, and a small studio are not selling the same thing. A site builder sells you templates and your own evenings. A freelancer sells a mix of skill and availability that swings wildly from a great value to a stalled project depending on who you hire. A studio build sells one party accountable for the finished, working site, structure and words included.

The published 2026 ranges below reflect the broader market for each path. What we would actually charge for a given project depends on page count, who writes the copy, and how much of the search engine foundation gets built in from day one rather than bolted on after the site already looks finished.

What Actually Moves the Number Inside a Band

Two proposals that both say ten pages can land thousands apart for real reasons. Who writes the words matters more than almost anything else on the list: a builder that leaves the writing to you has handed off the single hardest task in the whole project, and that unfinished homework is the actual reason so many website builds quietly stall in month three with a launch date nobody mentions anymore. Custom design built around your business costs more than a rented template, and it should, since the template was never built with your services or your market in mind.

The search engine foundation is the other quiet variable. Clean URLs, correct heading structure, fast load times, and pages built around what your customers actually search are decisions that belong at the wireframe stage, not a patch applied after a site already looks done. Retrofitting that foundation after launch costs more than building it in from the start.

The Ongoing Costs That Show Up After Launch

The build price is not the last number you will see. A few small, recurring costs follow every website, and the size of one of them depends heavily on how the site was built in the first place:

Ongoing costPublished 2026 range
Domain registration$10 to $20+ per year
Hosting$5 to $50+ per month
Basic maintenance$30 to $150+ per month
Full service maintenance plan$200 to $500+ per month

That maintenance spread is not random either. A plugin heavy platform is software that needs patching, and skipping that upkeep is how small business sites end up compromised. A hand built static site carries almost none of that overhead, since there is no plugin stack or database to maintain in the first place, so the same budget can go toward new pages instead of keeping the machine from breaking.

Why a Cheap Site That Cannot Rank Costs More in the Long Run

The build price is not actually the number to optimize for. A site nobody can find through search brings in nothing, regardless of how little it cost to build, and nothing divided into any invoice is still nothing. A more expensive site that shows up for real searches and turns even a modest share of that traffic into calls earns its cost back in a way you can actually track, while the cheaper option that nobody finds just sits there as a sunk cost.

A land surveying client of ours is the clearest proof of that math: we built and optimized the site as a single project rather than two separate ones, and months after launch it keeps earning new visitors straight from search, not from anything we are still paying to run. In our experience, the businesses that end up paying twice are the ones that bought a cheap site first, watched it rank for nothing, and then paid again for a rebuild plus the SEO work that should have been part of the plan from the start.

Frequently asked questions

Are these your prices?

No. These figures are published 2026 market ranges gathered so you have something real to measure a proposal against. They are not what we personally charge for a website; that number depends on page count, who writes the copy, and how much search engine foundation gets built in from the start.

What is a realistic website budget for a small business?

A small business should plan on $2,000 to $10,000+ for a professionally designed and written site under the published 2026 figures. A freelancer build typically lands at $500 to $5,000+, and a do it yourself builder can run for as little as $0 up to $50+ per month. Where a given project actually lands mostly comes down to page count and who is doing the writing.

Why do custom built sites cost more than a template?

A template is decorated, not engineered around your business: the words, the page structure, and the technical foundation search engines actually read are all generic. A custom build spends money on research, writing, and structure matched to what your own customers search, which is exactly the part a template skips.

What ongoing costs come after the site is live?

A domain costs $10 to $20+ a year to register, and most small business hosting plans run $5 to $50+ monthly. Maintenance is the variable piece: a plugin heavy platform commonly needs $30 to $150+ per month in upkeep, while a hand built static site needs very little beyond content updates.

Is a cheap site worth it if my budget is tight right now?

A site is only worth what it earns back, so the honest comparison is cost against customers produced, not cost against the invoice total. In our experience, a modest budget spent on a site nobody can find through search does less for a business than a smaller number of well built pages that actually rank.
