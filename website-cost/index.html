How Much Does a Website Cost in Georgia?

How much does a website cost? It depends on who builds it, and on the bills that keep arriving after launch.

Get a free visibility audit

Website Design Cost, by Who Builds It

A built site runs $500 to $10,000+ depending on who builds it and how many pages it carries. Four paths, and what separates the rows is less size than who answers for it when a page is wrong the day after launch.

Build typeTypical cost
DIY site builder$0 to $50+ per month
Freelancer build$500 to $5,000+ one time
Full custom build by a studio$2,000 to $10,000+ one time
Ecommerce build$5,000 to $50,000+ depending on scope

A DIY builder puts you in charge of every decision, including the ones you didn't know you were making. A freelancer puts one person's schedule and skill level on the line.

A studio puts a business, and a contract, behind the finished result. Page count moves the number, and so does how much of the work, and the risk, you keep for yourself.

Three Quotes for the Same Site Rarely Mean the Same Thing

Put one nine-page scope out for bids, a home page, four service pages, three town pages, and a contact form, and the replies can land anywhere from four figures to five. The gap is rarely padding on one end or laziness on the other.

The lowest quote often assumes you write every word yourself and drops a stock theme over it with your logo swapped in. The middle quote usually includes written copy for each page and treats how search engines read the site as something a plugin handles automatically.

The highest quote priced in the research up front: what your customers type into Google, headings and page titles built around those exact terms, and load time checked before launch instead of after a complaint comes in.

All three are pricing different jobs that happen to share a name. Two questions sort out which one you're being offered faster than the price does: who writes the words on each page, and who answers for how those pages get found.

What Owning the Site Costs After It Launches

The build price is a down payment on the full bill. A domain runs $10 to $20+ per year, and hosting for most small business sites runs $5 to $50+ per month whichever route you picked.

Where Website Design Cost Stops and Upkeep Starts

Upkeep is the line that swings with which build you picked. A site running on a plugin-heavy platform is software, and software needs someone watching for updates, patching, and backups.

Routine patching runs $30 to $150+ a month, and $200 to $500+ for a fuller plan that layers on hands-on content edits and a monthly performance summary. A site built by hand has no plugin stack to patch, so that monthly line shrinks to near zero and the freed-up budget goes toward new pages.

What the Site Earns Back

A website is worth what it earns back, and that's the figure that decides whether the build price was money well spent. A site nobody can find through search brings in nothing, whatever it cost to build.

One client's site was built and optimized as a single project, and it has kept pulling new visitors from search well past launch week. In our experience the businesses that end up spending the most bought the cheapest option first, watched it sit unseen for a year, then paid again for a rebuild plus the search work that should have shipped with it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a website cost for a small business in Georgia?

Most small business sites land between $500 and $10,000+, and the number tracks page count and who writes the copy more than anything else. A DIY builder can start near nothing per month, a freelancer typically runs $500 to $5,000+ one time, and a full custom build runs $2,000 to $10,000+ one time. Hosting and a domain follow every one of those.

Why did three quotes for what sounds like the same site come back so different?

Usually because they are pricing different jobs. A low quote often assumes you supply the writing yourself and skips search structure entirely; a higher quote prices in research, page-by-page writing, and a technical foundation built to be found. Ask what is included before setting two invoices side by side.

Does a plugin-free site really need less upkeep than a WordPress build?

Usually, on the ongoing side. A plugin-heavy platform needs $30 to $150+ per month in basic patching and updates just to stay current and secure, while a site assembled with no plugin layer at all has very little comparable to maintain, so that budget can go toward new pages.

Does a website price normally include the SEO work, or is that priced separately?

Builders split on this, which is worth pinning down before you sign. Some builds price in page structure, load speed, and keyword-researched content from the first wireframe; others hand over a finished template and treat search visibility as a separate project to buy later. Ask which one is on the table.

Is a cheap site worth it if my budget is tight right now?

A site is worth what it earns back, so the comparison that counts is cost against customers produced. A modest budget spent on a site nobody can find through search does less for a business than a smaller number of well-built pages that rank.
