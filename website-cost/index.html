We Price the Build and the Search Work as One Project

We build the site and the search work together. Page structure, URLs, headings, and load speed get settled at the wireframe stage around the terms your customers actually type into Google, so the site can start earning search traffic the day it launches.

After launch the work keeps going. Published 2026 ranges for a built site run $500 to $10,000+ depending on who builds it and how many pages, and the hosting that follows every launch runs $5 to $50+ a month. Three Quotes for the Same Site Rarely Mean the Same Thing

Put one nine page scope out for bids, a home page, four service pages, three town pages, and a contact form, and the replies can land anywhere from four figures to five. The gap is rarely padding on one end or laziness on the other.

The lowest quote often assumes you write every word yourself and drops a stock theme over it with your logo swapped in. The middle quote usually includes written copy for each page and treats how search engines read the site as something a plugin handles automatically. The highest quote priced in the research up front: what your customers type into Google, headings and page titles built around those exact terms, and load time checked before launch instead of after a complaint comes in.

All three are pricing different jobs that happen to share a name. Two questions sort out which one you're being offered faster than the price does: who writes the words on each page, and who answers for how those pages get found.

What Owning the Site Costs After It Launches

The build price is a down payment on the full bill. A domain runs $10 to $20+ per year, and hosting for most small business sites runs $5 to $50+ per month whichever row of the table above you picked.

Upkeep is the line that does depend on that choice. A site running on a plugin heavy platform is software, and software needs someone watching for updates, patching, and backups. Published rates for that run $30 to $150+ monthly for routine patching, and $200 to $500+ for a fuller plan that layers on hands-on content edits and a monthly performance summary. A site built by hand has no plugin stack to patch, so that monthly line shrinks to near zero and the freed up budget goes toward new pages.

What the Site Earns Back

None of the numbers above account for what the site earns once it's live, which is the figure that decides whether any of them were worth paying. A site nobody can find through search brings in nothing, whatever it cost to build. One client's site was built and optimized as a single project, and it has kept pulling new visitors from search well past launch week. In our experience the businesses that end up spending the most bought the cheapest option first, watched it sit unseen for a year, then paid again for a rebuild plus the search work that should have shipped with it.

Frequently asked questions

Are these your prices?

No. These figures are published 2026 market ranges, gathered so you have something to measure a proposal against. They describe the market, not our invoice. Our own number depends on page count, who writes the copy, and how much search engine foundation gets built in from the start.

Why did three quotes for what sounds like the same site come back so different?

Usually because they are pricing different jobs. A low quote often assumes you supply the writing yourself and skips search structure entirely; a higher quote prices in research, page by page writing, and a technical foundation built to be found. Ask what is included before setting two invoices side by side.

Does a plugin free site really need less upkeep than a WordPress build?

Usually, on the ongoing side. A plugin heavy platform needs $30 to $150+ per month in basic patching and updates just to stay current and secure, while a site assembled with no plugin layer at all has very little comparable to maintain, so that budget can go toward new pages.

Does a website price normally include the SEO work, or is that priced separately?

Builders split on this, which is worth pinning down before you sign. Some builds price in page structure, load speed, and keyword researched content from the first wireframe; others hand over a finished template and treat search visibility as a separate project to buy later. Ask which one is on the table.

Is a cheap site worth it if my budget is tight right now?

A site is worth what it earns back, so the comparison that counts is cost against customers produced. In our experience, a modest budget spent on a site nobody can find through search does less for a business than a smaller number of well built pages that rank.
