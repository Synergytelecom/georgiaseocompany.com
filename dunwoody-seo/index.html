Why Dunwoody Sits on Two Search Markets at Once

Perimeter Center fills one end of the city with office towers, and the searches coming out of them are business to business: somebody in a suite looking for a vendor, a service, a contractor who can be there this week.

Dunwoody Village and the neighborhoods along Mount Vernon Road and Chamblee Dunwoody Road produce the other kind, a homeowner with a problem and a phone in hand.

Those two audiences use different words for the same service, and one page trying to serve both usually serves each of them badly. So the site gets split by intent, and the profile's categories and services get set for whichever half brings the work.

Web Design for Dunwoody, GA Businesses

The Suite Number Problem in Dunwoody Office Towers

A business in a Perimeter Center building has an address with a suite number, a shared lobby, and often a shared mail room. Google is strict about this. The listed address has to be a place a customer could actually reach you during your posted hours, suite included, and it cross-checks that whole line against the directories.

Two things go wrong routinely. The suite gets dropped from half the records, so the address reads two different ways depending on where Google looks.

Or a coworking desk gets listed as a permanent office, which puts the profile a single complaint away from a suspension. Both are fixable before they cost you the listing, and both take a careful pass through the citation records.

The county line adds one more wrinkle. The Perimeter office district straddles DeKalb and Fulton, so a company a block off Ashford Dunwoody Road can end up with a Dunwoody sign, a Sandy Springs neighbor, and results pulling from both. The profile's coverage gets set to the buildings and the streets you actually work.
