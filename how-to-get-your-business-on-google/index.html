How to Get Your Business on Google

If a search for your own business name comes back empty, here is what usually causes that, and the specific steps to fix it yourself first.

The Symptom

You search your business name, or the exact service you offer plus your town, and nothing from your business appears, not in the map, not in the regular results, nothing. That is a different problem than ranking poorly. A business that ranks poorly is at least on the board. A business that is missing entirely usually has not been set up on Google at all, or was set up once and stalled partway through. Whether you searched how to get my business on google, claim my business on google, or how to verify my business on google, the fix below is usually the same handful of causes.

In Our Experience, the Usual Causes, Most Common First

  1. No Google Business Profile has ever been created. This is the most common cause by far, especially for a newer business or one that has always relied on word of mouth. There is simply nothing on Google to find.
  2. A profile was started but never verified. Someone began the setup process, entered a name and address, and stopped before finishing verification. An unverified profile does not show up in search results at all.
  3. The profile exists, but someone else controls it. A past marketing vendor, a former employee, or a previous owner of the business set it up under a login the current business no longer has access to.
  4. A duplicate listing is splitting the identity. An old, auto generated, or accidentally re created profile exists alongside the real one, and Google has not decided which one to trust.
  5. The website has no signals pointing to it yet. A brand new site with no links, no citations, and no Business Profile referencing it can sit invisible for a while simply because nothing has told Google it exists.

Fix It Yourself: The Setup Steps

  1. Search your exact business name in Google Maps, in a private or incognito window. This rules out your own browser showing you a personalized result and tells you whether a profile already exists, unclaimed or not.
  2. If nothing exists, create the profile at google.com/business. Sign in with an account your business controls, not a personal or former employee's login. The name field should match your actual signage and paperwork word for word; packing extra keywords into it is against the rules and a common reason a fresh profile gets flagged.
  3. If an unclaimed profile already exists, claim it instead of creating a new one. Google's "own this business" flow attaches it to your account without creating a competing duplicate.
  4. Choose the closest matching primary category, not the broadest one. Pick what the business actually is, and add real services underneath it rather than trying to cover every possibility in the name or category field.
  5. Decide honestly whether customers visit you or you go to them. A storefront shows its address. A business that works at the customer's location hides the address and lists service areas instead, a separate setting that gets missed often.
  6. Complete verification promptly and only once. Postcard, phone, or video, depending on what Google offers for your business type. If a verification attempt gets rejected, fix exactly what the rejection named before trying again rather than resubmitting the same information repeatedly.

What Happens After You're Set Up

Being visible and ranking well are two different things. Once a profile is created and verified, in our experience it takes some time to fully settle, often a couple of weeks, as Google gathers more confidence in the information. Give it that window, then stand somewhere in your actual service area with a phone and search your own services the way a stranger would, and you should at least appear somewhere, even if not yet at the top.

If you are live and showing up for your own business name but still invisible for the actual services you sell, that is no longer a setup problem, it is a ranking problem, and it is covered separately in our guide to ranking higher on Google.

A first time claim and verify, with no ownership dispute and no duplicate to untangle, is genuinely something most owners can do themselves in an afternoon. Where it gets harder is exactly where the steps above stop being enough.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a cost to set up a Google Business Profile?

No, setup and verification do not cost anything, and neither does earning a spot in Maps or the regular search results afterward. Google Ads is the one paid product in the family, and it is entirely optional and separate from any of this.

How long does verification actually take?

It depends on the method. Phone or email verification can complete the same day. Postcard verification can take up to two weeks to arrive. Video verification timing varies but is often faster than a postcard.

Can I use my home address if I work from home?

Only if customers actually visit that address. If they do not, Google requires you to hide the address and list service areas instead, which also avoids publishing a home address publicly.

What if my business name already shows up when I search, just not in Maps?

That usually means a profile exists somewhere, unclaimed, suspended, or set up under someone else's login, rather than nothing existing at all. The private window search in the steps above is the fastest way to tell which case applies.

Should I list every service I offer, or just the main one?

List every real service you want to be found for. A profile that only names one service will not surface for searches about the others, even if you actually do that work.
