What gets settled before anyone designs a page

We settle the structure, the URLs, the headings, and the load speed before anyone designs a page, so the finished site can start earning rankings the day it launches. Every page is built for a service you sell or a town you sell it in, on a layout that works on a phone first.

If what you want is a new look over the same structure underneath, a design shop will do it faster and for less money. Our build is for a business that needs the site to bring in work.

Most website projects run the other order: design the pages, launch, then hand the site to whoever does the SEO. By then the URLs and the heading structure are already set, and half the SEO work becomes undoing decisions rather than building on them. Someone searching website design near me is usually on a phone on a weak signal, which is the case the layout gets built for first.

What the build includes

Every build carries the same six pieces, sized to the page count:

  • Mobile-first layout. Built and checked on phone screens first, since that's where most local search traffic arrives.
  • Page speed. Compressed images, light pages, and hosting picked for load time, measured in PageSpeed Insights before and after launch.
  • URLs and headings. One clear address per page and a heading order a search engine can read as an outline.
  • A page per service and per town. What you sell, and where you sell it, so each search has a page aimed at it.
  • Contact paths. Phone number and contact form on every page, up where a customer looks first.
  • Analytics. Connected at launch, so you can see which pages produce calls and form fills.

The order we build in

Structure decisions are cheap to make early and expensive to undo late, so the sequence holds on every build:

  1. Sitemap. Every page the business needs gets mapped first, service pages and town pages included, before any visual design starts.
  2. Wireframes. Each page's content and heading order gets laid out, so the outline works for the visitor and for the search engine reading it.
  3. Design and build. The visual layer goes on top of that structure, built mobile-first, with load speed and image weight checked at each stage.
  4. Launch. Analytics connected, the site submitted to search engines, redirects mapped and tested.

The mistakes we clean up on a rebuild

Most web work that stops short of a full rebuild is cleanup, and the same few mistakes come up again and again.

One page trying to cover everything

A single services page covering everything the business does gives a search engine nothing specific to rank and gives the visitor no clear next step, because it's addressing every customer type at once.

Photos dropped in at full resolution

A picture taken on a phone and uploaded untouched can slow a whole page down by itself, and the visitor who leaves and the crawler that gives up are answering the same delay.

A rebuild that drops the old addresses

This one costs the most and shows up only after launch. Old page addresses that already ranked get replaced with nothing pointing at the new ones. In Search Console, open Indexing, then Pages: the reasons list carries rows named "Not found (404)" and "Page with redirect", and a rebuild done without a redirect map fills the first row with pages that used to bring you customers. We map the redirects before anything old goes offline, so a 301 hands the old address, and the ranking history attached to it, to the page that replaced it.

Frequently asked questions

Do you only build new sites, or fix existing ones too?

Both. Some businesses need a full rebuild, others need specific fixes: speed, mobile layout, or missing pages, while keeping most of what already works. We scope it after seeing the site.

How long does a web design project take?

A standard business site typically takes several weeks from sitemap approval to launch, longer for larger sites with many service and location pages. We give a specific timeline once we know the page count.

What platform do you build on?

We choose the platform based on what the site needs to do and what you want to be able to update yourself afterward, rather than defaulting to one system for every client.

Will a new website hurt my current rankings?

A poorly executed migration can, mainly through broken redirects or lost pages. We plan redirects and preserve what already ranks before anything old goes offline, which is the step that gets skipped when this goes wrong.

Do I need SEO work along with a new website?

A site can launch on its own, but a fast, well-structured site without any supporting SEO work will not rank itself. The build pairs naturally with at least Google Business Profile and on-page work, since that is what gives a new site a reason to show up in search.

Who owns the site once the project is finished?

You do. The finished site is yours, along with the logins set up for it during the build, and that does not change if you later move the SEO work elsewhere or bring it in house.

Getting a new website quoted right now?

A rebuild isn't always the answer. Send us the site you have and we'll tell you what already ranks on it, what's worth carrying over, and whether the money would do more somewhere else first.
