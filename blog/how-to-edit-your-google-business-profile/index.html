How to Edit Your Google Business Profile

You can edit the whole listing from your phone in a few minutes, and a handful of those edits go past a review before anyone else sees them.

Two Ways Into Your Google Business Profile

There's no separate dashboard to log into anymore for a single-location business. You edit the listing from inside Google itself.

  1. From Google Search. Sign in with the account that manages the listing, then search your own business name. A management panel appears above the results with buttons for Edit profile, Read reviews, and the rest. That panel is the editor.
  2. From Google Maps. Open the app, tap your account picture, then tap Your Business Profiles. Same fields, laid out for a thumb, which is the faster route when you're standing on a job site.

Businesses with several locations still work out of Business Profile Manager, where locations sit in groups and some fields can be changed across a group at once.

If none of that appears when you search your own name, one of three things is true. You're signed in with the wrong Google account, which is the usual answer. Nobody ever granted your account access, and adding a user to the profile covers how that gets fixed. Or the listing was never claimed at all, which puts you at how to get your business on Google instead of here.

Editing Hours, Phone, and Website on the Profile

Open Edit profile and the fields sit under a few tabs. About covers the name, category, and description. Contact covers phone and website. Hours covers the schedule.

Hours. Set the regular weekly schedule first, then add special hours for the days you break from it. Holidays are the ones that bite. Leave them blank and Google warns searchers that hours might differ, which reads as uncertainty right at the moment somebody's deciding whether to drive over. If you close for a stretch, the temporarily closed setting says so honestly and keeps the listing intact.

Phone. The primary number is the one on the call button, so it needs to ring somewhere a person answers during your posted hours. A second number can go in the additional field. If you use a tracking number, make sure the same number appears on the website, because a mismatch between the two records is exactly the kind of inconsistency Google weighs against a business.

Website. Point it at the page that answers the search, which for most local businesses is the home page and for some is a specific service page. Check that the link still resolves after any website rebuild, since an old address left in that field sends every click to an error.

Changing the Categories on Your Google Business Profile

The primary category decides which searches the listing is even eligible to appear for, which makes it the field worth the most thought and the field most likely to move your rankings in either direction.

Category first: that field names what the business is. The services menu below it is where the detail goes, one job per line. Owners tend to reach for the broadest option on the category list, and the broad one drops you into a contest with every business across three trades. The narrower, more accurate one puts you up against the handful actually doing your work.

How to Compare Categories Without Guessing

Search the service plus your town in a signed-out window, then open the listings holding the top map spots and look at what category each one has selected. Google shows it right under the business name. Where the leaders agree and you differ, you've found something worth changing. Where they disagree with each other, the category is probably not what's separating them.

Add additional categories only for work you genuinely sell. Stacking unrelated ones to widen the net dilutes the match on the searches you actually want and can draw a rules review you didn't need.

Adding and Replacing Google Business Profile Photos

Photos live under Edit profile, then Photos, or behind the Add photo button on the management panel. Google sorts them into slots: logo, cover, exterior, interior, team, and at work.

  • Shoot your own. Finished jobs, real crews, the actual building. A searcher comparing three listings can tell stock photography from a photograph of your work, and the comparison is happening whether or not you meant to enter it.
  • Keep the files reasonable. A well-lit JPG or PNG a few hundred pixels on a side or larger uploads cleanly. Full camera-resolution files are slower to handle and buy you nothing here.
  • Replace, don't just accumulate. A profile whose newest photo is three years old reads as a business that's gone quiet, even when the crew is booked solid.
  • Customer photos work differently. You can delete photos your own account uploaded. A photo a customer posted stays unless it breaks Google's content rules and gets reported on that basis.

A small batch every month beats a hundred uploaded once. It also gives the profile a steady signal of activity, which is part of what the monthly work on a client account exists to produce.

Which Google Business Profile Edits Get Held for Review

Some edits publish the moment you save them. Others go into a queue where Google checks them, and the editor marks those as pending until the check clears.

The reviewed group is fairly predictable. Changes to the business name, the address, the primary category, and the phone number draw the most scrutiny, because those are the fields somebody would falsify to hijack a listing. Big jumps get looked at harder than small corrections, so moving an address across town gets more attention than fixing a misspelled street.

Google's own guidance is that many updates appear within minutes and reviewed ones can take a few days. Some sit longer than that, and no published turnaround covers the slow ones, so anyone quoting you a firm number for a pending edit is guessing. The useful habit is to make one meaningful change, let it clear, then make the next one, because a pile of simultaneous edits to the sensitive fields is a reliable way to attract a rules review.

When an Edit Gets Rejected or Reverts

A rejected change arrives with a reason attached, and that reason is the only thing worth editing before the next try. Push identical information back through and it lands in front of the same check that just turned it down. Each refusal on the record makes the attempt after it a harder sell.

Edits that revert on their own are usually a different animal. Members of the public can suggest edits to your listing, and Google sometimes applies them, especially hours and closures. Data Google pulls from elsewhere can also overwrite a field. Either way, check the profile after any edit you care about rather than assuming it stuck.

When a Profile Edit Needs More Than the Owner

Nearly all of this is owner work. Hours, photos, services, and the description are safe to change on a Tuesday afternoon, and nobody has to be paid for the privilege.

Three situations are worth handing off:

  • The listing went down after an edit. A name, address, or category change can trigger a suspension, and the appeal has to answer the exact reason the notice gives. Google Business Profile not showing up covers that path.
  • Your changes keep getting undone. Repeated reverts usually mean a suggested edit, a stale data source, or a duplicate listing feeding conflicting information into Google.
  • You can't reason your way to a category. Where the leaders in your market disagree with each other, picking well takes looking at what each of those searches actually returns rather than at the category list.

The ongoing version is what a client buys from us. Somebody watches what the market starts typing, keeps the category, the service list, and the service areas pointed at it, puts fresh photos and posts up on a schedule, and sends back the listing's position town by town.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Google Business Profile edit take to show up?

Many edits appear within minutes. Ones Google sends through a review, typically changes to the name, address, category, or phone, commonly take a few days, and some sit longer than that. Google publishes no firm turnaround for the slow ones.

Why does my edit say pending?

Pending means Google's checking the change before publishing it. That's normal for the sensitive fields. Leave it alone while it clears, because resubmitting the same edit repeatedly does nothing to speed the review up.

Can I change my business name on the profile?

You can edit it, and it should match your signage and paperwork. Changing it to something with extra keywords in it breaks Google guidelines and is a common trigger for a suspension, so a rename should reflect a real change to the business.

Someone changed my hours and I didn't do it. How?

Members of the public can suggest edits to a listing, and Google sometimes applies them, particularly hours and closures. Data pulled from other sources can also overwrite a field. Set it back and check the profile again in a few days.

Do I need a computer to edit my Google Business Profile?

No. The Google Maps app gives you the same fields as a desktop browser. Signing in with the account that manages the listing is the only requirement either way.

Editing a Google Business Profile Is the Easy Half

Fifteen minutes in the editor fixes a surprising amount. Correct the category, fill in every service you sell, set the holiday hours, and put up four current photos, and the listing is ahead of most of what it competes with.
