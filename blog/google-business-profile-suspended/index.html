Google Business Profile Suspended: What It Means and How Appeals Work

By Eric Kirby · Published October 30, 2026

Finding your Google Business Profile suspended means Google decided the listing, or the account that manages it, broke one of its guidelines, and the way back is an appeal you can file only once. Reinstatements are part of the profile work we do from Rome for businesses across Georgia, and the first step is the same for every owner: find out which rule Google says was broken.

What a Google Business Profile Suspension Means

Google says it may suspend or disable profiles that don't follow its guidelines. If Google goes as far as removing a profile, the public can't reach it, and the owner and managers can't act on it. New content added to a suspended profile, an edit or a photo, can be rejected until the profile is reinstated.

A suspension can also come from the account above the profile. When a Google Account is restricted for a pattern of policy violations, every profile it manages is suspended, and the account has to be reinstated first. A restriction on an account tied to another Google product can suspend a profile the same way.

The appeals tool shows the decision that applies to you: its date, the reason, a link to the policy Google says was broken, and whether it can be appealed. Read that before you change anything.

Why Google Suspends Business Profiles

The causes trace back to Google's guidelines for representing your business and its policy overview. These are the ones Google spells out:

  • Extra words in the business name. A name padded with services, towns or slogans breaks the rule that it match the real-world name, and Google says that can lead to suspension.
  • A P.O. box as the address. Google suspends profiles set at one. Virtual offices aren't eligible either, and an office in a coworking space qualifies only with clear signage, the business's own staff there during business hours, and customers received at that location.
  • A business that isn't eligible. Online-only businesses, lead generation companies and properties listed for rent or sale can't hold a profile, and Google disables a profile when it finds the business doesn't exist at the address it claims.
  • Prohibited content anywhere on the profile. Google lists its prohibited and restricted content policy first among the rules a profile has to follow to avoid suspension.
  • A pattern of violations on the account. Repeated breaches can get the account restricted, and a restricted account suspends every profile on it.

Match the policy named in the appeals tool against your profile field by field. An appeal filed while the problem is still on the profile has nothing to stand on.

How to Reinstate a Google Business Profile

Google offers one route back, its appeals tool, and one firm warning: don't create a new profile for the same business while you wait.

  1. Fix the problem first. Bring the profile in line with the guidelines before you appeal: the real-world name, a real address or a hidden one with service areas, and one profile per business. If the suspended profile won't accept an edit, say in the appeal exactly what you're correcting, with documents that show the right details.
  2. Open the appeals tool while signed in to the Google Account that manages the profile, and select Confirm, or Switch account if it's the wrong one.
  3. Select the profile, then the decision you're appealing, and read the reason and the linked policy the tool shows.
  4. Attach your evidence with Choose file. You can add more than one file.
  5. Explain what you fixed in the text box, plainly and briefly.
  6. Check the confirmation box and select Submit appeal. An appeal can only be submitted once, so attach everything first.

Google emails the decision. If the account itself is restricted, appeal the account first from the restrictions section of your Google Account, then appeal the profile once the restriction is lifted. An owner with more than 10 suspended profiles can request bulk reinstatement in the same tool, with a spreadsheet of the profile IDs.

Evidence That Helps an Appeal

Evidence is optional in the appeals tool, and it's how you show the business is what the profile says it is. Google lists these documents as the kind that strengthen an appeal:

  • Official business registration
  • A business license
  • Tax certificates
  • Utility bills for the business, such as electricity, phone, water or internet

Every document should carry the same business name and address as the profile. A registration under one name with a profile under another, or a utility bill at a different address, works against the appeal it's attached to. For a name problem, documents showing the name the business really trades under make the case better than anything written in the text box.

How Long Reinstatement Takes

Google says appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days, and it asks owners not to file multiple appeals for the same issue before a decision arrives. The tool tracks the status as Submitted, Approved, Not Approved, Can't be appealed, or Eligible for appeal.

A denial can still leave one door open. Google says it may be able to do an additional review of a denied request, where you can add evidence that wasn't in the original appeal.

When a profile comes back, Google reinstates it along with its owners and managers. Check the reviews afterward: Google notes that reviews sometimes go missing after a reinstatement, and its support team can help with that. Contacting Google Business Profile support covers the routes in.

Staying Clear of Another Suspension

Google may reject big changes to a business name or category, and when a different business takes over a location, its policy is to mark the old profile permanently closed instead of renaming it. Google can also revoke access, after a warning, from owners who don't sign in to their profile for long stretches.

So keep the name exactly as it reads on your signage and paperwork, keep one profile per business, and have somebody open the listing every month. Reinstatement and the monthly upkeep afterward are both part of our Google Business Profile management.

Frequently asked questions

Should I make a new profile while the old one is suspended?

No. Google's help says not to create a new Business Profile for the same business, and a second listing breaks the one-profile rule on its own. Appeal the original.

How many times can I appeal a suspension?

Once. If that appeal is denied, Google says it may be able to do an additional review, where you can add evidence the first appeal didn't include.

Will my reviews come back when the profile is reinstated?

Not always. Google reinstates the profile along with its owners and managers, but its help notes that reviews sometimes go missing after a reinstatement, and says to contact support when that happens.

Can one suspension affect other profiles I manage?

It can when the problem is the account. If Google restricts an account for a pattern of violations, every profile it manages is suspended, and the profiles still need an appeal once the account restriction is lifted. A manager's account being removed doesn't affect the locations themselves.

Fix the Cause Before You File the Appeal

An appeal is one attempt, so it goes in after the profile follows the guidelines, with documents that match it, and with a short, plain account of what changed.
