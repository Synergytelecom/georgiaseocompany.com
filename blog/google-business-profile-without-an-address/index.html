Can You Have a Google Business Profile Without an Address?

By Eric Kirby · Published August 9, 2026 · Updated August 13, 2026

Yes, and most trades in Georgia should. Google still asks for a real address during setup. The question is whether that address gets published.

Yes, and Google Still Wants the Address Once

A business whose work happens at the customer's place can run a full Google Business Profile with no address showing on it. Google calls that a service area business, and it's the correct setup for plumbers, electricians, tree crews, movers, cleaners, mobile mechanics, junk haulers, landscapers, surveyors, and anybody else whose office is a truck.

Here's the part that trips people up. You still enter an address during setup, because Google verifies businesses and it verifies them somewhere.

That's usually where you keep the equipment, where the mail comes, or where the paperwork gets done, and for a lot of small businesses that means a home. What happens next is a setting: you tell Google not to show it, and the listing goes live with your service areas in place of a street line.

What the hidden address still does, quietly, is anchor where Google thinks you are. It's out of the public listing and it's very much in the calculation, which is the source of nearly every surprise in the rest of this page.

A few setups Google won't accept. A rented mailbox or a virtual office as your address. A coworking desk you don't staff. An address you share with another business that has its own listing. Every one of those is a suspension waiting to happen, and the reinstatement costs far more than the listing gained.

Setting Up a Service Area Business, Field by Field

The sequence is short, and doing it in this order saves a second pass.

  1. Enter the real address when Google asks whether you have a location customers can visit. Answer honestly. Claiming a storefront that isn't there is the fastest route to a suspension.
  2. Choose no for customer visits, which is the switch that hides the street line on the public listing while keeping it on file with Google.
  3. Add your service areas by name. Towns, cities, or counties, entered individually. Google caps how many you can list, so this is a choosing exercise, and the honest limit is closer than most owners expect.
  4. Set the primary category before you leave. A service area listing lives or dies on that field, since it has no storefront and no signage doing any work for it.
  5. Complete the verification Google assigns you. Service area businesses draw video verification more often than storefronts do, which means a recorded walk showing your equipment, your branded vehicle, your tools, and the address you gave. Have the truck and the signage ready before you start recording.
  6. Fill in hours, phone, website, services one at a time, and photographs of real jobs. With no address on the card, those fields are the only proof a stranger gets that the business is real.

One correction worth making early. If your listing currently publishes a home address and the work happens at customers' properties, changing that setting now is the right move. Take the address down and put the towns up.

How a Google Business Profile Without an Address Reaches Other Towns

Owners assume service areas work like a delivery radius, where anything inside the line gets the same treatment. They work more like a declaration.

Your service area list tells Google which places you'll travel to. It does very little to move you up in the results somebody sees in those places, because Google rebuilds the three map cards around wherever that searcher is standing, and distance between them and your hidden address is one of the strongest inputs it uses.

Add a town forty minutes out and you become eligible there. You'll still sit behind the businesses whose addresses are in that town.

What actually extends the reach is three things doing the same work at once:

  • Service areas naming the towns, so the eligibility exists in the first place.
  • A real page for each town on your website, with the town in the page title and details that apply nowhere else. That page can rank in the results underneath the map even where the listing loses the pack, and those clicks are calls.
  • Consistent details on the other records that carry the business, so everything Google can find about you outside the profile says the same name and the same number.

Any one of the three by itself moves almost nothing. Together they pull a listing into towns it has never appeared in. Coverage from Rome out toward Cartersville or Calhoun gets built exactly that way, one town page at a time, and Google Maps SEO is that work as an ongoing job.

What Hiding the Address Costs You in the Map Pack

Honest accounting, because a hidden address is a trade rather than a free win.

  • Proximity keeps pulling results back toward the searcher, and your anchor point stays wherever the hidden address sits. A shop with a storefront in the town somebody is searching from starts ahead of you there.
  • No directions button, no walk-in, no sign on a busy road. The card gives a customer a phone number and a website, so those two have to carry everything.
  • Some searchers trust an address. A hidden one reads as smaller to a certain kind of buyer, and photographs of a branded truck and real crews are how you answer that.
  • Verification runs harder. Video verification asks for evidence a home-based business has to assemble on purpose.
  • Fewer legitimate second listings. A storefront chain can hold a listing per location. A service area business covering nine towns holds one listing, and stacking more is the mistake that ends in suspensions and duplicates. Merging duplicates covers the cleanup when it has already happened.

What you get in exchange is worth the trade for most trades: the correct setup, no suspension risk from a published address you can't support, no strangers arriving at your house, and eligibility across every town you actually drive to.

Which Georgia Businesses Should Publish the Address Anyway

Hiding it is wrong for a real slice of businesses, and the test is one question: can a customer show up during business hours and get helped by somebody?

Publish the address when the answer is yes. A shop, a clinic, a salon, a restaurant, a law office, a dealership, a gym, a store. Those get the address, the directions button, and the local search advantage that comes with sitting in the town where people are searching. Hiding it throws away an asset you're already paying rent on.

The middle case is a hybrid: a real counter customers use, plus crews running to job sites. Publish the address and add the service areas. Google supports both on one listing, and a business with a staffed location gains nothing by pretending otherwise.

Where the call gets genuinely hard is a home-based business that occasionally meets clients at the house. Consultants, photographers, some trades. The safe reading is that a location has to be staffed during posted hours and available to customers without an appointment made for that purpose. When it isn't, hide it and list the towns.

Trade by trade, the answer is usually settled by what the customer needs. Pages like plumber SEO and junk removal SEO assume a hidden address, and dental SEO assumes a published one, because that's how each trade actually operates.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to give Google my home address to get a profile?

You have to give Google an address it can verify, and for a home-based business that is usually the house. The setting that matters is the next one, where you tell Google customers do not visit. The address stays on file and comes off the public listing.

Can I use a PO box or a virtual office as my business address?

No. Mailboxes, mail drops, virtual offices, and unstaffed coworking desks all break the guidelines Google publishes, and each one is a common cause of suspension. Use the real place where the business operates and hide it.

How many service areas can one listing cover?

Google caps the list, so the practical question is which towns earn a slot. Pick by demand and by drive time rather than by hope, since a town on the list you never reach adds nothing and a nearby town left off costs eligibility.

Will hiding my address hurt how far my listing reaches?

It changes what the card offers rather than the reach itself. Your hidden address still anchors where Google thinks you are, so proximity works the same either way. What extends reach is service areas, a page for each town, and consistent records, working together.

What does video verification actually ask for?

A recorded walk showing that the business is real and operates where you said. Expect to show branded vehicles, tools and equipment, signage, and the location you entered, then a management step like opening a till or accessing your own records. Have all of it staged before you start.

The Address Setting Nobody Goes Back to Check

This gets decided once, during a setup nobody enjoyed, and then it sits untouched for years. Meanwhile the trucks start running to two more counties, the shop opens a real counter, or the home address is still sitting on a public listing it shouldn't be on.
