Waco, Georgia and Waco, Texas Are Fighting Over the Same Word

Type the town name into a search with nothing attached to it and Texas wins every time, by a margin no amount of monthly work will ever close. Knowing that before you pay anybody to chase the bare word saves you a year.

What converts is the qualified phrase: the trade plus the town plus the state, or the trade plus Haralson County, typed by somebody who lives here and knows which Waco they mean. Those phrases carry a fraction of the competition a metro keyword does, and they belong to whoever bothers to write a page for them. Almost nobody local has.

The same problem lands on your listing. Your business name, street address and phone number have to read identically everywhere Google checks them, and a directory that guessed the wrong state is a record quietly working against you. Straightening that out is dull and checkable, and it moves things more often than anything clever does.

Frequently asked questions

Should our pages say Waco or Waco, GA?

Always with the state attached in titles, headings and the listing itself, because the bare word belongs to Texas as far as a search engine is concerned. Inside the copy the town name alone reads fine once the page has established where it sits.

We are in Waco but most jobs land in Bremen and Carrollton. How does that get set up?

Address where you operate, service area covering both, and a page of its own for every town you want calls from. Moving the map pin to a bigger town backfires, since Google checks the address against reality and a mismatch can cost you the listing outright.

Do you build sites for businesses this small?

Yes, and small usually means fewer pages, which is where most of the cost rides. A web designer who bills by the page has every reason to talk you into pages you would never use.
