How to Rank Higher on Google

You are visible somewhere, just not where you want to be. Here is what usually holds a ranking back, and what actually moves it.

Where You Stand Right Now

Your business shows up. It is on Google somewhere: page two of the organic results, the bottom of the map pack, a spot that technically counts as "ranking" but brings almost no calls. That is a meaningfully different situation than being invisible entirely, and it calls for different fixes, since the site or the profile clearly works well enough to be indexed and shown, it is just losing the competition for a better spot.

The Five Things That Usually Explain a Stall, in Order of How Often We See Them

  1. An incomplete or inconsistent Google Business Profile. Missing categories, an outdated service list, or photos that have not been touched in months are the single most common, and most fixable, cause of a stalled local ranking. It is also the one entirely within an owner's own control, which makes it the first place worth checking.
  2. Content that does not match the actual search. One broad page trying to represent every service a business offers rarely beats a competitor with a dedicated page for the exact thing being searched. A page has to be specifically about a search to have a real shot at ranking for it.
  3. Activity that stalled after the initial setup. A profile or a website that got real attention at launch and then went quiet, no new reviews, no new content, no updates, tends to lose ground to competitors who kept working after their own launch.
  4. Technical drag. A slow load time, a layout that breaks on a phone screen, or pages that error out quietly discount every other signal working in a site's favor, even when the content itself is genuinely good.
  5. Simply being newer or less established. Sometimes the honest answer is that a competitor has held their position longer, with more time for reviews, links, and trust to accumulate. This is not a checklist item to fix in an afternoon, it is a gap that closes with sustained effort over time.

Two Different Rankings, Moving at Different Speeds

"Ranking higher" usually means one of two different things, and they do not always move together. If you searched how to rank higher on google maps specifically, the Business Profile fixes below matter most for you: map pack position responds to Business Profile changes and, in our experience, can shift within a few weeks, since Google rereads a profile constantly. Organic position responds to the page itself and to trust built over time, and tends to move slower, over months rather than weeks. Fixing one does not automatically fix the other, which is why an owner can feel like nothing worked when in fact the map pack moved and the organic listing simply has not caught up yet.

Knowing which one you actually care about changes where the effort goes first. A business that lives and dies by nearby phone calls gets more out of tightening the Business Profile than out of a long content push, while a business competing for broader, non-local searches gets little benefit from map pack work at all.

What to Do About It

Two different speeds are worth separating here, since chasing everything at once tends to produce less than fixing the fast moving items first while the slower ones build in the background.

What you can move quickly:

  • Audit your Google Business Profile against two or three competitors currently outranking you: category, services listed, photo count, and how recently each was updated.
  • Fill in every field the profile actually offers, description, services, hours, rather than the minimum needed to publish it.
  • Run a basic mobile and speed check on your site and fix whatever the worst offender turns out to be, usually oversized images.

What takes longer, but compounds:

  • Build one dedicated page per real service and per town you serve, instead of relying on one all purpose page that covers none of them especially well.
  • Get in the habit of asking every satisfied customer for a review on the spot, spread out steadily, rather than a one time push that fades after a few weeks.
  • Publish something new on a regular schedule. Consistency matters more than volume, and search engines reward a site that keeps showing signs of life.

When Outside Help Actually Pays Off

A single location business willing to work through the list above, and stick with it, can move a genuinely stalled ranking on its own, especially the Google Business Profile fixes, which tend to show change sooner than content or authority work does.

Three situations call for more than a solo effort. Several towns or services that each need their own dedicated page take more time to build out properly than most owners have to spare alongside running the business itself. Real fixes already made and given genuine time, tracked on a set schedule rather than guessed at, with nothing moving anyway, usually point to a competitive gap rather than a mechanical one. And simply wanting this measured and reported on consistently, instead of checked every so often on your own, is exactly the difference our local SEO and content work make.

Frequently asked questions

Is ranking higher on Google Maps a different job than ranking higher in regular search?

Largely, yes. Maps position comes almost entirely from the Google Business Profile: category, completeness, reviews. Regular organic position comes from the page itself and trust built over time. A business can be doing well at one and poorly at the other, since the two barely share signals.

Do reviews matter as much as backlinks for moving up?

For most of the trades we track, reviews carry real weight in the map pack while backlinks barely register there. For the organic results below it, the reverse leans true: earned links and on-page relevance do more of the work than review count does. Which one to prioritize depends on which surface actually matters for the search in question.

Does publishing more content always help rankings?

Not on its own. Content aimed at a real, specific search a customer actually types tends to help; content published just to hit a schedule with no search demand behind it usually does not move anything.

If I can only fix one thing this month, what should it be?

An owner's direct control is the deciding factor here, and nothing sits more squarely inside it than the Google Business Profile: accurate categories, complete services, and a real posting and review pace beat everything else on this list for most small, local businesses.

Does spending money on ads help move a ranking up?

It does not touch organic or map pack position at all, since ads sit in a separate, paid section of the results page. Both of the free positions are earned instead, through the fixes described on this page, whether or not a budget for ads exists alongside them.
