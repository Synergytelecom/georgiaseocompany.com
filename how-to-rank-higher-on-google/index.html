How to Rank Higher on Google

A stalled ranking moves when somebody works it on a schedule instead of in bursts.

You Rank Somewhere, Just Not High Enough to Get Calls

Your business shows up. It's on Google somewhere: page two of the organic results, the bottom of the map pack, a spot that technically counts as "ranking" while bringing almost no calls. Being invisible is a different situation, and it calls for different fixes. The site or the profile works well enough to be indexed and shown, and it's losing the competition for a better spot.

Rankings at that stage move on cadence as much as on any single fix. Somebody has to work the same market, the same profile, and the same pages every month, because the competitors sitting above you are doing exactly that.

Five Reasons a Ranking Stalls, Ranked by How Often We See Them

  1. An incomplete or inconsistent Google Business Profile. Missing categories, an outdated service list, or photos nobody has touched in months are the most common cause of a stalled local ranking, and the most fixable. It's also the one fully inside an owner's control, which makes it the first place to look.
  2. Content that does not match the actual search. One broad page trying to represent every service a business offers rarely beats a competitor with a dedicated page for the exact thing being searched. A page has to be specifically about a search to have a shot at ranking for it.
  3. Activity that stalled after the initial setup. A profile or a website that got attention at launch and then went quiet, no new reviews, no new content, no updates, loses ground to competitors who kept working after their own launch.
  4. Technical drag. A slow load time, a layout that breaks on a phone screen, or pages that error out quietly discount every other signal working in a site's favor, however well the content itself reads.
  5. Being newer, and nothing more than that. Sometimes the answer is that a competitor has held their position longer, with more time for reviews, links, and trust to accumulate. That gap closes with sustained effort over months, which puts it well outside what an afternoon of fixes reaches.

Two Different Rankings, Moving at Different Speeds

"Ranking higher" usually means one of two different things, and they move on separate clocks. Map pack position responds to Business Profile changes and can shift within a few weeks, since Google rereads a profile constantly.

Organic position responds to the page itself and to trust built over time, and moves slower, over months more than weeks. Fixing one leaves the other where it was, which is why an owner can feel like nothing worked when the map pack moved and the organic listing hasn't caught up.

Local Calls or Broader Search

Knowing which one you care about changes where the effort goes first. A business that lives and dies by nearby phone calls gets more out of tightening the Business Profile than out of a long content push, while a business competing for broader, non-local searches gets little benefit from map pack work at all.

How to Rank Higher on Google Maps, and What Takes Longer

Separate the work by speed. Fix the fast-moving items now and let the slower ones build in the background. For Google Maps specifically, the Business Profile items are the ones that matter.

What you can move quickly:

  • Audit your Google Business Profile against two or three competitors currently outranking you. Category, services listed, photo count, and how recently each was updated.
  • Fill in every field the profile offers, description, services, hours, well past the minimum needed to publish it.
  • Run a basic mobile and speed check on your site and fix whatever the worst offender turns out to be, usually oversized images.

What takes longer, but compounds:

  • Build one dedicated page per service and per town you serve, since a search matches the page written for it.
  • Ask every satisfied customer for a review on the spot, steadily, because one big push fades after a few weeks.
  • Publish something new on a regular schedule. Consistency matters more than volume, and search engines reward a site that keeps showing signs of life.

Frequently asked questions

Is ranking higher on Google Maps a different job than ranking higher in regular search?

Largely, yes. Maps position comes almost entirely from the Google Business Profile, meaning category, completeness, and reviews. Regular organic position comes from the page itself and trust built over time. A business can be doing well at one and poorly at the other, since the two barely share signals.

Do reviews matter as much as backlinks for moving up?

In the map pack, reviews carry serious weight while backlinks barely register. For the organic results below it, the reverse leans true: earned links and on-page relevance do more of the work than review count does. Which one to prioritize depends on which surface actually matters for the search in question.

Does publishing more content always help rankings?

Only when it's aimed at a specific search a customer types. Content published just to hit a schedule, with no search demand behind it, usually does not move anything.

If I can only fix one thing this month, what should it be?

The Google Business Profile, because it is the piece sitting fully inside your own control. Accurate categories, complete services, and a steady posting and review pace beat everything else on this list for most small, local businesses.

Does spending money on ads help move a ranking up?

No. Ads sit in a separate, paid section of the results page and do not touch organic or map pack position. Both of those are earned through profile and page work, with or without an ad budget running alongside.
