Before the Map Pack, Look at Who Else Is on the Page

Jefferson, the Jackson County seat, roughly 16,500 people, posted a directory share higher than anywhere else measured in this fourteen-city band: 35 percent of the organic listings across its six searches, more than one in three, were directory and aggregator sites rather than businesses with a real Jefferson address. That is the standout number on this page, and it says something the map pack numbers alone do not: an individual Jefferson business is fighting for organic visibility against big aggregator sites more than it is in almost any other town in this band.

Why Being Better Does Not Automatically Outrank a Directory

A local Jefferson business can have a better, more specific answer to a search than a national directory site does, and still lose the organic spot. Google's organic ranking leans heavily on domain-wide trust signals built over years and thousands of pages, not just on which single page answers one search most usefully. A directory with broad authority and a Jefferson-specific listing page can outrank a genuinely better-matched local business site for exactly that reason.

That is not a permanent ceiling. It means the fix is different from the one that works in the map pack. A Google Business Profile earns pack visibility on its own signals: category, reviews, proximity. Outranking a directory in the organic results below the pack takes a website built with real depth, specific to Jefferson, sustained over time, not a single well-written page. The two problems require two different kinds of work, run at the same time rather than one after the other.
