A Third of Page One Belongs to Directories

Jefferson is the Jackson County seat, roughly 16,500 people, and it posted a directory share higher than anywhere else measured in this fourteen-city band. Across its six searches, 35 percent of the organic listings, more than one in three, were directory and aggregator sites rather than businesses with a Jefferson address. That's the standout number on this page, and it carries information the map pack figures leave out. A Jefferson business fights for organic visibility against big aggregator sites harder than in almost any other town in this band.

Why a Better Answer Still Loses to a Directory

A local Jefferson business can have a better, more specific answer to a search than a national directory does and still lose the organic spot. Google's organic ranking leans heavily on domain-wide trust built over years and thousands of pages, alongside how well a single page answers one search. A directory with broad authority and a Jefferson-specific listing page outranks a better-matched local business site for exactly that reason.

The fix differs from the one that works in the map pack. A Google Business Profile earns pack visibility on its own signals: category, reviews, proximity. Outranking a directory in the organic results below the pack takes a website with depth, specific to Jefferson, sustained over time. Two problems, two kinds of work, run at the same time.
